# Elevation and river data for Cannae: candidates by licence and resolution

*Research note for GitHub issue #44 (part of wayfinder map #36). Verified 2026-09-06 by fetching each licence and download page and, for the Copernicus GLO-30 DEM, by reading the Cannae and Little Bighorn windows straight from the public AWS bucket and contouring them locally (rasterio 1.5.1 / GDAL 3.12.4, scikit-image 0.26, mapshaper 0.6.x). Every byte count below is measured on those files, not estimated. Place coordinates used to sample heights are typed from general knowledge and flagged as approximate.*

## Gist

Five global DEM families could supply relief for the Ofanto valley, and they fall into three licence classes: public domain (SRTM 1 arc-second and ASTER GDEM v3, both NASA "openly shared, without restriction"; Natural Earth's shaded relief, but at 1 arc-minute it is 12 by 18 pixels across the battle box and useless), attribution with bespoke terms (Copernicus GLO-30/GLO-90, a 30 m surface model with a mandatory DLR/Airbus credit line, a liability sentence, and a pass-through obligation; the EU-DEM 25 m that preceded it is withdrawn and points at GLO-30), and CC BY 4.0 (Italy's TINITALY 10 m terrain model, the only true 10 m DTM of Apulia with a plain licence). GLO-30 is the easiest to get: one unsigned HTTP range read of a cloud-optimised GeoTIFF returns the whole 25 by 22 km Cannae box in 2.4 s. Measured on that box, elevation in vector form costs: contour polylines at 20 m interval simplified to 60 m, 34 lines and 2,870 vertices, 61 kB (15 kB gzipped); at 10 m, 116 kB; hypsometric band polygons are roughly nine times the size of the lines at the same interval because every band edge is stored twice and the surface model's speckle produces thousands of one-cell islands; a 16-bit PNG of the raw 30 m grid is 266 kB (355 kB as a data URI), a Terrain-RGB PNG 765 kB, and a 90 m subsample 47 kB. GeoJSON (RFC 7946) allows a third coordinate for elevation and foreign members, but has no raster type. The Ofanto's modern course is available as OpenStreetMap (ODbL; 1,396 nodes in the valley box), Natural Earth 1:10m rivers (public domain; a 33-vertex line, 5 vertices in the battle box), EU-Hydro (Copernicus attribution) and HydroRIVERS (15 arc-second). Its 216 BC course is unknown: Polybius and Livy put a camp on each bank and the Roman line facing south with its right on the river, Kromayer's right-bank, downstream placement is the working consensus, and the site's own Wikipedia article says the bank is "considerable controversy"; a 2024 geomorphic paper describes the field as floodplain and low terrace at about 25 m with the Cannae hill at 59 m, and 19th-century maps show the lower course and mouth moving, so any modern river line is a placeholder, not a reconstruction. Little Bighorn is covered by the same global DEMs (GLO-30 tile N45 W108 read the same way; 235 m of relief in the box, Last Stand Hill about 75 m above the river), so the route carries over; what changes is that a public-domain USGS 3DEP 1 m lidar DEM (published 2026-08-13) and a 1/3 arc-second DEM exist for the site, and the NPS reports the river actively abandoning meanders along the battlefield boundary, so the "modern river as placeholder" caveat applies there too. No pick is made.

## What the map file needs (ADR-0005, ADR-0007)

A GeoJSON `FeatureCollection` with `Polygon`/`MultiPolygon` features carrying `properties.kind: "land"` and `Point` features carrying `properties.kind: "place"`; `[lon, lat]` coordinates; no styling; strict validator, so a new feature kind (contour, band, river) or a foreign member (a raster) is a schema change; the map file carries its own `license` and `attribution`, and the attribution is drawn as furniture. ADR-0005 defers "rivers, roads, and terrain" to the land-battle generalisation, which is what #44 feeds. ([ADR-0005](../adr/0005-map-background-is-a-referenced-geojson-file.md), [ADR-0007](../adr/0007-battle-data-ships-cc-by-with-no-share-alike-sources.md))

The measured extents: **Cannae** 41.20-41.40 N, 16.00-16.30 E (25 by 22 km; Canne della Battaglia, the Ofanto from San Ferdinando to the sea, the edge of Barletta). **Little Bighorn** 45.50-45.62 N, 107.50-107.35 W (12 by 13 km; both monument units, Crow Agency, the river valley).

## Per candidate

### 1. Copernicus DEM GLO-30 and GLO-90

**Licence.** The Copernicus Data Space collection page gives "Free license (worldwide availability)" for GLO-30 and GLO-90, EGM2008 as the vertical datum, and describes the product as a DSM that "represents the surface of the Earth including buildings, infrastructure and vegetation". ([COP-DEM collection](https://dataspace.copernicus.eu/explore-data/data-collections/copernicus-contributing-missions/collections-description/COP-DEM)) The licence PDF it links (issue date 21 February 2025, 24 pages) contains, after the restrictive CCM licence for the DGED instances, a separate "Licence for Copernicus DEM instance COP-DEM-GLO-30-F Global 30m Full, Free & Open / Licence for the use of the Copernicus WorldDEM-30" (and the same for GLO-90). Its terms, quoted from the PDF text:

- Article 3: "The rights granted under this Licence are worldwide and without limitation in time."
- Article 4: "(a) reproduction; (b) distribution; (c) communication to the General Public; (d) adaptation, modification and combination with other data and information."
- Article 5: "The use rights granted under this licence are free of charge to the User."
- Article 6(a): when distributing or communicating to the public, "the User shall inform the General Public of the source by using the following notice: © DLR e.V. 2010-2014 and © Airbus Defence and Space GmbH 2014-2018 provided under COPERNICUS by the European Union and ESA; all rights reserved."
- Article 6(b): where the data "have been adapted or modified, the User shall provide the following notice: 'produced using Copernicus WorldDEM-30 © DLR e.V. 2010-2014 and © Airbus Defence and Space GmbH 2014-2018 provided under COPERNICUS by the European Union and ESA; all rights reserved'."
- Article 6(c): users who redistribute, "modified or not", must add to "a licence or any legal warning or notice covering their distribution" the sentence "The organisations in charge of the Copernicus programme by law or by delegation do not incur any liability for any use of the Copernicus WorldDEM-30".
- Article 6(d): no impression of official endorsement. Article 6(e): subsequent users must be bound by the same obligations. Article 7: "as is", no liability. Article 9: IPR stays with Airbus; "the User is not receiving any IPR titles".
- The preamble: "The higher resolution, Copernicus WorldDEM-10 is subject of a separate licence and distribution to the general public of these higher resolution DEMs is expressly excluded from this Licence."

([Licence PDF](https://dataspace.copernicus.eu/sites/default/files/media/files/2025-06/copernicus_contributing_mission_data_access_v2_cop_dem_licenses.pdf), text extracted locally with pypdf)

*Read against ADR-0007.* This is an attribution-only class (derivatives allowed, no share-alike, commercial use not excluded), but it is not CC BY: the credit text is fixed, a liability sentence must accompany the map file's licence notice, and the file must pass the obligations on. A contour file derived from GLO-30 is an adaptation, so it carries the 6(b) "produced using" line as its `attribution`, and the repo's data licence text needs the 6(c) sentence. There is no SPDX identifier; the validator's allowlist would need a new entry (a `LicenseRef-` style id) if this source is used. Whether contour lines are an "adaptation" of the DEM or a new work is not something the licence answers; the restrictive CCM licence's definitions (an "Altered Product" is one that allows retrieval of the primary heights, a "Value Added Product" does not) suggest contours at 20 m intervals are closer to value-added, but the free licence does not use those terms.

**Resolution and coverage.** The AWS registry: buckets `copernicus-dem-30m` and `copernicus-dem-90m`; "GLO-30 Public and GLO-90 are available on a free basis for the general public under the terms and conditions of the Licence"; "GLO-30 Public provides limited worldwide coverage at 30 meters because a small subset of tiles covering specific countries are not yet released to the public by the Copernicus Programme"; tile folders are `Copernicus_DSM_COG_[resolution]_[northing]_[easting]_DEM/` with 10 for GLO-30 and 30 for GLO-90, and a `tileList.txt` per bucket. ([AWS registry](https://registry.opendata.aws/copernicus-dem/), [bucket readme](https://copernicus-dem-30m.s3.amazonaws.com/readme.html)) Search results say the 2023 release opened the previously withheld Armenia, Azerbaijan and Moldova tiles (search snippet, not verified against the tile list). Italy and Montana are in the public set: both tiles below were read.

**Measured.** Tile `Copernicus_DSM_COG_10_N41_00_E016_00_DEM.tif`, 3600 x 3600 cells at 0.000277 degrees (1 arc-second in both axes at this latitude), float32, no nodata. Window 16.00-16.30 E, 41.20-41.40 N: 1080 x 720 cells; elevation -3.5 to 210.0 m, mean 55.7 m. Sampled at typed coordinates (approximate): Monte di Canne / antiquarium 41.302 N 16.155 E = 52 m; the Ofanto below Canne 41.31 N 16.16 E = 11 m; San Ferdinando 41.30 N 16.07 E = 70 m; Barletta edge 41.32 N 16.28 E = 24 m. For comparison Mahaney reads the Barletta 1:50,000 sheet as Mt. Cannae 59 m, the Vicus monument 59 m a.s.l., and "The battlefield is at about 25 m a.s.l." ([Mahaney 2024](https://journals.pan.pl/Content/130738/PDF/04_Mahaney-1.pdf)) Tile `Copernicus_DSM_COG_10_N45_00_W108_00_DEM.tif`, window 107.50-107.35 W, 45.50-45.62 N: 540 x 432 cells, 914 to 1,149 m.

**Tool steps (verified, Python; the gdal CLI equivalent is one `gdal_translate -projwin` against `/vsicurl/`).**

```sh
# window read, unsigned, from the public COG (2.4 s for the Cannae box)
python - <<'EOF'
import rasterio
from rasterio.windows import from_bounds
url = "https://copernicus-dem-30m.s3.amazonaws.com/Copernicus_DSM_COG_10_N41_00_E016_00_DEM/Copernicus_DSM_COG_10_N41_00_E016_00_DEM.tif"
with rasterio.Env(AWS_NO_SIGN_REQUEST="YES"), rasterio.open(url) as src:
    win = from_bounds(16.0, 41.2, 16.3, 41.4, src.transform)
    arr = src.read(1, window=win)
EOF
# gdal equivalent
gdal_translate -projwin 16.0 41.4 16.3 41.2 --config AWS_NO_SIGN_REQUEST YES \
  /vsicurl/https://copernicus-dem-30m.s3.amazonaws.com/Copernicus_DSM_COG_10_N41_00_E016_00_DEM/Copernicus_DSM_COG_10_N41_00_E016_00_DEM.tif cannae_glo30.tif
gdal_contour -a elevation -i 20 cannae_glo30.tif cannae_contours.geojson -f GeoJSON
npx mapshaper cannae_contours.geojson -simplify interval=60 keep-shapes -o precision=0.00001 cannae_contours.geojson
```

The pip wheel for `gdal` failed to build on this Windows machine; `rasterio` (which bundles GDAL 3.12) installed cleanly and was used instead, with scikit-image `find_contours` standing in for `gdal_contour`. Same marching-squares algorithm; vertex counts should be within a few percent.

## Carrying elevation in the map file

**What GeoJSON allows.** RFC 7946 3.1.1: a position's "optional third element" is "altitude or elevation"; "Implementations SHOULD NOT extend positions beyond three elements". 6.1: foreign members "MAY be used in a GeoJSON document", with "no normative processing model". The seven geometry types are all vector; there is no grid or raster construct. 11.2: "6 decimal places ... amounts to about 10 centimeters". ([RFC 7946](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/rfc7946)) So contours and bands are native (elevation either as `properties.elevation`, as `gdal_contour -a` writes it, or as a z coordinate, as `-3d` writes it); a raster can only ride as a foreign member (a base64 PNG or an array of heights), which the strict ADR-0005 validator would have to admit explicitly. mapshaper drops Z on import, so `properties` is the safer home for the level. ([gdal_contour](https://gdal.org/en/stable/programs/gdal_contour.html), [mapshaper reference](https://mapshaper.org/docs/reference.html))

**Tools.** `gdal_contour -i <interval>` (lines), `-p -amin min -amax max` (band polygons between levels), `-fl` fixed levels, `-3d`; the GeoJSON driver's `COORDINATE_PRECISION` (default 7 in RFC 7946 mode). mapshaper `-simplify interval=<m>` (Douglas-Peucker or Visvalingam), `-o precision=`. d3-contour contours a row-major array of values client-side and returns GeoJSON MultiPolygons per threshold, which is the "ship a raster, contour in the browser" route. ([d3-contour](https://d3js.org/d3-contour/contour)) maplibre-contour does exactly that from Terrain-RGB tiles in MapLibre. ([maplibre-contour](https://github.com/onthegomap/maplibre-contour))

**Measured sizes, Cannae box (1080 x 720 cells of GLO-30, 25 x 22 km, 213 m of relief).** Raw contours are one vertex per cell-edge crossing; "simplified" is mapshaper Douglas-Peucker at the stated tolerance with `keep-shapes`, then closed rings under 8 vertices dropped (the DSM's buildings and tree crowns produce thousands of one-cell rings: 2,265 raw lines at 10 m become 103 real contours). Coordinates at 5 decimals (about 1 m).

Hypsometric polygons were not island-filtered, which is why their feature counts do not fall with simplification; a `-filter-islands` or `-dissolve` pass, or a DTM source, would cut them to a few dozen polygons, but the band edges are still stored twice (once per adjacent band), so expect roughly double the line figure, never less. Coordinate precision matters less than simplification: 5 dp versus 4 dp saves 9 percent on the raw lines.

**Measured sizes, Little Bighorn box (540 x 432 cells, 12 x 13 km, 235 m of relief).** Contours at 20 m simplified 60 m: 36 lines, 1,739 vertices, 42 kB (9.6 kB gz); at 10 m: 70 lines, 3,630 v, 88 kB; at 50 m: 16 lines, 703 v, 17 kB. Raster: 16-bit PNG 135 kB (180 kB base64), Terrain-RGB 317 kB, 90 m PNG 23 kB. Bands at 20 m simplified: 471 polygons, 228 kB.

**Rendering.** Contour lines need only the polylines and draw directly on canvas with the existing projection; an engraved look is line weight by level (index contours every fifth line) and a parchment stroke. Hachures (Lehmann: short strokes along the slope direction, spaced and thickened by slope) need slope and aspect at each stroke, which means either a raster (compute Horn's 3 x 3 gradient client-side, as `gdaldem slope/aspect` does) or, from contours alone, the normal of each contour segment and the distance to the neighbouring contour; the QGIS hachure script by Huffman and Woodruff's canvas "sketchy hachures" both start from a DEM and derive slope, aspect and contours internally. ([Huffman, automated hachuring](https://somethingaboutmaps.wordpress.com/2024/07/07/automated-hachuring-in-qgis/), [Woodruff, sketchy hachures](https://github.com/awoodruff/sketchy-hachures), [gdaldem](https://gdal.org/en/stable/programs/gdaldem.html)) Hypsometric bands are fills, closest to the "engraved plate" tint layers. A raster in the file gives the renderer every option (contours, hachures, hillshade) at the cost of a foreign member and a browser decode: canvas `getImageData` returns 8-bit channels, so a 16-bit greyscale PNG loses its low byte unless the height is split across channels, which is what Terrain-RGB (`height = -10000 + (R*256*256 + G*256 + B) * 0.1`) does. ([Mapbox Terrain-RGB](https://docs.mapbox.com/data/tilesets/reference/mapbox-terrain-rgb-v1/), [MDN getImageData](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/CanvasRenderingContext2D/getImageData))

Wikipedia gives the river as 134 or 170 km depending on source, rising near Nusco at 715 m, passing "Canosa di Puglia, San Ferdinando di Puglia, and Cannae before entering the Gulf of Manfredonia between Margherita di Savoia and Barletta", ending "in a delta and an estuary". ([Ofanto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ofanto))

**The 216 BC course.** What the sources fix: Polybius 3.110, Aemilius "encamped with two-thirds of it on the banks of the Aufidus ... For the other third of his army he caused a camp to be made across the river, to the east of the ford, about ten stades from his own lines, and a little more from those of the enemy". 3.113: Varro "got the army in motion from both the camps. Those from the larger camp he drew up in order of battle, as soon as he had got them across the river ... selecting the south as the aspect of the whole. The Roman horse he stationed on the right wing along the river"; Hannibal "getting them across the river at two spots, drew them up opposite the enemy. On his left wing, close to the river, he stationed the Iberian and Celtic horse". (Shuckburgh translation, [Gutenberg 44125](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/44125/pg44125.txt)) Livy 22.44 has the two camps with the river between supplying water, and 22.46 the Volturnus "blew great clouds of dust into their faces"; the Perseus fetch of 22.46 summarised the facing as Romans north, but the Latin at 22.46.9 is "Romanis in meridiem, Poenis in septentrionem versis" (Romans facing south), agreeing with Polybius; not re-fetched, flagged. ([Livy 22.44](http://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/text?doc=Perseus:text:1999.02.0144:book=22:chapter=44), [Livy 22.46](http://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/text?doc=Liv.+22.46&fromdoc=Perseus%3Atext%3A1999.02.0144))

What is disputed: the Canne della Battaglia article states the town sits "on a hill on the right (i.e., south) bank, 9.6 kilometres (6 miles) southwest of its mouth" and that "There is considerable controversy as to whether the battle took place on the right or the left bank of the river". ([Cannae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cannae)) The positions, from a secondary summary (a blog, cited as such): Kromayer (1912) put the field "on the river's right bank, downstream from the hill of Cannae, on a front over four kilometres wide", Romans facing roughly south-west, the Roman right and Carthaginian left on the river, and this "eventually [became] generally accepted"; 19th-century writers (Hesselbarth, Arnold) put it upstream on ground that "is rugged and hilly, unsuitable for an infantry battle"; De Sanctis, Lehmann and Delbruck argued for the left bank; Connolly (1981) suggested "the Aufidus had flowed rather further north in 216 BC" and placed the camps near San Ferdinando; Goldsworthy has moved between a four-kilometre and a two-kilometre front anchored on the river and the hill. The same summary's verdict on the channel: "there's no evidence at all for what path it took in Hannibal's day". ([Thirsty Gargoyle, Locating the Battle of Cannae](http://thethirstygargoyle.blogspot.com/2013/07/armageddon-on-aufidus-locating-battle.html)) The only geomorphic treatment found, Mahaney 2024 (Studia Quaternaria 41(1), CC BY-NC-SA 4.0), describes the field as "the floodplain and low terrace" north of the escarpment, "oriented northeast in line with the meandering course of the Ofanto River", the battlefield "at about 25 m a.s.l.", the Vicus monument at 59 m, and proposes a hidden defile south-east of the museum; it does not reconstruct the 216 BC channel. ([Mahaney 2024](https://journals.pan.pl/Content/130738/PDF/04_Mahaney-1.pdf)) A ResearchGate figure caption found by search, "Shorelines and lower Ofanto River course from 1808 to 1869", shows that even the historical lower course and mouth moved within the 19th century (snippet only, paper not fetched). The Italian excavations at the antiquarium (Gervasio's necropolis, later re-dated medieval) were not verified against a primary source in this session.

**What it means for a map.** The modern line, from any of the sources above, is a placeholder for a channel nobody can locate; the units' positions will be authored to Kromayer's or another modern reconstruction, and the phase notes, not the geometry, carry that choice. Two ways to draw it, both honest: a `kind: "river"` line cut from OSM or EU-Hydro (real, registered, obviously modern), or an authored line labelled conjectural in the sources table. Which bank the battle sits on is a battle-file decision (positions), not a map-file one, and a map that carries only the modern river does not foreclose it.

## Does the route cover Little Bighorn?

Yes for the data, with one difference that matters to the licence decision.

- **Elevation.** The same GLO-30 bucket serves `N45_00_W108_00` and the window read the same way (540 x 432 cells, 914-1,149 m). SRTM tile `N45W108` covers it. Sampled from GLO-30 at typed coordinates (approximate): the monument / Last Stand Hill (45.5703 N 107.4275 W, the coordinates on its Wikipedia page) 1,005 m; the river at the Medicine Tail ford 45.567 N 107.445 W about 934 m; the Reno-Benteen site 45.541 N 107.417 W 942 m; the valley floor at Crow Agency 925 m. About 75 m from river to Last Stand Hill, 235 m across the box. The NPS geologic inventory describes "dissected uplands, rugged bluffs", terraces "ranging from 3 m (10 ft) to 170 m (560 ft) above the river", Deep Ravine with headcuts, and Medicine Tail Coulee as "a broad drainage". ([NPS GRI 2011/407](https://npshistory.com/publications/libi/nrr-2011-407.pdf), [NPS battle story](https://www.nps.gov/libi/learn/historyculture/battle-story.htm)) At 30 m the coulees read as broad folds and Deep Ravine barely at all; at 20 m intervals the box yields 36 contours, which is legible but coarse for a fight decided by ravines.
- **The US-only public-domain alternative.** USGS: "All 3DEP products are available, free of charge and without use restrictions"; "USGS-authored or produced data and information are considered to be in the U.S. Public Domain"; The National Map's terms: "Map services and data downloaded from The National Map are free and in the public domain. There are no restrictions". ([3DEP products](https://www.usgs.gov/3d-elevation-program/about-3dep-products-services), [USGS copyrights](https://www.usgs.gov/information-policies-and-instructions/copyrights-and-credits), [TNM terms](https://www.usgs.gov/faqs/what-are-terms-uselicensing-map-services-and-data-national-map)) The National Map API over the Little Bighorn box returns 6 one-metre tiles: two from `MT_LittleBigHorn_2014` (a 5.5 square mile NPS/USGS lidar flight of 12 June 2014 at 0.35 m post spacing, published 2020) and four from `MT_Statewide_Phase5_D23` (published 2026-08-13, about 275 MB each, GeoTIFF on `prd-tnm.s3.amazonaws.com`, no login), plus 7 editions of the 1/3 arc-second tile `n46w108` (410-470 MB) and 85 lidar point-cloud tiles. ([TNM Access, 1 m](https://tnmaccess.nationalmap.gov/api/v1/products?datasets=Digital%20Elevation%20Model%20(DEM)%201%20meter&bbox=-107.5,45.5,-107.35,45.62&outputFormat=JSON), [1/3 arc-second](https://tnmaccess.nationalmap.gov/api/v1/products?datasets=National%20Elevation%20Dataset%20(NED)%201/3%20arc-second&bbox=-107.5,45.5,-107.35,45.62&outputFormat=JSON), [2014 lidar report](https://ftpgeoinfo.msl.mt.gov/Data/Spatial/MSDI/Elevation/Lidar/Reports/BIGHORN_2014_LtlBigHorn.pdf)) Transects pulled from the 1 m DEM through the USGS Elevation Point Query Service put the ravine notches below Last Stand Hill at 20-40 m wide and 6-15 m deep, and Medicine Tail Coulee at about 700 m rim to rim: the coulee survives at 30 m, the ravines (Deep Ravine, where Company E died; the Reno retreat crossing) are a pixel or less and vanish. So for Montana a public-domain, bare-earth, 1 m source exists where for Apulia the public-domain options are 30 m radar and the 10 m DTM is CC BY. Under ADR-0007's "prefer public domain" rule the pick differs by country, but the pipeline (clip, contour, simplify, `kind: "contour"`) does not; what the 1 m source changes is the size arithmetic (a 1 m grid over 12 km is 150 million cells, so the map file would carry contours cut from it, or a resampled raster, never the grid).
- **Rivers.** The National Hydrography Dataset "was retired" on 1 October 2023 but "will continue to be available"; its successor is the 3D Hydrography Program; mapped at 1:24,000; NHDPlus HR for HU4 1007 (Big Horn) is on the same `prd-tnm` bucket. ([NHD](https://www.usgs.gov/national-hydrography/national-hydrography-dataset)) Public domain. OSM way 70194278 "Little Bighorn River" has 228 nodes (ODbL). Natural Earth has the Bighorn but not the Little Bighorn (grepped the global file and the North America supplement).
- **The 1876 channel.** The NPS inventory: "The river continues to rework the older deposits in the river valley, abandoning established meanders and forming new ones"; along the Custer unit boundary "there is a well-developed meander that is very close to being abandoned through channel migration"; its Figure 3 compares the modern channel with an 1891 depiction and notes the meanders "have shifted significantly"; the cultural landscape inventory lists "the re-alignment of Medicine Tail Ford from its 1876 channel". ([NPS GRI 2011/407](https://npshistory.com/publications/libi/nrr-2011-407.pdf)) Scott's 2010 oxbow report georeferenced the historical maps: Maguire's 1876/1881 sheets misregister by tens of metres (RMSE 77.6 m) and show the river "in the bluffs"; Blake's 1883 GLO plat (RMSE 8.4 m) and Sampson's 1901 map "may be among the closest to approximating the 1876 river channel" and show the three oxbows "in nearly the same spatial positions which they occupy today"; between the 1935 aerial photographs and 2005 "there are no significant channel changes"; "no formal geomorphological investigation of the river channel ... has been undertaken". ([Scott 2010, oxbow report](https://www.friendslittlebighorn.com/LittleBighornOxbowArcheologicalReport.pdf), [Scott, MWAC Technical Report 124](https://npshistory.com/series/archeology/mwac/tech/124.pdf)) So the modern line is a fair proxy at map scale, the fords are not, and the same placeholder caveat as the Ofanto applies at a 150-year rather than 2,200-year remove.
- **Public-domain historical maps.** Maguire's 1876 "Custer's Battlefield" (War Department, Office of the Chief of Engineers; NARA 137929409) is on Commons as a US government work, 6,232 x 4,926 px. ([Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Custer%27s_Battlefield_-_NARA_-_137929409.jpg)) The USGS 1891 "Custer Battlefield" 1:5,280 contoured sheet is public domain ("All maps on this page were published by the U.S. Geological Survey and are in the public domain") and is the best 1876-era terrain-plus-river base. ([PCL Map Collection](https://maps.lib.utexas.edu/maps/topo/montana/), [Commons copy](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Custer_Battlefield_1891_1-5280_-_PCL_Map_Collection_-_txu-pclmaps-topo-mt-custer_battlefield-1891.jpg)) The Library of Congress map search returned HTTP 403 twice.

**Verdict.** The same route (global 30 m DEM to contours, plus a river line) produces a Little Bighorn map without a new format decision, and every global source covers the site. What is different is per-map, not per-format: a public-domain 1 m DEM and public-domain hydrography exist for the US site, and the battle's decisive features are below 30 m, so the per-map source pick under ADR-0007 ("prefer public domain") lands on USGS data rather than GLO-30, and the interval and simplification tolerance need to be chosen per map rather than fixed in the format. Whether to note "prefer a national public-domain source where it exists and beats the global baseline" as a rule is a decision for the map format ticket.

## For the map format decision

| Question | Options this note measured | What travels with the choice |
|---|---|---|
| Elevation source for Cannae | GLO-30 (attribution, bespoke); SRTM (public domain, login); TINITALY 10 m (CC BY 4.0) | GLO-30: fixed credit, "produced using" line, liability sentence, pass-through, new licence id in the validator. SRTM: nothing. TINITALY: attribution string, already in the allowlist class |
| Vector form | Contour lines (61 kB at 20 m / 60 m simplification; one new feature kind); hypsometric bands (about 0.5 MB at 20 m; one new polygon kind, fills only); raster foreign member (266 kB 16-bit PNG or 765 kB Terrain-RGB at 30 m, 47 kB at 90 m; validator must admit a non-GeoJSON member; renderer gains hachures and hillshade) | Lines and bands are ordinary GeoJSON and mapshaper-simplifiable; a raster is the only form that supports hachures without a second derivation step |
| Interval | 10 m shows the Cannae hill as five rings and the terrace edges; 20 m shows the hill as two rings; 50 m loses the hill | File size scales linearly with the number of levels |
| River | OSM (ODbL, makes the map file ODbL); EU-Hydro (Copernicus credit); Natural Earth (public domain, 5 vertices); authored conjectural line | Whichever is chosen, the 216 BC channel is unknown and the phase notes say so |
| Little Bighorn | Same pipeline; 3DEP public domain 1 m or 10 m available | Per-map source pick, no format change |

Mixed files inherit the strictest class of any input, as in the Cadiz note: GLO-30 contours plus an OSM river is an ODbL file that must also carry the Copernicus notices; SRTM contours plus a Natural Earth river is public domain throughout.
