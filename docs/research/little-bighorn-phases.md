# Little Bighorn phase list at command granularity

*Research note for GitHub issue #123 (part of the wayfinder map, #121). Written 2026-09-07.*

## Question

What is the phase list for the Battle of the Little Bighorn (25 to 26 June
1876) at the level a plate can draw, from the division of the regiment at the
divide through Reno's valley fight and retreat, Custer's movement along the
bluffs and the destruction of his battalions, the siege of Reno Hill through
the 26th and the withdrawal of the village; which units stay under sixteen
drawn at once; which positions and states are testimony, which archaeology and
which reconstruction; whether the v2 arms carry dismounted cavalry and Native
fighters; what map data exists, including the 1876 course of the river; which
public-domain sources support it, with locators and licences; and what schema
v2 cannot express?

## Short answer

**Fourteen phases over two days, five units at the coarse level and nine at the
fine one, a battle clock honest to about the half-hour for Reno's fight and to
nothing better than "the same hour" for Custer's, and a battle in which one
side's whole action and the other side's decisive hour are reconstruction.**
The sources give watch times in plenty, but the watches were never set to the
sun and never to each other: Wallace, the regiment's itinerist, told the Court
"I never have claimed that it was the local time of the place" (RCOI vol. 1,
p. 77) and Godfrey said "We did not have the local time, our watches were not
changed" (RCOI vol. 2, p. 102). Witnesses put Benteen's arrival on the hill
anywhere between half past two and after four. Custer's five companies left no
army witness; their phases rest on the positions of the dead (Maguire's 1876
survey, Bradley, Godfrey), the Native accounts, and the 1984-85 cartridge-case
archaeology, which fixes *where* men fought on the Custer field better than any
testimony and says nothing about *when*.

Custer's wings and the village's two bodies are the fine level; at the coarse
level phases 8 and 9 draw one Custer marker on Calhoun Hill then Last Stand
Hill and one village marker. If fewer phases are wanted, merge 2+3 (the order
and the crossing are fifteen minutes apart on every watch), 10+11 (the perimeter
and the dark) and 13+14. Phases 6, 7, 8 and 9 must stay separate: they are the
four moments where the sources have different commands doing different things
at once, and 8 and 9 are the only two in which Custer's battalion is drawn
fighting.

Three things the sources agree on, and the note leans on: the regiment was
divided about noon on the watches; Reno struck the upper (southern) end of the
village where the Hunkpapa lodges stood, and the Cheyenne circle stood at the
lower (northern) end opposite Medicine Tail Coulee; and Custer's fight opened
after Reno had reached the bluffs ("Custer's attack did not begin until after
Reno had reached the bluffs", Godfrey p. 384; "Then the second band of white
warriors came", Crow King 1881) and was over before the survivors on Weir Point
saw the field.

- **Reno's skirmish line and the timber**: no gazetteer point. MWAC 124 puts
  the first line by "eleven .45-55 cartridge cases in the fields west of the
  Garryowen Post Office ... in a roughly linear alignment from northwest to
  southeast" and the timber fight on "the land circumscribed by the abandoned
  river meander" (the Garryowen bend). **Garryowen**: 45.5267 N, 107.4194 W
  (Q965380).
- **The village**: Sitting Bull's Hunkpapa circle "located just north of the
  Garryowen store" (MWAC 124); the Cheyenne circle "about two miles north from
  the present railroad station at Garryowen ... Across the river east of us and
  a little upstream from us was a broad coulee ... having now the name Medicine
  Tail coulee" (Wooden Leg pp. 206-207). Length: "nearly three miles long" (NY
  Herald 1877); "more than two and a half miles" (Crow King 1881); "more than
  two miles down the river" from an ash grove at the upper end (Iron Thunder
  1881); "It lined the west bank of the river for 3 miles" (Utley 1969). So
  from about 45.53 N to 45.57 N on the west bank.
- **The divide (Crow's Nest)**: about 45.445 N, 107.139 W (an OSM node tagged
  "Traditional Crows Nest?"; no gazetteer entry; uncertain). Godfrey: the
  village "about twelve or fifteen miles beyond the divide" (p. 367); Utley
  1969: "Some 15 miles to the west". **Crow Agency**: 45.6014 N, 107.4597 W.
  **Mouth of the Little Bighorn**: 45.7381 N, 107.5694 W.
- **Distances in the record.** Maguire's report: "About 3 miles below Reno's
  position, we found the hills covered with the dead bodies of officers and
  men" (p. 702); his 1877 report: "This valley is about 1½ miles wide ... The
  stream varies in width from about 30 to 75 yards" (p. 1349). Maguire's
  testimony: Reno's hill to Custer's hill "Four and a half miles" by the trail;
  hill to the ford at B "Two and four tenths miles" by the trail, "Two and two
  tenths miles" straight; the right of the skirmish line to a point opposite B,
  following the timber, "One and eight tenths miles"; the first dead soldier
  "Six-tenths of a mile" from B (RCOI vol. 1, pp. 10-13). Terry: Custer's
  trail "passes along and in the rear of the crest of the bluffs on the right
  bank for nearly or quite three miles; then it comes down to the bank of the
  river, but at once diverges from it as if he had unsuccessfully attempted to
  cross; then turns upon itself, almost completing a circle, and closes"; "The
  village in the valley was about three miles in length, and about a mile in
  width" (pp. 462-464). Godfrey: Custer's line "the first considerable ridge back from
  the river, the nearest point being about half a mile from it. His front was
  extended about three fourths of a mile" (p. 381); from Weir's point the
  Custer field was "two miles away" (p. 374). NPS: Reno "crossed the river about
  two miles south of the village"; Weir Point "a mile and a half north of
  Reno's position".
- **Sun.** 25 June 1876 at 45.57 N, 107.43 W: sunrise 04:12, sunset 19:53,
  civil dawn 03:34, civil dusk 20:31 local mean solar time; day length 15.7
  hours (author's computation, NOAA algorithm; not a source figure). Local
  mean time is 79 minutes behind Chicago mean time. The witnesses' "daylight"
  at 02:30 to 03:30 and "dark" at 21:00 to 22:00 bracket the watches to within
  about an hour of local sun time, which is as far as the record can be pushed;
  see the clock section.
- **Numbers.** Godfrey: "28 officers and about 700 men" for the regiment
  (p. 358), "roo [sic, 100] rounds of carbine and 24 rounds of pistol
  ammunition" per man (p. 364). NPS: Reno "three companies (140 men)", Benteen
  "three companies (125 men)", Custer "five companies (210 men)", "the pack
  train with ammunition and supplies (125 men)"; the village "approximately
  7,000 ... including between 1,500 and 2,000 warriors". Reno's report: "at
  least twenty-five hundred warriors". Godfrey (McLaughlin's estimate): "between
  twelve and fifteen thousand" people and "between twenty-five hundred and
  three thousand" fighting men (p. 363). Losses: "The killed of the entire
  command was 265, and of wounded we had 52" (Godfrey p. 383); Terry: "must
  reach 250; the number of wounded is 51"; Maguire: "16 officers; 252 enlisted
  men; 9 civilian employés: 277 killed; 59 wounded" and "fully 3,000 warriors"
  (p. 703); GRI: "263 killed and 5 mortally wounded ... All 210 men under

  seven Cheyennes"; Red Horse "136"; Crow King "from thirty to fifty"; NPS "an
  estimated 60-100".

The **Arikara and Crow scouts** (Varnum) are not a unit: they fought on Reno's
left, were "overwhelmed and rolled up by a mass of Dakota horsemen" (Arikara
p. 11), drove off a pony herd and were chased "seven or eight miles" (p. 152),
and about forty of their captured ponies reached the hill (p. 130). A
`detachment` arrow from `reno-battalion` toward the pony herd in phase 4 shows
the raid; the men who left the field for good are a strength cost to Reno's
battalion, the same rule ADR-0018 applied to Dumanoir. If they are wanted as a
unit they are a sixth root, `cavalry`, and the roster stays at ten.

The **village's non-combatants** (some 7,000 people, the pony herds, the
lodges) are not a unit; they are `work` points on the map and caption matter
("The women and children were hastily moved down the stream where the
Cheyennes were encamped", Gall 1886). **Terry's column** never reaches the
field inside the battle the file holds, and a unit appearing mid-battle is
deferred (ADR-0003, ADR-0017); the 27th is a caption coda as the Nile's 3
August is.

**Could the Native side be more than two bodies?** The accounts name
band-sized parties under named men (Crow King "eighty warriors", Runs-the-Enemy
"a band of one hundred and thirty", Red Cloud "about a hundred"), a screen left
at Reno Hill ("Sioux men, go watch the soldiers on the hill and prevent their
joining the different soldiers", Red Horse), and Lame White Man's Cheyennes at
the break toward the river (Wooden Leg). None of these has a position in more
than one phase, and Crow King's "No one chief was above another in that fight"
(1881) is the honest summary. Two bodies is the most the sources will bear, and
the coarse level's one body is the honest default that every visit opens on.

## The phases

Times are watch times as the sources read them, chosen from the spread tabled
in the clock section; the `t` written is the reading the note prefers and the
`notes` field must carry the others. Positions are the author's geometry on the
reference points above unless a source or the archaeology fixes them, and the
conjecture table below says which. Headings: the valley runs north-north-west,
so Reno's advance and line face about 330 and the Hunkpapa body facing him
about 150; the bluff line north of Reno Hill runs north-north-west too, so
Custer's column heading is about 340 until it turns for the ford (about 250)
and again north up Deep Coulee (about 350); the Keogh wing on Calhoun Hill
faces south-south-west (about 200) toward Greasy Grass Ridge and the Yates wing
on Custer Hill west-south-west (about 240).

### 1. `divide` - The regiment is divided at the divide (12:15, day 0)

- **Time.** Wallace: "About a quarter after 12 o'clock the command was
  halted; three companies were given to Major Reno, three companies were given
  to Capt. Benteen, and one company to Capt. McDougall, who was placed in
  charge of the pack train, and General Custer took the other five companies
  with him" (RCOI vol. 1, p. 22; OCR). Benteen: "It was after 12 o'clock when
  I started off by Lieut. Wallace's watch. I had a watch which was wound but I
  did not look at it that whole day or the next" (vol. 2, p. 31). Godfrey RCOI:
  "that was about 12 o'clock" (vol. 2, p. 90); Mathey "near 12 o'clock" (vol.
  2, p. 126); DeRudio "about 11 or 12 o'clock" (vol. 1, p. 303). Godfrey 1892:
  "We crossed the dividing ridge between the Rosebud and Little Big Horn
  valleys a little before noon. Shortly afterward the regiment was divided into
  battalions" (p. 368). Reno's report: "Attacking in the daytime at 12 m". The
  best-agreed instant of the day.
- **Positions.** All four army units at the divide, in column, heading about
  280 down Reno Creek. This is 20 km east of the valley; the extent question is
  taken up under map data. The village intact in camp; both fine-level bodies
  at their circles.
- **Captions.** Godfrey p. 368: "that he had not intended to make the attack
  until the next morning, the 26th, but our discovery made it imperative to act
  at once, as delay would allow the village to scatter and escape." Low Dog: "I
  was asleep in my lodge at the time. The sun was about noon".

### 2. `lone-tepee` - Custer orders Reno to charge; Benteen is off to the left (14:15)

- **Time.** Wallace: "Approximately it must have been about a quarter after
  two. The exact time I can't tell" (vol. 1, p. 77), fixed by "I looked at my
  watch about the time Gen. Custer called Major Reno over" (p. 77). Girard
  corrected his record to "about 2 o'clock" for the order (vol. 1, p. 108).
  Reno's own report has "until about 12.30 a. m., [p. m. ?] when Lieutenant
  Cook, adjutant, came to me and said the village was only two miles above,
  and running away" (p. 477, the editor's bracket in the 1876 printing), two
  hours off every other watch and his own "I charged about 2 p.m." of the 27th.
- **Reno.** Intact, column, at the trot for the river; an `intent` arrow at
  the village. The order (Reno-rpt, p. 477): "to move forward at as rapid a
  gait as prudent, and to charge afterward, and that the whole outfit would
  support me. I think those were his exact words." Godfrey p. 369 gives the
  same words.
- **Benteen.** Intact, column, coming back to the trail behind Reno: "I
  accordingly did so, reaching the trail just in advance of the pack-train"
  (Benteen-rpt). His scout to the left, "about fully ten miles" of bluffs, is
  his own motion between phases 1 and 2, a track, and is caption matter.
- **Custer.** Intact, column, "Custer's column followed Reno's closely"
  (Godfrey p. 368). **Pack train.** Intact, column, at the morass on Reno Creek.
- **Village.** Intact; Gall 1886: "We saw soldiers early the morning crossing
  the divide. When Reno and Custer separated we watched them until they came
  down into the valley."
- **Captions.** Godfrey p. 369: "We passed a burning tepee, fired presumably
  by our scouts"; Benteen-rpt: "a burning lodge with the dead body of an Indian
  in it on a scaffold."

### 3. `reno-crosses` - Reno crosses the river; Custer turns up the bluffs (14:30)

- **Time.** Wallace: "It was probably say 20 minutes after two when we
  crossed the creek first" (vol. 1, p. 57). Reno-note: "I charged about 2
  p.m." Herendeen: "about 2 o'clock" for entering the timber later, "I know it
  was between two and three o'clock" (vol. 1, p. 308).
- **Reno.** Intact, `line`, position at Ford A, heading 330. Reno-rpt
  (p. 477): "I at once took a fast trot, and moved down about two miles, when I
  came to a ford of the river. I crossed immediately, and halted about ten
  minutes or less to gather the battalion, sending word to Custer that I had
  everything in front of me, and that they were strong. I deployed, and, with
  the Ree scouts on my left, charged down the valley, driving the Indians with
  great ease for about two and a half miles." (The Court's typescript copy,
  Exhibit 4, reads "about 2 miles".) Godfrey p. 370: "Reno moved forward in column of fours about half a
  mile, then formed the battalion in line of battle across the valley with the
  scouts on the left; after advancing about a mile further he deployed the
  battalion as skirmishers."
- **Custer.** Intact, column, heading 340 along the bluffs east of the river:
  "Custer had moved off to the right, being separated from Reno by a line of
  high bluffs and the river" (Godfrey p. 370). Curley 1876: Custer "moved to the
  right around the base of a high hill overlooking the valley of the Little
  Horn through a ravine just wide enough to admit his column of fours".
- **Village.** Coarse: intact stepping to engaged in phase 4. Fine:
  `village-south` heading 150, turning out ("Reno swept down so rapidly upon
  the upper end that the Indians were forced to fight", Gall); `village-north`
  intact at the Cheyenne circle.
- **Captions.** Sitting Bull 1877, asked where the camp was first attacked:
  "Here" (pointing to Reno's crossing on the map). "About what time in the day
  was that?" "It was some two hours past the time when the sun is in the centre
  of the sky."

### 4. `skirmish-line` - Reno dismounts and deploys; the scouts are rolled up (14:50)

- **Time.** No witness fixes the halt; Wallace's chain runs crossing 14:20,
  timber 14:30, hill 15:15-15:30 (p. 57), which puts the line between. NPS:
  "About 200 yards from the village, Reno was stopped." "Within 15 minutes Reno
  decided that his men could be overrun".
- **Reno.** Engaged, `line`, dismounted, heading 330, in the fields west of
  the Garryowen bend (archaeology: the eleven cases "in a roughly linear
  alignment from northwest to southeast"). Godfrey p. 371: "Reno, not seeing
  the 'whole outfit' within supporting distance, did not obey his orders to
  charge the village, but dismounted his command to fight on foot." The Arikara
  (p. 95): "The soldiers and the scouts dismounted, the horses were held in
  groups behind the line. The soldiers formed in line toward the right, the
  scouts at the left out toward the ridge". A `detachment` arrow toward the
  pony herd across the valley for the scouts' raid (Arikara pp. 151-152).
  Strength 1: "Reno's loss thus far was one wounded" (Godfrey p. 371).
- **Custer.** Intact, column, on the bluff above Reno (Godfrey's point A):
  "Some of Reno's men had seen a party of Custer's command, including Custer
  himself, on the bluffs about the time the Indians began to develop in Reno's
  front" (p. 373). DeRudio's testimony on Custer waving his hat is at vol. 1,
  pp. 303-322. Martin is sent back about now with Cooke's note.
- **Benteen.** Intact, column, at the morass: "I halted to water the animals
  ... This watering did not occasion the loss of fifteen minutes" (Benteen-rpt).
  **Pack train.** Intact, column, behind him.
- **Village.** Engaged, `line`, `village-south` heading 150 and working round
  Reno's left: "The movements of the Indians around the left flank and the
  flight of the scouts caused the left to fall back" (Godfrey p. 371); Low Dog:
  "The Indians held their ground to give the women and children time to get out
  of the way." `village-north` intact.
- **Captions.** Godfrey p. 370: the Indians "developed great force, opened a
  brisk fire, mounted, and made a dash toward the foot-hills on the left flank".
  Arikara p. 11: "the thin line of Arikara scouts extending from where the
  soldiers stood out to the left was overwhelmed and rolled up by a mass of
  Dakota horsemen who rode out from behind some low hills farther to the left."

### 5. `timber` - Reno falls back into the timber; Custer descends Medicine Tail Coulee (15:10)

- **Time.** Wallace: "I did not take much over ten minutes to get to the
  timber - say half past 2. If we were there 45 minutes, it would be a quarter
  after three" (vol. 1, p. 57). Girard first swore the command left the woods
  at "ten minutes past 1 o'clock" by a watch just back from "the jeweler's for
  ten or fifteen days, cleaning and repairing" (p. 98), then corrected it to
  "10 minutes past 2 o'clock" (p. 108). Godfrey: "Those present differ in
  their estimates of the length of time the command remained in the bottom
  after they were attacked in force. Some say 'a few minutes'; others, 'about
  an hour.'" (p. 371). Herendeen: "20 or 25 minutes perhaps half an hour"
  (p. 308).
- **Reno.** Engaged, `line`, in the timber on the Garryowen bend, heading
  330, strength 0.95. Godfrey p. 371: "well protected in front by the bank and
  fringe of timber, somewhat open in the rear, but sheltered by timber in the
  bottom." Reno-rpt: "Taking possession of a front of woods, and which
  furnished (near its edge) a shelter for the horses, dismounted and fought
  them on foot". Bloody Knife's death beside Reno is caption matter.
- **Custer.** Intact, column, heading 250 down Medicine Tail Coulee, an
  `intent` arrow at the ford. Martin's account and Curley's put him here, and
  so did the trail two days later: "In the mean time, Custer had gone down
  stream and attempted to make a crossing at the point B, but was met by an
  overpowering force, and the troops retreated to the hills in rear in order to
  procure a more defensible position" (Maguire p. 702); Terry's "as if he had
  unsuccessfully attempted to cross". Godfrey disagreed ("his column was never
  nearer the river or village than his final position on the ridge", p. 380),
  and the archaeology sides with Maguire: "the archeological and relic evidence
  consistently point to a movement of soldiers to the mouth of Medicine Tail
  Coulee where a small and perhaps brief action occurred" (MWAC 124).
- **Benteen.** Intact, column, past the burning lodge, meeting the sergeant
  and then Martin: "Benteen, come on. Big village. Be quick. Bring pacs. W. W.
  Cook P. Bring pac's." (Benteen-rpt). **Pack train.** Intact, column, on Reno
  Creek.
- **Village.** Engaged, `mass`, `village-south` closing on the timber on
  three sides, the grass fired; `village-north` intact but alerted: Gall via
  Godfrey p. 379: "he was excitedly hailed by 'Iron Cedar,' one of his
  warriors, who was on the high point, to hurry to him, that more soldiers were
  coming. This was the first intimation the Indians had of Custer's column".
- **Captions.** Reno-rpt: "I soon found myself in the near vicinity of the
  village, saw that I was fighting odds of at least five to one and that my
  only hope was to get out of the woods where I would soon have been surrounded
  and gain some high ground."

### 6. `retreat` - Reno's "charge" to the bluffs (15:30)

- **Time.** Wallace: "a quarter after three or approximately that time when
  we got on top of the hill or half past three taking the time it would take to
  get back to the hill" (vol. 1, p. 57). Culbertson: "between two and three
  o'clock" (p. 356). NPS: "At approximately 4 p.m." Reno-note: "meeting no
  support was forced back to the hills."
- **Reno.** **Broken**, `column`, heading 60 (the direction of the flight to
  the river; there is no front), position at the retreat crossing, strength
  0.65. Godfrey p. 371: "Reno's command left the bottom by troop organizations
  in column ... a pony-trail led up a funnel-shaped ravine into the bluffs.
  Here the command got jammed and lost all semblance of organization. The
  Indians fired into them, but not very effectively. There does not appear to
  have been any resistance, certainly no organized resistance, during this
  retreat." Losses: "three officers, including Dr. J. M. De Wolf, and
  twenty-nine enlisted men and scouts killed; seven enlisted men wounded; and
  one officer, one interpreter, and fourteen soldiers and scouts missing.
  Nearly all the casualties occurred during the retreat and after leaving the
  timber" (p. 372); Reno-rpt gives "three officers and twenty-nine enlisted men
  killed, and seven men wounded". About fifty-five of some 175 gone from the
  unit (the missing left it for the rest of the day; thirteen and Herendeen
  returned in phase 8 and cost nothing under ADR-0018). Archaeology: "The lack
  of distinct patterning in the artifact distribution in the retreat ravine is
  consistent with the historical accounts of the disorganized endeavor" (MWAC
  124).
- **Custer.** Engaged, `line`, at the mouth of Medicine Tail Coulee, heading
  250, an `intent` arrow across the ford at the Cheyenne circle. Curley 1876:
  "When they neared the river, the Indians, concealed in the undergrowth on the
  opposite side of the river, opened fire on the troops, which checked the
  advance." MWAC 124: "the evidence for combat at the mouth of Medicine Tail
  Coulee is meager ... but is definitely present"; "consistent with the
  historical accounts that a small action with only limited firing occurred at
  the ford." Utley 1988: "At first, only a handful of warriors, perhaps 30,
  held the ford against Yates." Fine level: both wings together here; the
  Finley-Finckle case matches suggest E and F "returned fire as the command
  reassembled on Calhoun Hill" (MWAC 124), so the split is phase 7.
- **Benteen.** Intact, column, on the last rise: "Another mile and a half
  brought me in sight of the stream and plain in which were some of our
  dismounted men fighting, and Indians charging and recharging them in great
  numbers ... I then noticed our men in large numbers running for the bluffs on
  right bank of stream" (Benteen-rpt). **Pack train.** Intact, column.
- **Village.** `village-south` engaged, pursuing to the river and the foot of
  the bluffs (Two Moon: "I saw the soldiers fall back and drop into the
  river-bed like buffalo fleeing. They had no time to look for a crossing. The
  Sioux chased them up the hill"); `village-north` engaged, `line`, at the
  ford, heading 70. Hodgson's death at the crossing and DeRudio, O'Neill,
  Girard and Jackson left in the timber are caption matter.
- **Captions.** Reno-rpt: "I accomplished this by mounting and charging the
  Indians between me and the bluffs on the opposite side of the river. In this
  charge 1st Lieutenant Donald McIntosh, 2nd Lieutenant Benj. H. Hodgson, 7th
  Cavalry and A.A. Surgeon J. M. DeWolf were killed."

### 7. `benteen-joins` - Benteen joins Reno on the hill; the warriors stream down-river (16:00)

- **Time.** The worst-agreed instant on the army side. Wallace: "about 4 or
  after 4" (vol. 1, p. 78, "That is simply my own estimate, without looking at
  my watch"). Benteen: "about 3 o'clock" (vol. 2, p. 8). Godfrey RCOI: "between
  3 and 4 o'clock" (vol. 2, p. 93); Godfrey 1892: "My recollection is that it
  was about half-past two when we joined Reno" (p. 374). Mathey for the packs:
  "about 3 o'clock when we reached Major Reno's command" (vol. 2, p. 127);
  McDougall: "a quarter to four or four o'clock" (vol. 2, p. 144). Sixteen
  o'clock is Wallace's, the itinerist's; the file's `notes` must give the
  spread.
- **Reno.** Engaged, `mass`, on the hill, heading 270 toward the valley,
  rallying. **Benteen.** Engaged, `line`, dismounted on the bluff edge: "Benteen's
  battalion was ordered to dismount and deploy as skirmishers on the edge of
  the bluffs overlooking the valley. Very soon after this the Indians withdrew
  from the attack" (Godfrey p. 372). Hare is sent for the ammunition mules
  (caption). **Pack train.** Intact, column, on the last miles.
- **Custer.** Engaged, `column`, heading 350, up the east bank of Deep Coulee
  toward Calhoun Hill: "The artifact patterning and distribution gives the
  impression of soldiers moving up Deep Coulee toward Calhoun Hill ... The
  distribution of Indian caliber bullets also gives the distinct impression
  that the army movement was under fire" (MWAC 124). Fine level: `keogh-wing`
  to Calhoun Hill, `yates-wing` beside and beyond it; the assignment of
  companies to wings is reconstruction (Gray, Fox).
- **Village.** Engaged, both bodies heading 340, riding down-river: "Suddenly
  they all started down the valley, and in a few minutes scarcely a horseman
  was to be seen" (Godfrey p. 373). Gall via Godfrey p. 380: "Before they could
  do so the great body of warriors had left Reno, and hastened to attack
  Custer. This explains why Reno was not pushed". Red Horse: "Like a whirlwind
  the word went around, and the Sioux all heard it and left the soldiers on the
  hill and went quickly to save the women and children."
- **Captions.** Benteen-rpt: "I then moved up to the bluffs and reported my
  command to Maj. M. A. Reno." Godfrey p. 372: "The valley was full of horsemen
  riding to and fro in clouds of dust and smoke, for the grass had been fired
  by the Indians".

### 8. `custer-engaged` - Firing down the river; the pack train arrives; Weir starts north (16:30)

- **Time.** Godfrey: "I remember distinctly looking at my watch at twenty
  minutes past four, and made a note of it in my memorandum-book, and although
  I have never satisfactorily been able to recall what particular incident
  happened at that time, it was some important event before we started down the
  river. It is my impression, however, that it was the arrival of the
  pack-train" (pp. 373-374). Girard fixes the heavy firing from Custer's
  direction "between 3 and 4 o'clock" (vol. 1, p. 130). Reno's report alone
  has "the firing in his direction about six p.m."; Crow King: "It was not more
  than half an hour after the long-haired chief attacked us before he and all
  his men were dead"; Curley 1876 has the fight "about 2:30 or 3 o'clock p.m.,
  and continued without intermission until nearly sunset", which the Herald
  itself doubted; Maguire, from the field: "this part of the fight did not,
  from all accounts, last over two or three hours" (p. 703); Utley 1969: "The
  fight probably opened shortly after 4 p.m., just as Reno reached the refuge
  of the bluffs. Probably by 5 p.m. not a man of Custer's battalion remained
  alive." The note follows Godfrey and Utley.
- **Keogh wing.** Engaged, `line`, on Calhoun Hill and below the crest of
  Custer Ridge, heading 200, strength 1 stepping down in phase 9. Godfrey p.
  380: "dismounted two troops, Keogh's and Calhoun's, to fight on foot. These
  two troops advanced at double-time to a knoll, now marked by Crittenden's
  monument. The other three troops, mounted, followed them a short distance in
  their rear. The led horses remained where the troops dismounted." Archaeology:
  "There is evidence of at least fifteen Springfield carbines and three Colt
  pistols in use in the Calhoun position"; "Captain Myles Keogh and the men of
  Company I deployed below the ridge top on the east side of Custer Ridge"
  (MWAC 124). The stampede of the led horses (Gall 1886: "The soldiers then
  abandoned their horses to fight on foot, leaving the horses in charge of a
  few men. These were soon dispatched by the Indians, and the already
  frightened horses stampeded, carrying in their saddles the reserve
  ammunition on which the soldiers depended") is caption matter.
- **Yates wing.** Engaged, `line`, on Custer Hill and the ridge north of it,
  heading 240. Godfrey p. 381: "Smith's troop deployed as skirmishers, mounted,
  and took position on a ridge ... Smith's skirmishers, holding their gray
  horses, remained in groups of fours." The loop north-west along the Custer
  Ridge extension and Cemetery Ridge is reconstruction: "It appears reasonable
  to conclude that the left wing and Custer did move north through Last Stand
  Hill and northwesterly along the Custer Ridge Extension at least one-quarter
  mile" (MWAC 124), and Fox's Cemetery Ridge episode has "little in the way of
  archeological evidence".
- **Village.** `village-south` engaged, `mass`, heading 20, below Calhoun
  Hill on Greasy Grass and Henry Ridges: "the warriors attacked from the south
  and southeast. These Indians found cover below the tops of ridges 100 to 800
  yards away"; "a total of at least 55 different firearms" on the lower part of
  Greasy Grass Ridge; "it appears that Calhoun's position was overrun by the
  same Indians who fired at the soldiers from Henry Ridge and Greasy Grass
  Ridge" (MWAC 124). Gall's route is testimony: "Gall collected his warriors,
  and moved up a ravine south of Keogh and Calhoun" (Godfrey p. 381).
  `village-north` engaged, `mass`, heading 70, crossed at the ford and coming up
  the ravines west of Custer Hill: "The warriors, under the leadership of
  Crow-King, Crazy Horse, White Bull, 'Hump,' and others, moved up the ravine
  west of Custer's Hill, and concentrated under the shelter of the ridges on his
  right flank and back of his position" (p. 381); Two Moons 1913: "The
  Cheyennes and the Sioux came up the coulee from the foot of Reno Hill, and
  circled about. I led the Cheyennes as we came up." Archaeology gives the
  northern body a position: "a knoll 660 feet northeast of Last Stand Hill"
  with non-government cases and split .45-55 cases.
- **Reno, Benteen, pack train.** Reno engaged, `mass`; Benteen engaged,
  `line`, with a `detachment` arrow from Benteen toward Weir Point for Weir's D
  company ("Weir, from the high point, saw the Indians in large numbers start
  for Edgerly", Godfrey p. 373); the pack train engaged, `mass`, on the hill
  ("McDougall came up with the pack-train and reported the firing when he
  reported his arrival to Reno", p. 373). Reno's thirteen men and Herendeen
  rejoin (p. 374).
- **Captions.** Godfrey p. 373: "We heard two distinct volleys which excited
  some surprise ... I have but little doubt now that these volleys were fired
  by Custer's orders as signals of distress and to indicate where he was." Two
  Moon 1898: "Then the Sioux rode up the ridge on all sides, riding very fast.
  The Cheyennes went up the left way."

### 9. `weir-point` - The command reaches Weir Point and sees the end of Custer's fight (17:00)

- **Time.** Godfrey: "About five o'clock the command moved down toward
  Custer's supposed whereabouts, intending to join him" (p. 374). Varnum for
  the return to the stand: "About half past 5 o'clock is as near as I can fix
  it" (vol. 1, p. 176). Wallace: "We got back between 5 and 6 o'clock; it must
  have commenced about 3 o'clock" (p. 41). Two Moon 1898 has the last stand end
  with "about a hundred men and five horsemen stood on the hill all bunched
  together" and then "the bunch of men, may be so forty, started toward the
  river".
- **Custer's battalion.** **Destroyed**, strength 0, both wings. Gall via
  Godfrey p. 381: "the whole mass rushed upon and crushed Calhoun. The maddened
  mass of Indians was carried forward by its own momentum over Calhoun and
  Crittenden down into the depression where Keogh was, with over thirty men,
  and all was over on that part of the field." Then "Gall's bloody work was
  finished before the annihilation of Custer was accomplished, and his
  victorious warriors hurried forward to the hot encounter then going on, and
  the frightful massacre was completed." The break toward Deep Ravine ("About
  twenty-eight bodies of men belonging to this troop and other organizations
  were found in one ravine nearer the river", p. 381; Maguire p. 703: "The
  column which retreated along the line B H E must have been dismounted, and,
  fighting along the whole distance, a portion of its men, taking to the ravine
  H for shelter, must have been surrounded by the Indians. There were
  twenty-eight bodies found in this ravine. From H to E stretched a line of
  dead men with skirmish intervals. The crest E was literally covered with
  dead officers and men") is caption matter, and
  the archaeology cannot say whether it "was organized or a helter-skelter
  event". Whether the collapse ran Calhoun, Keogh, Custer Hill in that order is
  Gall's account and Fox's model, not something the ground proves; MWAC 124:
  "Tactical disintegration of the then surviving elements of the command most
  likely occurred." At the fine level `keogh-wing` steps to destroyed here and
  `yates-wing` with it; a split phase 9a/9b would put fifteen minutes between
  them on no source's watch.
- **Reno, Benteen, pack train.** All engaged, `column`, strung along the
  bluffs to Weir Point, heading 340. Godfrey p. 374: "Looking toward Custer's
  field, on a hill two miles away we saw a large assemblage ... The firing
  ceased, the groups dispersed, clouds of dust arose from all parts of the
  field, and the horsemen converged toward our position." Archaeology: "evidence
  for the use of twelve different guns moving from Sharpshooter Hill then to the
  knoll, and finally to the Company K position in the perimeter" (MWAC 124).
- **Village.** Both bodies engaged, heading 160, converging on Weir Point.
- **Captions.** Godfrey p. 381: "All the Indian accounts that I know of agree
  that there was no organized close-quarters fighting, except on the two
  flanks; that with the annihilation at Custer's Hill the battle was virtually
  over." Red Horse: "The different soldiers [i. e., Custer's battalion] that the
  Sioux killed made five brave stands."

### 10. `reno-hill` - The command falls back to Reno Hill and is surrounded (19:00)

- **Time.** Godfrey: "They had in the mean time sent a large force up the
  valley, and soon our position was entirely surrounded. It was now about seven
  o'clock" (p. 375). NPS: "Here Reno and Benteen established a defense perimeter
  at about 7 p.m." Benteen: the fire "began about half-past five o'clock, when
  our line finished, or may be a little later" (vol. 2, p. 14). Reno-rpt: "This
  was about 6 p. m. We held our ground, with a loss of eighteen enlisted men
  killed and forty-six wounded, until the attack ceased, about 9 p. m." (p. 477).
- **Reno, Benteen, pack train.** All engaged, `mass`, on Reno Hill, heading
  350 toward Sharpshooter Hill, the heaviest fire. Godfrey p. 375 gives the
  layout: the ridge "occupied by five troops", the hospital and animals in the
  ravine, Moylan across the ravine with "the packs and dead animals being
  utilized for breastworks", Benteen's troop on "The high hill on the south."
  The K company rear guard on foot ("I dismounted my men to fight on foot,
  deploying as rapidly as possible without waiting for the formation laid down
  in tactics", p. 375) is caption matter.
- **Village.** Engaged, `mass`, ringing the hill: "The distribution of Indian
  associated cartridge cases confirms the historical accounts that the warriors
  surrounded the command and poured a heavy fire into the defense perimeter";
  "Sharpshooter Hill, a small knoll located north of the defense perimeter, and
  the ravines south of the defense perimeter" (MWAC 124). Fine level: one body
  north, one south, which is authoring, not testimony.
- **Captions.** Godfrey p. 375: the Indians "took possession of all the
  surrounding high points". Low Dog: "Then I turned round and went to help
  fight the other white warriors, who had retreated to a high hill on the east
  side of the river."

### 11. `dark` - Firing ceases at dark; the command digs in; the village dances (21:30)

- **Time.** Godfrey: "The firing continued till nearly dark (between nine and
  ten o'clock)" (p. 375). Reno: "Twilight lasted till about 9 o'clock, that is
  the way I get at the duration of the fight" (vol. 2, p. 185) and "They
  withdrew from us at about 9 o'clock" (p. 187). Girard, in the timber: "Nine
  o'clock, when we left the timber. It was then dark" (vol. 1, p. 98). Local
  civil dusk was 20:31; the watches were not far from the sun.
- **Units.** No position changes. The village unit drawn off to the camp,
  heading 350, engaged. Strengths: "Our loss on the hill had been eighteen
  killed and fifty-two wounded" by the end of the 26th (Godfrey p. 379), most of
  it on the 26th.
- **Captions.** Godfrey pp. 375-377: "Their camp was a veritable pandemonium.
  All night long they continued their frantic revels"; Reno-rpt: "All this night
  the men were busy and the Indians holding a scalp dance underneath us in the
  bottom and in our hearing."

### 12. `dawn-attack` - Fire reopens at first light; Benteen's charge (02:45, day 1)

- **Time.** Reno-rpt: "at daylight about 2-30 a.m. I heard the crack of two
  rifles." Godfrey: "the enemy opened fire at early dawn, between half-past two
  and three o'clock" (p. 377). Wallace: "At that season of the year, it must
  have been a little before 3 o'clock" (vol. 1, p. 66). Benteen: "At early
  day-light about half past 3 o'clock" (vol. 2, p. 20). Godfrey RCOI: "We did
  not have the local time, our watches were not changed. I think it was about
  three o'clock or earlier" (vol. 2, p. 102). Local nautical dawn was about
  02:45 and civil dawn 03:34.
- **Units.** Reno engaged, `mass`, strength 0.6; Benteen engaged, `mass`,
  strength 0.85 ("H ... suffered greater losses than any other", Godfrey
  p. 377); pack train engaged, `mass`. The village engaged, `mass`, ringing the
  hill, heading 170. Benteen's charge and Reno's sortie of "75 or 100 yards"
  are caption matter. McDougall: "The engagement began the next morning about
  half past two o'clock ... being very heavy towards 10 o'clock when they made a
  general sally" (vol. 2, p. 143).
- **Captions.** Reno-rpt: "We could see as the day brightened countless herds
  of them pouring up the valley from out the village and scampering over the
  high points towards the places designated for them by their chiefs and which
  entirely surrounded our position." Godfrey p. 378: "Benteen called out 'All
  ready now, men. Now's your time. Give them hell. Hip, hip, here we go!'"

### 13. `lull` - Water parties; the fire slackens and the warriors draw off (11:00, day 1)

- **Time.** Godfrey: "About 11 A. M. the firing was slack, and parties of
  volunteers were formed to get water ... By about one o'clock the Indians had
  nearly all left us, but they still guarded the river ... About two o'clock the
  Indians came back, opened fire, and drove us to the trenches again, but by
  three o'clock the firing had ceased altogether" (p. 378). Reno-rpt: "The fire
  did not slacken until about 9-30 a.m." Porter: "about 4 o'clock in the
  afternoon of the 26th the firing ceased altogether" (vol. 1, p. 208); Mathey:
  "about half past three or four o'clock" (vol. 2, p. 147); McDougall: "The
  Indians withdrew about five o'clock when Major Reno put us in a new
  position" (vol. 2, p. 144). The lull is one phase; the afternoon return and
  the cease are its caption.
- **Units.** No army moves. The village engaged, `mass`, drawing off toward
  the camp, heading 300, with an `intent` arrow at the river ford it still
  guarded.
- **Captions.** Godfrey p. 378 as above; Reno-rpt: "We took advantage of this
  lull to fill all vessels with water, and soon had it by camp kettle full."

### 14. `village-departs` - The village moves off toward the Bighorn Mountains (19:00, day 1; end 22:00)

- **Time.** Godfrey: "About 7 P. M. we saw emerge from behind this screen of
  smoke an immense moving mass crossing the plateau, going toward the Big Horn
  Mountains" (p. 378). Benteen: "They commenced moving about sunset and they
  were in sight till darkness" (vol. 2, p. 24). Reno-note: "They left their camp
  last evening at sundown moving due south in the direction of Big Horn
  mountains." Reno-rpt: "It was between 6 and 7 p. m. that the village came out
  from behind the dense clouds of smoke and dust. We had a close and good view
  of them as they filed away in the direction of Big Horn Mountains, moving in
  almost perfect military order" (p. 478). Terry: "till 6 o'clock of the 26th,
  when the Indians withdrew from the valley, taking with them their village."
  Wallace: "about sunset, or before sunset" (vol. 1, p. 67). NPS: "On the
  evening of June 26, the entire village began to move to the south." Local
  sunset 19:53.
- **Village.** Engaged, `mass`, strength 0.95, position on the bench west of
  the river moving south-south-west; the track carries the move and the last
  phase's end at 22:00 lets it run off. State stays `engaged`: a state is a
  condition and never a place.
- **Army units.** Engaged, `mass`; Reno-rpt: "During the night I changed my
  position in order to secure an unlimited supply of water". DeRudio's party
  comes in (Godfrey p. 379). The 27th (Terry "About 9.30 A. M.", Godfrey p.
  382; "about 10-30 a.m. that General Terry rode into my lines", Reno-rpt;
  Bradley's "one hundred and ninety-seven dead bodies", Helena Herald 1876) is
  caption matter beyond `end`, as the Nile's 3 August is.
- **Captions.** Utley 1969: "About 7 p.m. an immense procession of horsemen,
  women and children on foot, travois, ponies, and dogs emerged from behind the
  smoke." Two Moon 1898: "There were 388. There were thirty-nine Sioux and seven
  Cheyennes killed, and about a hundred wounded."

## Conjecture: what a phase can honestly assert

T = testimony (a witness who was there, army or Native), M = the Maguire 1876
survey or Godfrey's 1892 plan (positions of the dead), A = archaeology (the
1984-85, 1989, 1994-96 and 2004 surveys as reported in MWAC 124 and 94), R =
reconstruction (Gray, Fox, Utley, the NPS narrative, or this note's geometry).
Times are dealt with in the clock section and are T-with-a-spread throughout.

Two rules fall out. **Custer's battalion is drawn from the ground, not from a
watch:** every one of its positions from phase 5 to 9 is where cases and bodies
were found, and every one of its times is borrowed from what the men on the
bluffs heard. **The Native side is drawn from its own accounts and from the
archaeology, and the two agree better than either agrees with Godfrey:** the
Native cases on Greasy Grass Ridge, Henry Ridge and the knoll north-east of
Last Stand Hill are where Gall, Two Moon and Wooden Leg say they were. The
split of the village into two bodies is the note's device for showing that,
and its `notes` should say so in every phase where it is not attested (1-2, 10-14).

## Battle clock: what precision is honest

- **The watches were not set to the sun and not to each other.** Godfrey: "We
  compared watches to get the official time" on the evening of 22 June (p. 365).
  Wallace, asked whether "the time by your watch was the true time of day, when
  you looked at it, or may it have been an hour or more slow or fast": "I am not
  sure about that. It may have been fast or it may have been slow. I never have
  claimed that it was the local time of the place" (vol. 1, p. 77). Godfrey: "We
  did not have the local time, our watches were not changed" (vol. 2, p. 102).
  Girard's watch had just come from the jeweller and he moved his own times by
  an hour under cross-examination (vol. 1, pp. 98, 108, 124). Varnum: "Any
  statement I may make in regard to time would be a guess on my part ... I base
  my opinion a good deal on other people's opinions" (vol. 1, p. 145). Benteen
  and Reno "fixed by the best information I could get" (vol. 2, p. 187).
- **The spread on the same event:**

  | Event | Earliest | Latest | Preferred |
  |-------|----------|--------|-----------|
  | Division at the divide | "about 11 or 12" (DeRudio) | "a quarter after 12" (Wallace) | 12:15 |
  | Custer's order to Reno | "about 12.30" (Reno-rpt, "a. m." as printed) | "a quarter after two" (Wallace) | 14:15 |
  | Reno crosses | "about 2" (Reno-note) | "20 minutes after two" (Wallace) | 14:30 |
  | Reno leaves the timber | "10 minutes past 2" (Girard, corrected from 1:10) | "a quarter after three" (Wallace) | 15:20 |

- **The honest statement.** Reno's fight is pinned to about half an hour by
  Wallace's chain of watch readings; Benteen's arrival to about an hour and a
  half; Custer's fight has no watch of its own and is placed by the ear of men
  three miles away and by the light. Local sun events bracket the regiment's
  watches to within about an hour of local mean time, so a viewer reading "16:30"
  should read "late afternoon by the regiment's watches". Gray's 1991 time-motion
  reconstruction, the standard modern chronology, is in copyright and was not
  read for this note; if a file follows it, `notes` must say so.
- **In the file.** `t` copies one witness's reading verbatim, as the schema
  requires, and each phase's `notes` names the witness and the spread. Nothing
  finer than the quarter-hour is claimed anywhere and the half-hour is claimed
  only for Reno's fight. `dates` is `["25 June 1876", "26 June 1876"]`,
  phases 12-14 carry `day: 1`, `end` is `"22:00"` on `end_day: 1`, and the 27th
  is a caption coda. A `playback_rate` plan: phases 1-2 fast (two hours of
  approach march), 3-9 slow (the two and a half decisive hours), 10 moderate,
  11 very fast (five hours of dark in a few seconds), 12-13 fast, 14 moderate.

## Arms: does v2 carry dismounted cavalry and Native fighters?

**Yes, without a change, and ADR-0015 already decided how.** The 7th Cavalry
fought almost every minute of this battle on foot: Reno's skirmish line
("dismounted his command to fight on foot", Godfrey p. 371), the timber, the
bluff edge ("dismount and deploy as skirmishers", p. 372), Keogh's and
Calhoun's companies ("dismounted two troops ... to fight on foot", p. 380), the
perimeter. ADR-0015 rejected arm as per-phase state: "Cavalry that dismounts
... is two units or caption matter". So every 7th Cavalry unit is `cavalry`
throughout, the skirmish line is `formation: line` with the dismounting and the
horse-holders (one man in four; Remington's "Dismounted - the fourth troopers
holding the led horses", p. 376) in the caption, the mounted advance and the
retreat are `column`, and the perimeter is `mass`. The chart plate's barred
rank bar is the sign whether the men are in the saddle or not, and that is
right: the label says what the unit is, the caption says what it is doing.

The Native fighters were mounted for the valley fight and the pursuits and
largely on foot for the close work on the Custer field ("A large force
dismounted and advanced up the slope far enough to be able to see the soldiers
when standing erect ... In the mean time Gall was massing his mounted warriors
under the protection of the slope", Godfrey p. 381; Low Dog's "As we rushed
upon them the white warriors dismounted to fire"). The arm of a unit is that of
its main body (ADR-0017), and the main body of a plains village's fighting men
is mounted: `cavalry`, with "on foot" in the captions where the accounts say
so. Nothing in the fixed list is wrong for them; what is odd is the legend
row, which will key the village as "cavalry" beside the 7th. That is a label
and legend matter, not an arm, and the note recommends against a `warrior` or
`irregular` arm: it would be a sign every view must draw for a distinction the
side name already carries. The one arm this map may need is not this
battle's.

## Map data

- **Extent.** The valley fight, the bluffs and the Custer field fit in a box
  from 45.49 to 45.60 N and 107.50 to 107.35 W (about 12 by 12 km). The divide
  and the Crow's Nest lie 20 km east of that, and phase 1 puts every army unit
  there. Three ways out: (a) widen the extent to 107.10 W, making a 31 by 21
  km landscape plate in which the valley fight occupies the western third and
  the approach march is drawn; (b) start the phase list at the lone tepee and
  put the divide in the first caption, with units entering from the east edge
  (positions outside the extent are legal and clipped); (c) keep (a) but drop
  the Crow's Nest and cut at 107.20 W. The note prefers (a): the approach and
  the division are the best-attested hour of the day and the plate should show
  them. Proposed: north 45.62, south 45.43, west -107.52, east -107.10,
  `scale_unit` `km`.
- **The battle is inland.** Nothing in the extent is sea. ADR-0012 withdrew
  the `ground` field and said "A genuinely inland battle can add the field
  additively when one is authored". This is that battle, and it does not need
  the field: one `land` polygon larger than the extent makes everything land,
  the river is a `river` line "clipped to the land", and the v1 rule stands. The
  schema fog on #121 can record that the first inland battle was authored
  without `ground`.
- **Elevation.** SRTM 1 arc-second tile **N45W108** covers the whole extent;
  `https://opentopography.s3.sdsc.edu/raster/SRTM_GL1/SRTM_GL1_srtm/N45W108.tif`
  answers HTTP 200 (12.6 MB GeoTIFF, anonymous, no login), the same route the
  Cannae map used. NASA's SRTM is public domain (LP DAAC: "openly shared,
  without restriction"). USGS 3DEP 1/3 arc-second (10 m) tile n46w108 also
  answers 200 at `https://prd-tnm.s3.amazonaws.com/StagedProducts/Elevation/13/TIFF/current/n46w108/USGS_13_n46w108.tif`
  and is a US government work. Relief is small: the valley floor about 930 m,
  the bluffs "from 80 to 90 feet" above it (Maguire), Reno Hill "the highest
  land in the neighborhood" with about 150 feet between it and the ford (vol. 1,
  p. 12), Last Stand Hill about 1,000 m. A 10 m interval draws the bluffs as
  three lines; a 5 m interval may be wanted on this map, which ADR-0012 leaves
  to the author. Every ridge that matters (Sharpshooter Hill, Weir Point,
  Nye-Cartwright, Calhoun Hill, Custer Ridge, Greasy Grass Ridge) is under 100 m
  of relief.
- **The river in 1876 and today.** One map file is one moment (ADR-0012), and
  the Little Bighorn has moved. GRI 2011: "Comparison of the Little Bighorn
  River channel between modern channel (blue) and an 1891 map depiction
  produced soon after the battle (orange). This comparison highlights that the
  meanders have shifted significantly"; "By the early 1960s, more than one-half
  (approximately 53%) of the 193 km (120 mi) of the main stem of the Little
  Bighorn River had been modified via channel relocation, riprap, channel
  clearing, and diking". The Oxbow report registers the 1891 USGS survey
  (Thompson, Tweedy and Marshall, field work 1891, printed 1908, 1:24,000 with
  25 ft contours) to the modern map at 4.6 m RMS and finds "major changes in
  the river valley southwest of Last Stand Hill" and "changes in the valley
  below Weir Point", while on the Custer field "there are no significant
  channel changes" between 1935 and 2005; the eight Maguire sheets register at
  77.6 m RMS at best. The Garryowen bend "was an active channel during the
  battle" on the reading MWAC 124 follows ("Reno's timber fight area was
  protected by the river on two sides"), and the Medicine Tail ford has been
  re-aligned by a culvert. **Recommendation:** an 1891 map file, the river
  traced from the 1891 USGS sheet (a USGS work, public domain; the sheet itself
  was not located online for this note and is the first follow-up), the
  Garryowen bend restored as the sources describe it, the modern channel from
  the USGS National Hydrography Dataset (public domain) only where the 1891
  sheet is silent, and the captions of phases 4-6 saying which river is drawn.
  An OpenStreetMap river would make the file ODbL and is ruled out by
  ADR-0007.
- **Works and places.** The camp circles are `work` points, as Cannae's camps
  are: Hunkpapa (north of the Garryowen store), Blackfeet, Sans Arc,
  Miniconjou, Oglala (back from the river), Cheyenne (opposite Medicine Tail
  Coulee), in Wooden Leg's order, with Red Horse's order in the research note
  rather than the file. Places: Reno Hill, Weir Point, Medicine Tail Coulee,
  Calhoun Hill, Custer Hill, Deep Ravine, Reno's crossing, the lone tepee, the
  divide. No 1890s town (Garryowen, Crow Agency) belongs on an 1876 plate.
  Modern land cover is irrigated farmland on the valley floor and grazing on
  the bluffs, inside the Crow Reservation (GRI); none of it is drawn.
- **Period plans.** Maguire's map (RCOI Exhibit 2, viewed; public domain)
  shows the river with its 1876 meanders, the village as tepee symbols across
  the west bank, the timber, Reno's rifle pits, the dotted trails, twelve
  numbered officers' graves from Calhoun and Crittenden to Custer and a scale
  of miles, and Maguire told the Court "except with regard to the relative
  position of points, is a mere sketch; it is not like a survey made with a
  transit and chain" (vol. 1, p. 10). Godfrey's 1892 plan (p. 370, lettered A
  to E; public domain) was read only in OCR. Neither is geo-registered enough
  to trace a river from; both fix where the dead lay. The 1891 USGS sheet, the
  1935 aerial photograph ("the earliest aerial photography of the river that is
  known", Oxbow) and the NPS handbook maps are the geo-registered public-domain
  layers.

## Model extraction from Godfrey: what was done and how it fared

As the map's standing practice allows, a first draft of the phase list was
extracted by a model (a sub-agent reading the archive.org OCR of the *Century*
article) before any other source was read. It produced 23 rows with a verbatim
sentence and a page number for each, from the 2 a.m. halt on the 25th to the
burials on the 28th, plus Godfrey's watch statements, his formation language,
his Gall sequence, his map legend and his figures.

How it fared, judged against the rest of the reading:

- **Good as a skeleton for the army side.** Twenty of its rows survive in
  the fourteen phases above or in their captions, every locator checked out
  against the OCR, and its rows for Reno's fight, Weir's advance, the perimeter
  and the 26th needed no correction of substance. It caught Godfrey's own
  hedges (the 4:20 note "I have never satisfactorily been able to recall what
  particular incident happened at that time", the "some say 'a few minutes';
  others, 'about an hour'") without being asked.
- **Wrong on the clock wherever Godfrey is.** It took "about half-past two"
  for Benteen joining at face value; the Court's witnesses put it at three to
  after four, and Godfrey himself said "between 3 and 4" under oath in 1879. A
  single-source extraction inherits its source's watch.
- **Blind to the Native side.** Godfrey names chiefs and reproduces Gall, but
  the draft had one column for "Indians" and nothing for where any body of
  warriors was; the two-body split, the camp-circle order and every Native
  position came from the 1877-1931 accounts and the archaeology, none of which
  a Godfrey extraction can supply.
- **Wrong on the Custer field where Godfrey is.** It carried Godfrey's denial
  that Custer went to the ford ("his column was never nearer the river or
  village than his final position on the ridge") as the phase, where Martin,
  Curley and the cartridge cases say otherwise. It could not produce positions
  for Custer's companies, only the legend's lettered points.
- **Too many phases before the battle and after it.** Six of its rows were 22
  to 24 June or the 27th and 28th; the phase list needs none of them.

Verdict: the practice works for the side the source belongs to, and only for
the sequence; times, positions and the other side have to come from the other
sources, and the correction took longer than the extraction. That is the same
lesson Cannae's Polybius-first reading taught, with the Native accounts here
playing Livy's part.

## What v2 cannot express

1. **Evidence class.** No field says a position is testimony, archaeology or
   reconstruction (ADR-0001, no uncertainty field). At Cannae that cost one
   sunrise; here it costs the whole Custer field and the whole Native side. Every
   phase's `notes` must carry the conjecture table's letters, and the schema fog
   on #121 ("conjecture") should know that this battle is the one where a
   viewer will most want to see them on the plate. The note does not recommend a
   field: the letters are prose about sources, which is what `notes` is for, and
   a `basis` enum would be styling in data the moment a view drew it.
2. **A unit with no witness.** Custer's battalion needs a position, heading,
   formation and state in all fourteen phases; from 5 to 9 those are the
   author's reading of bodies and cartridge cases. The schema treats it exactly
   like Reno's battalion. That is right and unavoidable; `notes` says so five
   times.
3. **A watch that is not the sun.** The clock is a reading (ADR-0013) and
   `t` copies one witness verbatim, so nothing breaks; but a battle where the
   same event has five readings ninety minutes apart, and where the sun says the
   watches were about an hour off local time, has nowhere to put the second
   witness but `notes`. Fine, and recorded.
4. **Dismounted cavalry and fighters on foot.** Carried by `cavalry` plus
   formation plus caption, as ADR-0015 decided; the horse-holders and the
   stampede of the led horses, the fact that decided Calhoun Hill, are prose.
5. **A body with no front.** The perimeter on Reno Hill was fired into "from
   opposite sides of the lines" (Reno-rpt); `heading` is required and single,
   so the author points it at Sharpshooter Hill. Cannae's encircled legions had
   the same problem and kept their heading south; a siege makes it the normal
   case rather than the last phase.
6. **A gap on the clock.** Phase 11 holds from 21:30 to 02:45 with nothing
   moving; v2 carries it as a phase with a high `playback_rate` and a caption
   that earns its place (the entrenching, the dance, the men in the timber).
   There is no "pause" or "gap" concept and the scrubber segment is sized by
   the phase's playback duration, so the author sets the rate. The fog item on
   #121 ("a siege's gaps on the clock") can close on this: the mechanism exists
   and is authoring.
7. **A village that leaves.** The camp circles are `work` points on a map that
   is one moment, so the tepees stay drawn after 19:00 on the 26th while the
   `village` unit's track walks away from them. Nothing marks a work as gone.
   Caption matter, and the last phase is short.
8. **A three-mile village as points.** `work` is a point, never a polygon
   (ADR-0012); six or seven points along the river stand for the circles, and
   the pony herds, the women and children and the wickiups are prose.
9. **Two "wings" that are not commands.** `commander` is an identity field and
   the Native bodies had none in the army sense; a name in it overstates what
   the accounts say. Recommend the field carry the name the sources attach with
   the qualification in `label` or `notes`, or be left empty on the two bodies.
10. **Units that arrive.** The thirteen men and Herendeen (phase 8), DeRudio's
    four (phase 14), the forty captured ponies, and Terry's column on the 27th.
    The first three are strength that returns, which ADR-0018 allows and costs
    nothing; Terry is outside `end`. Units appearing mid-battle stays deferred
    and this battle does not force it.
11. **A messenger.** Martin's ride with Cooke's note is the one thing every
    viewer knows about this battle and it is a single trumpeter; not a
    detachment, not a move, caption only.
12. **The inland map.** Solved without `ground` by one land polygon; recorded
    so the withdrawal in ADR-0012 stays withdrawn.
13. **A river of the wrong year.** As at Cannae, nothing marks geometry as
    reconstructed; the 1891 river stands for the 1876 one and the captions of
    phases 4-6 say so. Here the substitute is forty years off rather than two
    thousand, and a period survey exists, which is why the note recommends an
    1891 map file rather than a modern one.
14. **Sixteen units.** Never approached: nine on the roster, five or nine on
    the plate. Little Bighorn is not the battle that tests the ceiling; Midway
    is.

## Open follow-ups

- **The 1891 USGS sheet** (Thompson, Tweedy and Marshall) was not located
  online; it is the river the map file should trace. The USGS historical
  topographic map collection (topoView) is the place to look.
- **Senate Ex. Doc. 81** (Terry's dispatches as first printed) was not found
  in an open scan; the Secretary of War's 1876 volume and the Army and Navy
  Journal printings were used instead and agree with each other apart from
  telegraph garbles ("fifty-four" for "51" in the Journal's relay text).
- **Maguire's 1876 report** carries no prismatic compass, only odometers and
  a sextant; the compass is in his testimony. Neither report gives a village
  length; that figure is Terry's.
- **Godfrey's map** (p. 370) was read in OCR of its legend, not viewed; the
  archive.org leaf 47 image will show his drawn route.
- **The St. Paul Pioneer Press of 18 July 1886**, the Gall original, sits
  behind the Minnesota Historical Society's JavaScript hub; the three
  syndicated reprints agree with each other.
- **Wooden Leg's renewal** is a finding by absence in the 1958-60 Catalog of
  Copyright Entries; a search of the Copyright Office's records would settle it.
- **Coordinates** of the Crow's Nest, Ford A, the skirmish line, the timber
  and the camp circles are OSM nodes or prose and need fixing against the 1891
  sheet before they go in a file; every position in the phases above is the
  author's geometry on those points.
- **Gray 1991** was not read; if the file's clock follows his reconstruction
  rather than Wallace's chain, the phase `notes` must say so and the file may
  not cite him as a source (in copyright).
