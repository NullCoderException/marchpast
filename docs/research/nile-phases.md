# Nile phase list at squadron granularity and public-domain sources

*Research note for GitHub issue #42 (part of the wayfinder map, #36). Written 2026-09-06. Every locator below was fetched during this session; nothing is from memory. Where a fetch failed it is said so.*

## Question

What is the phase list for the Battle of the Nile (Aboukir Bay, 1 to 2 August 1798) at **squadron granularity**, from the sighting about 14:00 through the night action, L'Orient's explosion and the morning after to Villeneuve's escape; how should the schema record a battle that crosses midnight; which units does it need; what does each phase hold (positions in the bay relative to Aboukir Island and the shoal, headings, wind, state, strength, moves); which public-domain sources support it; and what can schema v1 not express?

## Short answer

**Eleven candidate phases**, spanning two calendar days. Times are battle clock (ships' time; the five primary witnesses disagree by up to 45 minutes on the same event, see the disagreement table). The headline time is Berry's unless noted; Berry, on Nelson's quarterdeck, is the only narrative that gives minutes throughout.

`end` = 2 August 14:00, the public thanksgiving in Vanguard, after which the picture stops changing. A twelfth phase on 3 August (Tonnant struck, Timoléon burnt about noon) is caption matter unless the file wants a third day.

If eight phases are preferred, merge 2 into 1, 7 into 6 and 10 into 9. Phases 4, 5, 8 and 11 must stay separate: they are where units change state.

**Midnight.** Phases 9 to 11 fall on 2 August. Schema v1's `t` is `"HH:MM"` on the battle's single `date` and must strictly increase, so this battle **cannot be written in v1 as one file**. Recommendation: add an optional per-phase `day` integer (0 = the battle's `date`, 1 = the next day, default 0) and order phases by (`day`, `t`); `end` gets the same treatment. Details in the implications section.

**Units.** Six at squadron granularity (three British, three French), plus an optional seventh for the French frigates. Three would do for the British as one unit and the French as van-and-centre versus rear, at the cost of losing Goliath's inshore pass and Bellerophon's drift, which is most of what makes the Nile the Nile.

## Fixed reference points

- **Aboukir (Nelson's) Island**: 31.3585 N, 30.1062 E (WP "Nelson's Island" infobox). Mahan: "Two miles northeast of the promontory of Aboukir is Aboukir Island, since called Nelson's, linked with the point by a chain of rocks. Outside the island, similar rocks, with shoals, prolong this foul ground under water to seaward, constituting a reef dangerous to a stranger approaching the bay." Hence **Aboukir point and castle** at about 31.33 N, 30.07 E (derived). Berry: Culloden "grounded upon the tail of a shoal running off from the Island, on which were the mortar and gun batteries of the Enemy"; Blanquet: "ran on the reef, E.N.E. of the Island".
- **The bay**: Mahan: "Aboukir Bay begins at a promontory of the same name, and, after curving boldly south, extends eastward eighteen miles, terminating at the Rosetta mouth of the Nile. From the shore the depth increases very gradually, so that water enough for ships-of-the-line was not found till three miles from the coast." EB1911: "some 15 m. north-east of Alexandria". Alexandria's Pharos is at 31.21 N, 29.885 E (derived from the WP coordinates of Alexandria; only needed for the Vanguard journal's 16:00 fix "Pharos Tower S.S.W. distant four or five leagues").
- **The French line**: Blanquet: "Having the Ships' heads S.E. from the Island of Bequier, forming a Line about 1300 fathoms N.W. and S.E., distant from each other eighty fathoms ... each with an anchor out, in the S.S.E." Mahan: "The general direction of his line was that of the wind, from northwest to southeast, with a very slight bend ... The leading, northwestern, ship was brought close to the shoal in thirty feet of water ... there were between the successive ships intervals of five hundred feet." Hood: "The Van ship of the Enemy being in five fathom." Berry: "moored in a strong and compact Line of Battle, close in with the shore, their line describing an obtuse angle in its form, flanked by numerous Gun-boats, four Frigates, and a battery of guns and mortars, on an Island in their Van." WP (citing Clowes, James): Guerrier "2,400 yards southeast of Aboukir Island", "about 1,000 yards from the edge of the shoals", the line "2,850 yards long" at "160 yards" spacing, "approximately 1,650 yards from the shore", frigates "approximately 350 yards west of the main line". Blanquet's 1,300 fathoms (2,600 yd) and 80 fathoms (160 yd) agree with these to within a ship's length.
- **Derived positions** (bearing 135 from the island, 1 nmi = 0.0167 deg lat, 0.0195 deg lon at 31.35 N): Guerrier 31.345 N 30.122 E; French van centre (ships 1 to 6) 31.342 N 30.126 E; centre (7 to 10) 31.338 N 30.131 E; rear (11 to 13) 31.334 N 30.134 E; frigates 31.339 N 30.124 E. Authoring estimates, to be checked against the Pocock and Dodd plans below.
- **Wind**: NNW throughout. Berry: "The wind was at this time N.N.W., and blew what seamen call a top-gallant breeze." Blanquet: "The 1st of August, wind N.N.W., light breezes and fine weather." Mahan: "a whole-sail breeze, too fresh for the lighter canvas"; and on the night of the 1st, "the wind being so scant that they could barely pass to windward of the reef". Written `{ "from": 337.5, "force": "fresh" }` for the afternoon, `moderate` after dark; the sources give no shift. (WP's "prevailing northeasterly wind" is an outlier and contradicted by every primary witness.)
- **Sunset**: Berry "thirty-one minutes past six P.M."; Mahan "quarter before seven"; Nelson to Howe "at twenty-eight minutes past six, the sun in the horizon". Total darkness about 19:00 (Berry). Moonrise at the explosion (Mahan).
- **Strengths**: Gaz "Line of Battle": 13 British of 74 guns plus Leander 50 and the brig Mutine; French 13 of the line (Guerrier, Conquérant, Spartiate, Aquilon, Souverain Peuple, Franklin, L'Orient, Tonnant, L'Heureux, Timoléon, Mercure, Guillaume Tell, Généreux) and four frigates (Diane, Justice, Artémise, Sérieuse). Southey: "1196 guns and 11,230 men" against "1012 guns and 8068 men". Gaz fates: nine taken, L'Orient and Timoléon burnt, Guillaume Tell and Généreux escaped; frigates Diane and Justice escaped, Artémise burnt, Sérieuse "Dismasted and sunk".

Three units would be: `british-squadron`, `french-van-and-centre`, `french-rear`. The British unit would sit on the French van with a `detachment` arrow for the reserve; the French van-and-centre would go `broken` at the explosion. Workable, but Goliath inside and Vanguard outside become one marker.

## The phases

Times are battle clock on 1 August unless the day is given. Positions are estimates from the reference points above. Headings: an anchored French ship rides to her bower anchor, head to wind, so the French units carry heading 315 (NW, along their own line) from the first phase; the British anchored by the stern after running down the line, so they carry the heading they arrived on, about 135 (SE), with springs to swing the broadside. Both are authoring choices the sources support only indirectly.

### 1. `sighting` — Zealous signals the French at anchor (14:00)

- **Time.** Berry and Blanquet agree on 14:00: Berry, "We saw the Pharos of Alexandria at noon on the first of August ... the Enemy's Fleet was first discovered by the Zealous, Captain Hood, who immediately communicated, by signal, the number of Ships, sixteen, laying at anchor in Line of Battle, in a Bay upon the larboard bow"; Blanquet, "At two o'clock in the afternoon, the Heureux made the Signal for 12 Sail, W.N.W." Mahan: "at 2.45 in the afternoon". **Vanguard's journal (Nic p. 49) puts the signal at 16:00**: "At 4, Pharos Tower S.S.W. distant four or five leagues, the Zealous made the signal for the French Fleet, sixteen Sail of the Line. At 5, bore up for the French Fleet." Two-hour spread; Berry, Blanquet and WP (14:00) carry it.
- **Inshore squadron / Nelson's division.** One body of ten ships steering about east (Mahan: "The remaining ten ships, which had been steering about east"), 9 nmi WNW of the bay mouth (WP, from the French sighting), about 31.42 N 29.92 E. Heading 90. Intact.
- **Reserve.** Mahan: "two ships, the 'Alexander' and 'Swiftsure,' were a dozen miles to leeward, having been sent ahead on frigates' duty to reconnoitre Alexandria ... At the same moment the 'Culloden' was seven miles to windward. She was signalled to drop the prize she was towing, and to join the fleet." Put the unit marker on Culloden (31.50 N 29.80 E) with a `detachment` arrow to Alexander and Swiftsure off Alexandria (31.26 N 29.86 E), or the reverse. Intact.
- **French van, centre, rear, frigates.** At anchor as in the reference points, heading 315, formation `column`, intact. Blanquet: "The second division of the Fleet sent a party of men on shore, to dig wells. Every Ship in the Fleet sent twenty-five men to protect the workmen" (the source of the French short-handedness). Brueys' council in L'Orient (EB1911).
- **Wind.** NNW, fresh.
- **Captions.** Berry (Nic p. 50): "The Enemy appeared to be moored in a strong and compact Line of Battle, close in with the shore, their line describing an obtuse angle in its form, flanked by numerous Gun-boats, four Frigates, and a battery of guns and mortars, on an Island in their Van." Gaz p. 915: "The Enemy were moored in a strong Line of Battle for defending the Entrance of the Bay (of Shoals), flanked by numerous Gun-Boats, Four Frigates, and a Battery of Guns and Mortars on an Island in their Van."

### 2. `haul-the-wind` — The squadron hauls its wind; cables out of the stern ports (15:00)

- **Time.** Vanguard's journal: "At 1/2 past 2, recalled the Alexander and Swiftsure. At 1/2 past 2, hauled our wind, unbent the best bower cable, took it out of the stern port and bent it again." WP: 16:00 Nelson slows to rig springs. Blanquet: "At 3 o'clock, the Admiral not having any doubt but the Ships in sight were the Enemy, he ordered the hammocks to be stowed for Action, and directed the Alert and Railleur Brig, Sloops of War, to reconnoitre the Enemy." Berry: "The Admiral hauled his wind that instant ... The Admiral made the signal to prepare for battle, and that it was his intention to attack the Enemy's van and centre, as they lay at anchor."
- **British.** Close-hauled on the starboard tack to weather the shoal, heading about 45 (NE), royals in (Berry: "It was necessary to take in the royals when we hauled upon a wind"). Position about 31.43 N 30.00 E. The reserve closing: Culloden from the NW, Alexander and Swiftsure from the SW along the reef's outer edge. All intact. An `intent` arrow on the inshore squadron toward the French van (31.345 N 30.122 E) shows the signalled plan.
- **French.** Unchanged. Blanquet: the French brig Alert tries "to draw them toward the outer shoal lying off that Island. But the English Admiral, without doubt, had experienced Pilots on board, as he did not pay any attention to the Brig's track ... he hauled well round all the dangers." Brueys briefly orders the line to sail, then countermands (WP, citing Mostert and Bradford; Blanquet only: "the Admiral got the top-gallant-yards across, but soon after made the signal that he intended engaging the Enemy at anchor").
- **Captions.** Berry (Nic p. 50): "His idea, in this disposition of his force was, first to secure the victory, and then to make the most of it according to future circumstances. A bower cable of each Ship was immediately got out abaft, and bent forward." Berry: "the Admiral viewed these with the eye of a seaman determined on attack, and it instantly struck his eager and penetrating mind, that where there was room for an Enemy's Ship to swing, there was room for one of ours to anchor."

### 3. `round-the-shoal` — Line of battle; Hood sounds round the shoal (17:30)

- **Time.** Mahan: "At about half-past five signal was given to form line of battle"; WP 17:30 for the hail to Hood, 18:00 under full sail. Blanquet: "At five o'clock, the Enemy came to the wind in succession. This manoeuvre convinced us that they intended attacking us that evening." Blanquet also has "At a quarter past five, one of the Enemy's Ships that was steering to get to windward of the headmost of her Line, ran on the reef, E.N.E. of the Island" — which can only be Culloden, whom the British put aground after dark (see disagreements).
- **Inshore squadron.** Goliath and Zealous leading, abreast the shoal NE of the island, heading E, then bearing up to run SW down onto the van: Mahan, "as the long column swept round to head for the French van, the ships turned their port broadsides to the enemy, and were steering southwesterly when they finally ran down." Position at `t` about 31.40 N 30.13 E, heading 90; the tween to phase 4 carries the turn (a quarter-circle, so an intermediate phase at 18:00 heading 225 is worth adding if the arc looks wrong). Intact.
- **Nelson's division.** Astern in the same column, Vanguard sixth (Mahan: "the flagship was dropped to sixth in the order"). About 31.42 N 30.10 E, heading 90. Intact.
- **Reserve.** Culloden approaching the tail of the shoal from the NW; Alexander and Swiftsure "coming up from the southwest, the wind being so scant that they could barely pass to windward of the reef, along whose northwestern edge they were standing" (Mahan). Intact.
- **French.** Unchanged; the island battery opens: Blanquet, "The Battery on the Island opened a fire on the Enemy, and the shells fell ahead of the second Ship in the Line."
- **Captions.** Hood (Nic p. 64): "he asked me if I thought we were far enough to the eastward to bear up clear of this Shoal: I told him I was in eleven fathoms; that I had no chart of the Bay; but if he would allow me, I would bear up and sound with the lead, to which I would be very attentive, and carry him as close as I could with safety: he said he would be obliged to me. I immediately bore away and rounded the Shoal, the Goliath keeping upon my lee-bow." Blanquet (Nic p. 67): "the English Admiral, without doubt, had experienced Pilots on board ... he hauled well round all the dangers."

### 4. `goliath-rounds-the-van` — Sunset: Goliath rounds Guerrier and anchors inside (18:30)

- **Time.** Berry: "The action commenced at sun-set, which was at thirty-one minutes past six P.M." Howe: "At twenty-eight minutes past six, the sun in the horizon, the firing commenced." Mahan: "a little after half-past six". Blanquet: "At half-past five, the headmost Ships of our Line, being within gun-shot of the English, the Admiral made the signal to engage, which was not obeyed until the Enemy were within pistol-shot and just doubling us ... The Conquérant began the fire." WP: French open fire 18:20. Southey: "It commenced at half after six."
- **Inshore squadron.** Goliath crosses Guerrier's bows and, her anchor hanging, brings up inside Conquérant; Zealous anchors inside Guerrier's bow; Orion, Theseus and Audacious follow inside. Position 31.343 N 30.121 E (inside the van), heading 135, `engaged`. Hood: "Captain Foley intended anchoring abreast of the Van ship, but his sheet-anchor, the cable being out of the stern-port, not dropping the moment he wished it, he brought up abreast of the second Ship, having given the Van one his fire. I saw immediately he had failed of his intention, cut away the Zealous's sheet-anchor, and came to, in the exact situation Captain Foley meant to have taken." Southey adds Orion "passing inside the GOLIATH, sunk a frigate which annoyed her" (Sérieuse).
- **Nelson's division.** Running down outside the line under fire, not yet anchored: Berry, "our leading Ships were unavoidably obliged to receive into their bows the whole fire of the broadsides of the French line, until they could take their respective stations". About 31.36 N 30.13 E, heading 225. Intact (under fire, silent).
- **Reserve.** Culloden strikes the tail of the shoal about dark: Mahan, "the day had closed when the ship neared the shoal ... Troubridge had the mishap to strike on the tail of the shoal, and there the ship stuck fast, pounding heavily until the next morning." Strength 0.75 from this phase, or from phase 5 if the author prefers the British timing over Blanquet's 17:15. Position on Culloden, about 31.375 N 30.135 E; `detachment` arrow to Alexander and Swiftsure still to the west (31.36 N 30.04 E).
- **French van.** `engaged`, doubled. Blanquet: "All the Van were attacked on both sides by the Enemy, who ranged close along our Line."
- **French centre and rear.** Intact. Frigates: Blanquet, "At 6 o'clock, the Sérieuse Frigate and the Hercule bomb cut their cables, and got under-weigh, to avoid the Enemy's fire. They got on shore." If a frigate unit exists it goes `broken` here with strength 0.75.
- **Wind.** NNW, moderate (Mahan's "scant" for the stragglers).
- **Captions.** Berry (Nic p. 50): "The Goliath and Zealous had the honour to lead inside, and to receive the first fire from the Van ships of the Enemy, as well as from the Batteries and Gun-boats with which their van was strengthened." Gaz p. 915: "whom I attacked at Sun-set on the 1st of August off the Mouth of the Nile."

### 5. `vanguard-anchors-outside` — Vanguard anchors outside Spartiate; darkness (18:45)

- **Time.** WP: Vanguard, Minotaur, Defence anchored 18:40. Blanquet: "At a quarter past 6, the Franklin opened her fire upon the Enemy from the starboard side: at three-quarters past 6, she was engaged on both sides. The L'Orient at the same time began firing her starboard guns, and at 7 o'clock, the Tonnant opened her fire." Berry: "At about seven o'clock total darkness had come on ... Our Ships, when darkness came on, had all hoisted their distinguishing lights ... The Van ship of the Enemy, Le Guerrier, was dismasted in less than twelve minutes, and, in ten minutes after, the second ship, Le Conquérant, and the third, Le Spartiate, very nearly at the same moment were almost dismasted."
- **Nelson's division.** Vanguard anchors "within half pistol-shot to Le Spartiate, the third in the Enemy's Line" (Berry); Minotaur ahead on Aquilon, Defence on Peuple Souverain, Bellerophon on L'Orient, Majestic fouls Heureux and settles on Mercure's bow (Mahan). Position outside the van-centre seam, 31.339 N 30.130 E, heading 135, `engaged`.
- **Inshore squadron.** Engaged as before.
- **Reserve.** Culloden aground; Leander and Mutine trying to get her off; Alexander and Swiftsure "took their stations in the darkness" (Southey), still approaching. Position unchanged; the `detachment` arrow now points at the French centre.
- **French van.** Engaged, Guerrier and Conquérant dismasted (Southey: "The two first ships of the French line had been dismasted within a quarter of an hour after the commencement of the action").
- **French centre.** `engaged`. **French rear.** Intact; Mahan: "their consorts to leeward looked helplessly on."
- **Captions.** Berry (Nic p. 50-51): "The Vanguard anchored the first on the outer side of the Enemy, and was opposed within half pistol-shot to Le Spartiate, the third in the Enemy's Line ... The Minotaur, Defence, Bellerophon, Majestic, Swiftsure, and Alexander, came up in succession, and passing within hail of the Vanguard, took their respective stations opposed to the Enemy's line. All our Ships anchored by the stern, by which means the British line became inverted from van to rear." Howe (Nic p. 230): "By attacking the Enemy's van and centre, the wind blowing directly along their Line, I was enabled to throw what force I pleased on a few Ships."

### 6. `reserve-arrives` — Bellerophon drifts out; Swiftsure, Alexander and Leander close on L'Orient (20:15)

- **Time.** Blanquet: "At 8 o'clock at night, the Ship which was engaging L'Orient, on the starboard quarter, notwithstanding her advantageous position, was dismasted and so roughly treated, that she cut her cables, and drove rather far from the Line ... at this very moment, the two Ships that had been perceived astern of the Fleet, and were quite fresh, steered right for the centre. One of them anchored on L'Orient's starboard bow, and the other cut the Line, astern of the L'Orient, and anchored on her larboard quarter." WP: Bellerophon cuts 20:20, Swiftsure sights her hulk "shortly after 20:00". Mahan: "The new-comers took their positions soon after eight." Berry: Aquilon and Souverain Peuple "were taken possession of by the British at half-past eight in the evening", Galwey sent to Spartiate at the same hour. Nelson's wound: Mahan "the precise moment has not been recorded"; WP "approximately 20:30"; Berry has it before 20:30 ("the Admiral, who was then below").
- **Nelson's division.** Engaged; a `detachment` arrow for Bellerophon drifting "out of the line to the lee side of the Bay" (Berry), to about 31.32 N 30.16 E. Blanquet at 20:15 also has Peuple Souverain drift "to leeward of the Line, and anchored a cable's length abreast of L'Orient".
- **Reserve.** `engaged`: Swiftsure outside abreast the Orient-Franklin gap, Alexander inside on L'Orient's larboard quarter, Leander athwart Franklin's hawse (Berry: "most judiciously dropped his anchor athwart hause of Le Franklin, raking her with great success, the shot from the Leander's broadside which passed that Ship all striking L'Orient"). Move the marker to 31.337 N 30.128 E; strength stays 0.75 for Culloden aground.
- **French van.** `broken`: three of six taken possession, Guerrier and Conquérant silenced (Guerrier's formal surrender not until 21:00, WP). Strength 0.5.
- **French centre.** Engaged; L'Orient now fought by Swiftsure, Alexander and Leander. Brueys killed about now (Southey, Blanquet).
- **Captions.** Southey ch. V: "It was the BELLEROPHON, overpowered by the huge ORIENT: her lights had gone overboard, nearly 200 of her crew were killed or wounded, all her masts and cables had been shot away; and she was drifting out of the line toward the leeside of the bay." Mahan ch. X: "Her foremast alone was then standing, and it fell immediately after." Southey: "Brueys was dead; he had received three wounds, yet would not leave his post: a fourth cut him almost in two."

### 7. `orient-burns` — Fire seen in L'Orient (21:10)

- **Time.** Berry: "At ten minutes after nine, a fire was observed on board L'Orient, the French Admiral's Ship, which seemed to proceed from the after part of the cabin." Blanquet: "At a quarter past 9 o'clock, L'Orient caught fire in the cabin; it soon afterwards broke out upon the poop." Mahan "a little before nine"; Southey "soon after nine"; EB1911 21:30; WP 21:00. Blanquet also: "At 9 o'clock the Ships in the Van slackened their fire, and soon after totally ceased."
- **French centre.** Engaged, flagship burning; Mahan: "The ships to windward held on; those to leeward for the most part veered or slipped their cables." Blanquet: Franklin "only eighty fathoms to windward of L'Orient, (entirely on fire,)"; at 22:00 "the main and mizen-masts fell, and all the guns on the main deck were dismounted."
- **Everyone else.** As in phase 6; Nelson on deck ordering the boats away (Berry). The van has fallen silent: `broken`, strength 0.25 if Franklin alone is counted as still fighting.
- **Captions.** Berry (Nic p. 51): "This circumstance Captain Berry immediately communicated to the Admiral, who, though suffering severely from his wound, came up upon deck, where the first consideration that struck his mind was concern for the danger of so many lives, to save as many as possible of whom he ordered Captain Berry to make every practicable exertion. A boat, the only one that could swim, was instantly dispatched from the Vanguard." Southey: "Her sides had just been painted; and the oil-jars and paint buckets were lying on the poop. By the prodigious light of this conflagration, the situation of the two fleets could now be perceived, the colours of both being clearly distinguishable."

### 8. `orient-explodes` — L'Orient blows up; the pause (22:00)

- **Time.** Berry: "The cannonading was partially kept up to leeward of the Centre till about ten o'clock, when L'Orient blew up with a most tremendous explosion. An awful pause and death-like silence for about three minutes ensued." Howe: "At five minutes past ten, when L'Orient blew up, having burnt seventy minutes, the six Van ships had surrendered." Southey "About ten o'clock"; EB1911 22:00; WP 22:00; Mahan "At quarter before ten"; Blanquet "three-quarters past 10". One-hour spread; 22:00 is the majority and the British witness.
- **French van.** `destroyed` (Howe: the six van ships had surrendered). Strength 0.
- **French centre.** `broken`. Blanquet: "At a quarter past 10, the Tonnant cut her cable, to avoid the fire from L'Orient ... The Heureux and Mercure conceived that they ought likewise to cut their cables. This manoeuvre created so much confusion among the Rear Ships, that they fired into each other ... The Tonnant anchored ahead of the Guillaume Tell, Généreux, and Timoléon; the other two Ships got on shore." Mahan: Tonnant "a mile behind her former position". Move the marker a mile SE (31.325 N 30.145 E) and drop strength to 0.25 (Tonnant alone still fighting).
- **French rear.** Intact, but shifting: Blanquet, "The Guillaume Tell, Le Généreux, and Le Timoleon, shifted their berth, and anchored further down, out of gun-shot." Move the marker SE, about 31.325 N 30.150 E.
- **British.** All three units `engaged`, unchanged positions; Berry: "A port fire from L'Orient fell into the main royal of the Alexander, the fire occasioned by which was, however, extinguished in about two minutes." The pause is worth a slow `playback_rate` on this phase (three minutes of battle clock at 1:1 would be a strong beat).
- **Captions.** Berry (Nic p. 51-52): "An awful pause and death-like silence for about three minutes ensued, when the wreck of the masts, yards, &c. which had been carried to a vast height, fell down into the water, and on board the surrounding Ships." Southey ch. V: "This tremendous explosion was followed by a silence not less awful: the firing immediately ceased on both sides; and the first sound which broke the silence, was the dash of her shattered masts and yards, falling into the water from the vast height to which they had been exploded." Blanquet (Nic p. 70): "Commodore Casabianca and his son, only ten years old ... were in the water, upon the wreck of L'Orient's mast, (not being able to swim,) seeking each other, until three-quarters past 10, when the Ship blew up."

### 9. `night-action` — The fight moves down to the rear (23:00, running past midnight)

- **Time.** Berry: "After this awful scene, the firing was recommenced with the Ships to leeward of the Centre, till twenty minutes past ten, when there was a total cessation of firing for about ten minutes; after which it was revived till about three in the morning, when it again ceased." Blanquet: "Towards 11 o'clock, the Franklin ... recommenced the Action with a few of her lower-deck guns ... At half-past 11 o'clock ... Citizen Martinet, Captain of a Frigate, ordered the Colours to be struck. The Action in the Rear of the Fleet was very trifling, until three-quarters past 11 o'clock, when it became very warm. Three of the Enemy's Ships were engaging them ... About 3 o'clock in the morning, [Tonnant] was dismasted, and obliged to cut her cable a second time ... At half-past 3 o'clock the Action ceased throughout the Line." EB1911: "By midnight the battle was over" (wrong by the primary witnesses). WP: Tonnant a dismasted hulk by 03:00.
- **British.** Berry: "such British ships as were in a condition to move, had gone down upon the fresh Ships of the Enemy, which occasioned these renewals of the fight." Mahan quotes Captain Miller: "My people were so extremely jaded, that as soon as they had hove our sheet anchor up they dropped under the capstan bars, and were asleep in a moment." Move the inshore squadron and Nelson's division markers a few cables SE; states `engaged`.
- **French rear.** `engaged` from 23:45. **French centre.** Broken, Tonnant fighting alone until 03:00. **French van.** Destroyed.
- **Captions.** Berry as quoted. Mahan: "Messages went from ship to ship, captains consulted together and proposed to move together, and did move separately."
- **Schema.** This phase begins at 23:00 on 1 August and its interval ends at 05:05 on 2 August. Under v1 the next phase's `t` would be less than this one's; see implications.

### 10. `daybreak` — Only Guillaume Tell and Généreux have colours flying (2 August, 05:05)

- **Time.** Berry: "At five minutes past five in the morning, the two Rear ships of the Enemy, Le Guillaume Tell and Le Généreux, were the only French ships of the Line that had their colours flying. At fifty-four minutes past five, a French frigate, L'Artemise, fired a broadside and struck her colours; but ... he set fire to his Ship." Blanquet: "At 6 o'clock, two English Ships joined those which had been engaging the Rear, and began firing on the Heureux and Mercure, which were aground. The former soon struck, and the latter followed ... At a quarter past 7, the Ship's crew of the Artemise Frigate quitted her, and set her on fire. At 8 o'clock she blew up." WP: "As the sun rose at 04:00" (an hour before civil sunrise for the latitude; Berry's 05:05 is preferred). Culloden afloat at 02:00 (WP citing James): Berry, "It was not till the morning of the second, that the Culloden could be got off ... her rudder was beat off."
- **British.** All `engaged` (cohesion held, firing over); Berry: "The Audacious was in the morning detached to her assistance [Bellerophon's]." Reserve strength back to 1 if the author counts Culloden refloated, or left at 0.75 since she never fought.
- **French centre.** `destroyed`: Heureux and Mercure taken aground, Tonnant a dismasted hulk that struck on the 3rd. **French rear.** Engaged, out of gunshot to the SE, colours flying. **Frigates.** Artémise burnt; Sérieuse sunk; Diane and Justice with the rear.
- **Captions.** Berry (Nic p. 52): "At five minutes past five in the morning, the two Rear ships of the Enemy, Le Guillaume Tell and Le Généreux, were the only French ships of the Line that had their colours flying." Blanquet (Nic p. 70): "The Enemy, without doubt, had received great damage in their masts and yards, as they did not get under weigh to attack the remains of the French Fleet."

### 11. `villeneuve-escapes` — Villeneuve cuts and stands out to sea; Zealous pursues (2 August, 11:00)

- **Time.** Berry: "At eleven o'clock, Le Généreux and Guillaume Tell, with the two Frigates, La Justice and La Diane, cut their cables and stood out to sea, pursued by the Zealous, Captain Hood ... but as there was no other Ship in a condition to support the Zealous, she was recalled." Blanquet: "at three-quarters past 11, Le Guillaume Tell, Le Généreux, La Diane, and La Justice, were under weigh, and formed in Line of Battle ... At noon, the Timoleon, which probably was not in a state to put to sea, steered right for the shore, under her foresail, and as soon as she struck the ground, her foremast fell." Mahan "At about noon"; Southey "in the forenoon"; WP 11:00 at the mouth of the bay.
- **French rear.** `broken`, strength 0.67 (Timoléon ashore), moving NE out of the bay: position at `t` 31.34 N 30.16 E heading 45, with a `detachment` arrow to sea (31.50 N 30.30 E) for the ships that got away; the marker itself can tween toward the bay mouth. Blanquet: "The French Division joined the Enemy's Ships, which ranged along their Line, on opposite tacks, within pistol-shot, and received their broadsides ... The Division was in sight at Sunset."
- **Inshore squadron.** Engaged; a `detachment` arrow for Zealous toward the escaping ships, recalled. Blanquet: "an English Ship having got under weigh, and making short tacks, to prevent her getting away".
- **Nelson's division, reserve.** Engaged, at anchor among the prizes. **French van, centre.** Destroyed.
- **Captions.** Gaz p. 915-916: "The Ships of the Enemy, all but their Two Rear Ships, are nearly dismasted; and those Two, with Two Frigates, I am sorry to say, made their Escape; nor was it, I assure you, in my Power to prevent them. Captain Hood most handsomely endeavoured to do it, but I had no Ship in a Condition to support the Zealous, and I was obliged to call her in." Southey: "'Victory,' said Nelson, 'is not a name strong enough for such a scene:' he called it a conquest."
- **End.** Berry: "on the morning of the second, he issued the following Memorandum ... At two o'clock accordingly on that day, public service was performed on the quarter-deck of the Vanguard." `end` = 2 August 14:00. The 3 August coda (Berry: "On the morning of the third, the Timoleon was set fire to, and Le Tonnant had cut her cable and drifted on shore"; Blanquet: Timoléon quitted "at noon the next day") is caption matter, or a twelfth phase if the file carries a `day` of 2.

## Implications for `nile.json` and for the schema

1. **Midnight is the blocker.** `t` is `"HH:MM"` on one `date` and must strictly increase; `end` must be later than the last `t`. Phases 10 and 11 (05:05 and 11:00 on 2 August) violate both. ADR-0002 already says "Multi-day battles are not v1." Options, in order of preference:
   - Add an optional per-phase `day` (integer, default 0, the offset from `date`), and allow `end` to be `{ "day": 1, "t": "14:00" }` or a parallel `end_day`. Ordering becomes (`day`, `t`). Additive, keeps every v1 file valid, and the caption band's date can advance. The renderer's clock readout needs the day too.
   - Allow `t` past `24:00` (`"29:05"` for 05:05 next day). Fewer fields, but the readout must normalise it and every log quote has to be converted by hand; ships' logs of the period already run a nautical day that starts at noon, which is confusing enough.
   - Split into two files, `nile-evening.json` and `nile-morning.json`. Works today with no schema change, at the cost of the pause across midnight being a page load.
   The first is recommended; the ticket answer for "a date per phase, or a clock past 24:00" is **a day offset per phase**.
2. **Anchoring.** Every French unit is stationary for the entire battle and every British unit for most of it. The schema expresses this fine (same `position` two phases running, `heading` held); what it cannot say is *that* a unit is anchored, so the renderer draws no anchor glyph and a viewer cannot tell "at anchor" from "becalmed". Caption matter in v1. A `formation` value `anchored` would be the smallest addition if wanted later.
3. **Shoals and the island battery.** The map file has only `land` and `place`. The shoal that runs NE from Aboukir Island, on which Culloden grounded and which forced the whole approach, cannot be drawn; nor can the island's mortar battery. `place` points can label "Aboukir Island" and "Aboukir Castle"; the shoal is caption matter. A `shoal` feature kind (polygon, hatched) is the natural v2 addition and is the same kind of thing as the land-battle `ground` field the schema doc reserves.
4. **Inshore versus outside.** At squadron granularity, two British units sit on either side of the French van, 300 yards apart. The renderer's minimum glyph size may make them overlap at the extent that shows the whole approach (9 nmi out). A tight extent (north 31.48, south 31.26, west 29.85, east 30.30, landscape) keeps the sighting position inside while giving the line about a third of the width.
5. **Strength and struck ships.** French van `destroyed` at the explosion, centre `destroyed` at daybreak, rear `broken` at the escape with strength 0.67; British reserve 0.75 for Culloden. All expressible.
6. **Frigates, gunboats, the brigs.** Four frigates, "numerous Gun-boats", two bombs and the decoy brigs are caption matter unless the seventh unit is used.
7. **The pause.** Three minutes of silence after the explosion is a `playback_rate` choice on phase 8 (for example 60 seconds per second for the whole phase, so the pause lasts three real seconds). No schema change.
8. **Wind.** NNW, `fresh` in the afternoon, `moderate` after dark; no shift is recorded, so every phase can carry the same value.

## Open follow-ups

- No transcription of Nelson's Nile dispatch or of Berry's narrative exists on Wikisource; the project would have to type them from Nicolas or the Gazette PDF if it wants to ship the text. The IA djvu text of Nicolas vol. III is usable but OCR-noisy (long s, italic passages).
- William James, *Naval History of Great Britain* vol. II (1822-4), and Clowes, *The Royal Navy* vol. IV (1899), are public domain and are what Wikipedia's minute-level times come from; neither was fetched here. They would let the file cite 18:20, 18:40, 20:20 and 03:00 to a PD source instead of Wikipedia.
- The Pocock chart (RMG PY0143) was identified but not viewed at resolution; the derived French-line coordinates above should be checked against it before authoring.
- The Commons category was enumerated and the licence templates of eleven files read through the API; the remaining files and the Pocock chart image were not opened. A pass with the images in view (Pocock, Dodd, Vivares) would settle the French line's exact placement relative to the island.
