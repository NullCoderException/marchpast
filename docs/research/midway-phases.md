# Midway phase list at force, carrier and strike granularity, a fourth arm for aircraft, and public-domain sources

*Research note for GitHub issue #124 (part of the wayfinder map, #121). Written 2026-09-07. Every locator below was fetched during this session; nothing is from memory. Where a fetch failed it is said so. A first draft of the phase list was extracted by a model from the 1943 combat narrative; how that fared is reported in its own section.*

## Question

What is the phase list for the Battle of Midway (4 to 7 June 1942) at the level a plate can draw, from the dawn launches on the 4th through the loss of four carriers and Hiryu's two strikes on Yorktown, the night withdrawal and the pursuit on the 5th and 6th; which clock the file runs on and where the day offsets (ADR-0013) fall; which units it needs at each of three levels and whether air strikes as units bust the sixteen-unit ceiling (ADR-0017); what each phase holds for fleets hundreds of miles apart; what aircraft need as a fourth arm; what a map file for open ocean holds; which public-domain sources support it, and which histories are unusable; and what schema v2 cannot express.

## Short answer

**Twenty-one candidate phases over four days** (day `0` to `3`), fifteen if merged as noted below. **The clock is Midway local, Zone plus 12** (twelve hours behind Greenwich), the convention of CINCPAC's report and of the 1943 combat narrative. Two other clocks appear in the primary sources and both must be converted on the way into the file: the carrier and task-force action reports keep **Zone plus 10** (Hawaii), two hours ahead of Midway; the Nagumo report keeps **Tokyo time, plus 9**, twenty-one hours ahead of Midway, so its "0130 on the 5th" is 04:30 on the 4th. The battle file therefore breaks the rule that every time is copied verbatim from the source that gives it; the verbatim reading goes into `references[].quote`, the converted one into `t`.

`end` = 7 June 05:30, `end_day` = 3, `dates` = `["4 June 1942", "5 June 1942", "6 June 1942", "7 June 1942"]`. The three-day prelude (the Occupation Force sighted 700 miles west on 3 June, the B-17 and PBY torpedo attacks on the transports) is caption matter for phase 1 unless the file wants 3 June as day `0`, which pushes every other day up by one and adds a fifth date.

If fifteen phases are preferred, merge 2 into 1, 5 into 4, 6 into 7, 11 into 10, 16 into 15 and 18 into 19. Phases 3, 4, 7, 8, 12, 13, 14, 15, 19, 20 and 21 must stay separate: they are where units change state.

**Units.** Five at force level (Mobile Force, TF 16, TF 17, Midway, CruDiv 7), seven with Yamamoto's Main Body and Kondo's Occupation Force, which never come within the extent of a 4 June plate. Nine at carrier level (four Japanese carriers, Enterprise, Hornet, Yorktown, Midway's air group, CruDiv 7 undivided), twelve at most. At strike level **a curated ten strikes plus the unsplit units come to fourteen or fifteen; the full set of sixteen strikes the sources describe comes to twenty-one and busts the ceiling**, because every strike is drawn in every phase whether or not it is in the air. The counts are worked in the roster section.

**A fourth arm.** A strike is a unit with `arm: "aircraft"`, `formation` `mass` (the Japanese flew "three divisional 'V''s of 9"), `heading` its course while flying and the direction of its attack run over the target, `strength` the fraction of its aircraft still with it (VT-8 goes from 1 to 0 in one phase), `state` `intact` until it attacks, `engaged` from the attack, `broken` when it scatters, `destroyed` when nothing comes home. Its position before launch and after recovery is its carrier's, copied coordinate for coordinate; in between it tweens along the flight, which the renderer's track already draws. What v2 cannot say is that a strike is **embarked**: before launch and after recovery the glyph sits on its carrier's glyph, and because a carrier with strike children is not drawn at the strikes level (ADR-0017's depth rule), the strike then sits on nothing at all. That, and the antimeridian, are the two decisions this note leaves to the map.

**Map.** Nagumo launched from about 30.9 N on the 180th meridian; Kaga and Hiryu sank at 179.3 W; TF 16 crossed 180 on the evening of 5 June and fought the 6th at 174 to 177 E; Mikuma sank at 173.5 E. **A 4 June plate fits in `west: -180, east: -173`; the whole battle does not fit any extent v2 can write**, because `west < east` on `-180 <= lon <= 180` cannot cross the date line. The open-ocean map holds Sand and Eastern Islands (`land`, two rings from Natural Earth 10m, which carries the atoll), the reef (`shoal`, from Natural Earth's reefs layer, three features), Kure Atoll 55 miles west-north-west (also in Natural Earth, inside any extent), a `place` for each atoll and a `work` for the air station on Eastern Island, and nothing else. Licence `public-domain`.

**Sources.** Everything needed is a United States government work and public domain: CINCPAC's report (serial 01849, 28 June 1942), the 1943 ONI combat narrative, the Enterprise, Hornet, Yorktown and Task Force 16 action reports, the 1947 ONI translation of Nagumo's report, and five USSBS interrogations (Amagai, Kawaguchi, Aoki, Watanabe, Ohara). Morison (1949), Fuchida and Okumiya (1955) and Lord (1967) are all in copyright and unusable for quotation; so are Prange, Lundstrom, Parshall and Tully, and Symonds.

## The clock

Three clocks run through the primary sources, and each source states its own.

| Clock | Who keeps it | Statement | Offset from Midway |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Zone plus 12** (Midway local) | CINCPAC's report; the 1943 combat narrative; NAS Midway | CINCPAC para. 13: "All times in this rept are Zone plus 12. In studying Task Force 16 and 17 reports it must be kept in mind that times given by them are Zone plus 10." CN ch. 4 fn. 10: "All times given in this narrative are zone plus 12." | none; **the battle clock** |
| **Zone plus 10** (Hawaii) | Task Force 16 and 17 reports; Enterprise, Hornet and Yorktown action reports | TF 16 report para. 3: "All times given are zone plus ten, which is two hours ahead of Midway time, zone plus twelve." Hornet para. 5: "At 090 (all times given hereafter are zone plus 10) commenced launching". Enterprise para. 1: "(times throughout are Zone plus 10)". | subtract two hours |
| **Tokyo, plus 9** | The Nagumo report and every Japanese message in it; the USSBS interrogators ask in "5 June Tokyo time" | NAG translator's note 4: "These Japanese dates of course would be reckoned as 31 May and 1 June in U.S. reports. It should be noted that all dates in this report and all times are in Tokyo time, Plus 9." | subtract twenty-one hours: add three hours and take the date back one day |

One event on all three: the dive-bombing of the carriers is "between 1020 and 1025" (CINCPAC para. 36), "1222" (Enterprise, Zone plus 10) and "0722" for Kaga, "0726" for Akagi, "0728" for Soryu on 5 June (NAG pp. 9-10, Tokyo). Yorktown's sinking is "0501, 7 June" (CINCPAC para. 65) and "0701" (Yorktown report). The narrative itself slips once: its account of Hornet's last attack on 6 June says "At 1645" in the text and "1445" in the chapter timeline; Hornet's report has "At 1645" in Zone plus 10, so the text carries an unconverted time and the timeline is right.

The Japanese clock also runs about a quarter of an hour behind the American on the same events where both are first-hand: Midway is bombed at "0630" (CN ch. 5) or "about 0633" (CINCPAC para. 20) but "between about 0345 and 0410" Tokyo, which is 06:45 to 07:10 (NAG p. 7); VT-8 attacks "at about 0920" (CINCPAC para. 31) but Akagi sights "14 enemy torpedo planes" at 0640 Tokyo, which is 09:40 (NAG p. 18). The disagreement table lists these. The file should take the Zone plus 12 reading where an American witness has one and the converted Tokyo reading where only the Japanese side saw the event (the launches, the sinkings, the scuttlings).

**Days.** Day `0` is 4 June, 04:30 to the evening sinkings; day `1` is 5 June from Yamamoto's cancellation at 02:55; day `2` is 6 June; day `3` is 7 June for Yorktown's sinking alone. The Nagumo report's 6 June entries are 5 June on the battle clock, so its "0200, 6th" scuttling of Akagi is `{ "day": 1, "t": "05:00" }`.

## Fixed reference points

Midway is at 28.21 N, 177.37 W (Wikipedia "Midway Atoll", rev. 1370264178; the Natural Earth land rings run 28.198 to 28.215 N, 177.32 to 177.39 W). One minute of latitude is a nautical mile; at 30 N a minute of longitude is 0.866 of one, so a degree of longitude is 52 miles. "Miles" in every source below are nautical.

- **Nagumo's launch position, 04:30**: not given as a lat/lon anywhere. Derived two ways. From the PBY's 05:52 fix, "bearing 320, distance 180, course 135, speed 25" (CINCPAC para. 18; ENT "0803" intercept), the force at 05:52 was at 30.5 N 179.6 W and 82 minutes earlier, on the 135-degree course NAG p. 12 prescribes after the launch, at about **30.9 N 180.0 W**. From the other end, CruDiv 8 at 0630 Tokyo (09:30) was "in position bearing 137 degrees distance 96 miles from take-off point" (NAG p. 19), and the force was bombed at 10:22 at "Lat. 30° 05' N, Long. 178° 50' W" (ENT) after turning north at 24 knots; back-plotting gives 30.9 N 180.2 W. **The Japanese carriers launched within a few miles of the 180th meridian**, about 215 miles from Midway. The B-17s found them at 08:14 "on bearing 320° about 145 miles from Midway" (CINCPAC para. 25). To be checked against CINCPAC enclosure (A) when that chart is seen.
- **The carriers when bombed, 10:22**: "Position of enemy force, Lat. 30° 05' N, Long. 178° 50' W" (ENT). Ships' course "270°" turning to "000°" and then "180°" as the American planes appeared (CN ch. 7); Akagi "turned to course 300 degrees" at 0706 Tokyo (NAG p. 8).
- **Sinkings and scuttlings (NAG pp. 9-10, Tokyo times)**: Soryu "sank at 1613" (19:13), "Position: 30-42.5N, 178-37.5W". Kaga sank at 1625 (19:25), "Position of Kaga's sinking: 30-20.3N, 179-17.2W". Akagi "was scuttled at 0200, in position 30-30N, 178-40W" on the 6th (05:00, 5 June). Hiryu: "At 0210 the Hiryu was scuttled by torpedo (1) from the Makigumo. Position of Hiryu's scuttling: 31-27.5N, 179-23.5W" (05:10, 5 June). Wikipedia's ship articles agree to the minute for Akagi, Kaga and Soryu; Hiryu's article carries no coordinates (revs 1370523916, 1366320513, 1363787496, 1370065445).
- **Hiryu when found, 14:30**: "one of the Yorktown's scouts contacted the Hiryu with 2 BB, 3 CA and 4 DD in 31°-15' N, 179°-05' W, course north, speed 20" (CINCPAC para. 45; YKT "about 1630", Zone plus 10, "latitude 31-15 N.. longitude 179-05 W"). Hit at 17:05 "position Lat. 31° - 40' N, Long. 179° - 10' W" (ENT "1905").
- **TF 16 and TF 17 on the morning of 4 June**: TF 17's designated 0630 (Zone plus 10, so 04:30) rendezvous was "31° 30' N; 176° 30' W" (ENT para. 1); Enterprise at 2000 on 3 June (18:00) was at "33° 16' N, 175° 46' W, course 100° T, speed 15 knots" (ENT para. 2). The two forces had met at "1530 on June 2d at latitude 32°04' N., longitude 172°45' W., about 350 miles northeast of Midway" (CN ch. 7), the vicinity the reports call "Point Luck" (ENT para. 1; HOR para. 2). At the 07:00 launch the carriers were "about 155 miles distant on bearing 239° from the task force" (CN ch. 7; HOR para. 5), which puts TF 16 at **31.5 N 176.6 W**; Tone's No. 4 plane put "10 (ships)" at "bearing 10 degrees, distance 240 miles from Midway, on course 150 degrees, speed 20 knots (0428)" (NAG p. 7), which is 32.2 N 176.6 W and forty miles too far north, a known error in that report. TF 17 "was about 10 miles to the N.E. of us" (TF16 para. 4a).
- **Yorktown**: after the bombing "Her position then was Latitude 33-51 N, Longitude 176 W, course 090°" at 14:02 (CINCPAC para. 42), which cannot be right for a ship that sank three days later at "30-36 N, 176-34 W" (para. 65) after a two-knot tow east and is a misprint for 30-51 or 31-51; Chikuma's plane at 1413 Tokyo (17:13) put her "listing and stopped in position 30-15N, l76-50W" (NAG p. 8). Authoring estimate for the afternoon of 4 June: **30.6 N 176.9 W**. Sank "at 0501, 7 June, in about 30-36 N, 176-34 W" (CINCPAC para. 65; CN ch. 13 prints "lat. 30°46' N., long. 167°24' W.", a misprint; Wikipedia rev. 1371062259 has 30.5997 N 176.5678 W).
- **TF 16 on the afternoon of 4 June**: Hornet's 16:03 strike went out "278° T., distant 162 miles" to the Hiryu group (HOR para. 10), which puts TF 16 near 30.9 N 176.0 W; Chikuma's plane at 1510 Tokyo (18:10) saw "2 enemy carriers (Yorktown or Hornet class) ... in position 30-23N, 176-05W ... course 270 degrees; speed 12 knots" (NAG p. 8). Use **30.5 N 176.2 W** at 17:05.
- **The night of 4-5 June and the pursuit**: Enterprise: "During the night June 4 - 5 distance from Midway of approximately 150 miles was maintained by steaming east and north and then retracing the track." Hornet: "the task force retired to the eastward until 0200, 5 June [00:00], when course was changed to 000° T., and at 0348 [01:48] to 270° T." CN ch. 11: at 04:20 course 230 "at 25 knots", at 06:00 "approximately 130 miles northeast of Midway", "At 1100 course was changed to 300° ... this course was maintained till sunset at 1900. Then, shortly after the 180th meridian was crossed, course was altered to the westward." Enterprise launched at 15:00 against an "objective given as at 1000, Lat. 32° - 00' N, Long. 179° - 32' W, course 310, speed 12 knots. (Approximate distance estimated to be 230 miles.)" and at 18:30 "Attacked 1 CL position Lat. 33° - 00' N, Long. 177° - 00' E" (ENT "1700", "2030"). So TF 16 at 15:00 was about **30.1 N 175.7 W** and the "1 CL" was Tanikaze: "From about 1500, 6th, the Tanikaze was subjected to attacks from about 50 enemy planes. She fought well without any support from any other units, and managed to down 4 of the enemy planes" (NAG p. 11).
- **The Japanese retreat**: Nagumo at 1830 Tokyo (21:30, 4 June): "While offering protection to the Hiryu we are retiring northwestward. Speed 18 knots" (NAG p. 11). Yamamoto at 0515 Tokyo (08:15, 5 June): "My position is 33-10N, 177-35E; course 310 degrees, speed 14 knots" (NAG p. 38). Chikuma "joins the Main Unit and the Occupation Force ... (33-0N, 176-55.0E.)" at 1000 Tokyo (13:00, 5 June) (NAG p. 39). The refuelling rendezvous "during the morning of 7 June in position 33N, 170 E" (Tokyo; 6 June on the battle clock) (NAG p. 38). Chikuma at 1410 on the 6th Tokyo (17:10, 5 June) is printed as "(26-31.0N, 163-21.0E.)", which is impossible and is a misprint.
- **CruDiv 7**: Tambor's night report, "many ships 90 miles west of Midway" (CINCPAC para. 48; CN ch. 11 "0215"); at 06:00 5 June "2 Mogami type cruisers bearing 272°, 115 miles from Midway, course 270°" (CN ch. 11), 28.1 N 179.5 W; bombed at 08:08 (CINCPAC para. 50). On 6 June "The second at 0645, bearing about 280°, distance 435 miles from Midway ... appears to have been the Mikuma and Mogami with 3 or 4 DD on course west, speed 10" (CINCPAC para. 55); ENT "8-B-2 ... position Lat. 29° - 33' N, Long. 174° - 50' E, course 270°" and "2 CA, 2 DD, Lat. 28° - 55' N, Long. 175° - 10' E, course 215°, speed 15" (CN ch. 12 prints the first as 174°30' E). Hornet's 09:50 attack was "142 miles bearing 235° T. from Hornet 1015 position" (HOR para. 14), which puts TF 16 at **30.3 N 177.4 E** on the morning of the 6th. Mikuma sank at 29.33 N 173.5 E (Wikipedia rev. 1369174497; no primary source here gives the position).
- **Wind**: Enterprise, 3 June 18:00: "Wind south 9"; 5 June: "Wind SE 9"; 6 June: "Wind SW 16" (ENT). 4 June: "the wind was light from the southeast" (CN ch. 7); "the wind was light (about 4 knots) and directly away from the enemy" (HOR para. 5). Written `{ "from": 135, "force": "light" }` for the 4th and 5th and `{ "from": 225, "force": "moderate" }` for the 6th. The wind matters here as it did not at the Nile: every launch and recovery turned the American carriers east, away from the enemy.
- **Strengths**: Tomonaga's strike "36 ship-based fighters, 36 ship-based bombers, and 36 ship-based torpedo planes" (NAG p. 7), 108. Kaga's air group "21 fighters (0) Type: 27 VB (99 Type); 18 VT (97 Type); same as all other carriers" (USSBS 1). Hornet "27 VF, 35 VSB, AND 15 VTB" (HOR para. 1). Launched on the 4th: Hornet "35 VSB, 15 VTB, 10 VF", Enterprise "35 VSB, 14 VTB, 10 VF" (CINCPAC para. 27; ENT says 33 VSB), Yorktown "17 VSB 12 VT 6 VF" (para. 34). Midway: "27 fighter planes available", "6 Navy TBF and 4 Army B-26 armed with torpedoes, and 27 Marine dive bombers", "The B-17 unit of 16 planes" (paras. 19, 25). Hiryu's strikes: "6 fighters and 18 bombers" and "4 fighters and 9 torpedo planes, supplemented by 2 fighters from the Kaga and 1 torpedo plane from the Akagi" (NAG p. 8); afterwards "At 1240, she only had 6 fighters, 5 bombers, and 4 torpedo planes left" (p. 8). Losses: VT-8 "Of the 15 planes, not one returned" (CN ch. 7); "Enterprise losing 10 out of 14 planes and Yorktown 10 out of 12" torpedo planes (CINCPAC para. 33); "Of the 33 SBD's, 18 failed to return" (CN ch. 7); Midway fighters "Of the 27 fighters available, 15 were lost and 7 severely damaged" (para. 19); Henderson's SBDs "His and 7 other planes were shot down" (para. 23).

**Counting the strikes level.** A level draws every unit at its depth plus every shallower unit with no children. With the ten core strikes: 10 strikes; Akagi, Kaga and Soryu, which have no strike children, 3; `crudiv-7` undivided, 1; total **14**. With `main-body` and `occupation-force` as roots, 16, exactly at the ceiling; split CruDiv 7 as well and it is 17, an error. With all sixteen strikes and the minimal set of forces: 16 + 3 + 1 = **20**, an error however the forces are cut. The count is the same in every phase: a strike that has not launched or has been recovered is a roster unit with a snapshot and a glyph like any other. So **strikes bust the ceiling unless curated to about ten**, and the curated set needs the two distant Japanese forces left off the roster.

Twelve is the guideline for labels; fourteen means short labels on the plate at the strikes level, and `short_label` on every strike ("VT-8", "Tomonaga", "Kobayashi", "VMSB-241").

**What the strikes level hides.** By ADR-0017's rule, a carrier with strike children is not drawn at the strikes level. At that level Hiryu, Hornet, Enterprise and Yorktown vanish and their strikes are drawn instead; Akagi, Kaga and Soryu, with no children, stay. A viewer at the strikes level watches VT-8 fly from an empty sea toward Akagi and the Hiryu strike fly from nowhere toward nothing. The workaround inside v2 is a child unit per carrier standing for the ship herself (`hiryu-ship` under `hiryu`), which doubles the carriers on the roster and pushes the count to 18. The alternative is a schema change; see the implications section.

**A two-level file** (Forces, Carriers) with every strike as an arrow is entirely writable in v2 today: `intent` from a carrier to its target at launch (Hornet toward "155 miles ... bearing 239°"), `detachment` for what left and did not return (VT-8 toward Akagi; Hornet's thirteen toward Midway), the carriers' own `state` going `engaged` when their strike attacks. The plate then never shows a strike in the air, which is most of what makes Midway Midway, but nothing about it is a schema question.

## The phases

Times are battle clock (Zone plus 12) on 4 June unless the day is given. Positions are from the reference points above and are estimates where marked. Headings: a carrier force's front is its course while it runs and the direction of its flight operations while it launches and recovers; a strike's is its course and, at the target, its attack run. Formation: the Japanese cruised in a box, "A square formation about 4000 meters apart" and "In daytime a circular formation was used, but at night a column was formed" (USSBS 1), so `mass` by day; the American task forces in their circular screens, "disposition 'Victor'", "Radius of the screen was one mile" (CN ch. 8), `mass` as well; a strike `mass`.

### 1. `dawn-launch` — Nagumo launches 108 aircraft against Midway (04:30)

- **Time.** NAG p. 7: "At 0130 on the 5th, under command of flight officer of the Hiryu, Lieut. Tomonaga, Organization #5 composed of 36 ship-based fighters, 36 ship-based bombers, and 36 ship-based torpedo planes, took off to attack Midway." Search planes "Between 0130 and 0200"; Tone's No. 4 plane, the one that mattered, at 0200 (NAG p. 40: "the take offs of the float recco planes were delayed as shown below ... #4 0200 Tone's #4 plane; sighted enemy"). Midway: "Before dawn on 4 June, PBY's took off from Midway" (CINCPAC para. 18); "0415 Search planes take off" (CN ch. 5).
- **Mobile Force.** About 30.9 N 180.0 W (derived), heading 135, `mass`, `intact`. NAG p. 13, Mobile Force SigOrd 100: "For three hours and 30 minutes following the first wave's take-off, the fleet will proceed on course 135 degrees, speed, 24 knots." `midway-strike` at the same position, heading 139 for Midway, `intact`.
- **TF 16, TF 17.** At the 04:30 rendezvous "31° 30' N; 176° 30' W" (ENT), heading 210 then 225, `intact`; TF 17 ten miles to the north-east. Wind SE, light.
- **Midway.** 28.21 N 177.37 W, `intact`. CruDiv 7 and the Occupation Force 400 to 500 miles west (CINCPAC para. 16: "Bearing was still about 261° from Midway, distance reported about 500 miles") are off the plate; caption.
- **Captions.** NAG p. 7 as quoted. CINCPAC para. 17: "The Japanese Main Striking Force assumed to have 4 carriers was not sighted on the third. These ships were apparently riding a weather front bearing down on Midway from the northwest."

### 2. `carriers-sighted` — A PBY reports two carriers bearing 320, 180 miles (05:52)

- **Time.** CINCPAC para. 18: "At 0545 the most important contact of the battle was made. A PBY reported many planes heading for Midway 150 miles distant on bearing 320; 7 minutes later another PBY sighted 2 of the enemy carriers and many other ships on the same bearing, distant 180 miles, coming in at 25 knots on course 135." ENT para. 3 has the intercepts at "0734", "0753", "0803" (Zone plus 10). TF16 para. 4a: "We received our first contact report at 0740 [05:40] ... Task Force SIXTEEN headed toward the contact at 24 knots."
- **Mobile Force.** 30.5 N 179.6 W, heading 135, `intact`; NAG p. 13: "0242 Sight one enemy flying boat in position bearing 166 degrees, distance, 40 kilometers. 0243 Fighters take off from all ships." The strike two-thirds of the way to Midway.
- **TF 16.** Heading 239 at 24 knots; `intact`. Fletcher at 06:07: "proceed southwesterly and attack enemy carriers when definitely located" (ENT para. 3).
- **Midway.** CINCPAC para. 19: "All serviceable planes at Midway were in the air before 0600 (except for 3 SB2U spares)". CN ch. 5: "At 0555 the air-raid alarm was sounded, and by 0600 or shortly after, every plane able to leave the ground ... was in the air."

### 3. `midway-bombed` — Marine fighters intercept; the first bomb falls (06:30)

- **Time.** CINCPAC para. 19: "at 0615, 14 of the 27 fighter planes available made contact 30 miles distant with 60 to 80 dive bombers ... and about 50 fighters"; para. 20: "The first bomb hit Midway at about 0633 from horizontal bombers. Dive bombing and strafing continued for about 17 minutes." CN ch. 5: "The first bomb fell on Midway at 0630." NAG p. 7: "bombs were dropped on military installations on Midway between about 0345 and 0410" (06:45 to 07:10).
- **Midway.** `engaged`, strength 0.5 for the fighters (CINCPAC para. 19: "Of the 27 fighters available, 15 were lost and 7 severely damaged"); the bombers and torpedo planes are already away. `midway-strike` over the atoll, heading 139, `engaged`, strength 0.94 ("All but 2 ship-based fighters, 1 ship-based bomber and 3 ship-based torpedo planes returned", NAG p. 7).
- **Mobile Force.** Heading 135, `intact`, about 30.3 N 179.3 W. NAG p. 7: "From about 0230, two to three enemy flying boats maintained continuous contact with us."
- **Captions.** CINCPAC para. 20: "Considerable damage was done to nearly all structures above ground, the most serious at the time being the destruction of the power plant on Eastern Island. Little damage was done to the runways, the Japanese apparently leaving these intact for their own anticipated use." NAG p. 40: "They also concentrated about 50 fighters (all Grummans), and intercepted our first attack wave at a point approximately 30 miles short of our target."

### 4. `tf16-launches` — Enterprise and Hornet launch; Tomonaga asks for a second attack; Tone's plane sights ten ships (07:00)

- **Time.** CINCPAC para. 27: "At about 0700 launching commenced". HOR para. 5: "At 090 (all times given hereafter are zone plus 10) commenced launching the Air Group for attack"; ENT: "0906 Commenced launching attack group of 33 VSB, 14 VT, 10 VF." TF16 para. 4a: "about 0900, we turned south into the wind and launched attack groups ... Launching time was about one hour. Carriers then headed for contact at 25 knots." NAG p. 7: "at 0415, the command plane of the Hiryu radioed that: 'There is a necessity for carrying out a second attack (0400).'" (07:00) and "Orders were issued for the ship-based attack planes to remove their torpedoes and replace them with #80 land bombs." Tone's No. 4: "Sighted what appears to be the enemy composed of 10 (ships), bearing 10 degrees, distance 240 miles from Midway, on course 150 degrees, speed 20 knots (0428)" (07:28). The B-26s and TBFs: CINCPAC para. 21, "The B-26's found their targets, 2 CV, about 0710 and made a most gallant attack"; NAG p. 7, "At about 0400 the first enemy wave attacked."
- **Mobile Force.** About 30.2 N 179.2 W, heading 135, `engaged` from 07:10 (under attack, firing). `midway-strike` returning, heading 315.
- **TF 16.** 31.5 N 176.6 W, heading 180 while launching (the light wind was from the south-east), `intact`; `vt-8`, `enterprise-strike` (and `hornet-group`) at the carriers' position, `intact`, then heading 239. TF 17 holding its group "temporarily ... in reserve until her scouts returned" (CINCPAC para. 27).
- **Midway.** `midway-torpedo` at the Mobile Force, `engaged` then `broken`, strength 0.3: "2 of the 4 planes did not return" and of the TBFs "Only one badly shot up plane returned" (CINCPAC paras. 21-22).
- **Captions.** CINCPAC para. 21: "This is likewise another historical event, and, it is hoped, one soon to be repeated under better conditions - our Army's first attack with torpedo planes." USSBS 2 (Kawaguchi): "About an hour after sunrise, we were attacked by torpedo bombers ... They were dropped at very great range and we were able to avoid them."

### 5. `midways-strikes` — Marine dive bombers, B-17s and Vindicators attack; the carrier reported at 08:20; Yorktown launches (07:55)

- **Time.** CINCPAC para. 23: "At 0755 a group of 16 Marine dive bombers, under Major L. R. Henderson, USMC made a gallant glide bombing attack on one of the carriers"; para. 25: "at 0814 began attacking from 20,000 feet"; para. 24: "at about 0820, the 11 SB2U Marine bombers from Midway made a glide bombing attack on a battleship". NAG p. 7: "At 0530, Tone's plane reported: 'The enemy is accompanied by what appears to be a carrier in a position to the rear of the others (0520).'" (08:20). CINCPAC para. 34: "At 0830 Yorktown commenced launching"; CN ch. 7 "0838"; YKT "From 1030 to 1050 took off an attack group composed of seventeen VSB and VB3, twelve VT from VT3, and six VF from VF3." Task Force 16's radar "At 0815 ... had picked up a twin float seaplane, 36 miles to the south" (para. 39).
- **Mobile Force.** About 30.0 N 179.0 W, `engaged`, heading swinging under attack (NAG p. 18, "0628 Course, 115 degrees"; "0638 Course, 300 degrees"). Decision: "After taking on the returning planes, we shall proceed north to contact and destroy the enemy task force" (0605 Tokyo, 09:05).
- **Midway.** `vmsb-241` at the target, `engaged`, strength 0.5 ("His and 7 other planes were shot down"); `b-17s` `engaged`, no loss.
- **TF 17.** `yorktown-strike` launching, `intact`.
- **Captions.** CINCPAC para. 26: "The Midway Forces had struck with full strength, but the Japanese were not as yet checked ... Most of Midway's fighters, torpedo planes and dive-bombers -- the only types capable of making a high percentage of hits on ships -- were gone, and 3 of the Japanese carriers were still either undamaged or insufficiently so to hamper operations." USSBS 1 (Amagai): "I think first high horizontal bombers, no hits. Then torpedo attack. Was dodged, no hits. Then dove bombers, 4 hits."

### 6. `nagumo-recovers` — The Midway strike lands; course 70 to "destroy the enemy in a daylight engagement" (09:00)

- **Time.** NAG p. 7: the strike "returned to their carriers by about 0600" (09:00); p. 18: "0630 Blinker from ComMobile Force to Mobile Force (received 0910): 'Battleships in 10-8-3 order. Course 70, speed 12.' 0630 ... 'Plan to destroy the enemy in a daylight engagement.'" and p. 19: "At 0630, we are in position bearing 137 degrees distance 96 miles from take-off point. We are on a northerly course, speed 24 knots." CINCPAC para. 29: "Sometime after 0830, when the last attack that morning by Midway planes was completed, the Japanese striking force commenced retirement to the north or northwest. Consequently it was not found in the estimated position by our carrier attack groups."
- **Mobile Force.** 29.8 N 178.9 W (derived), heading 70, `mass`, `engaged`. `midway-strike` at the same position, recovered, strength 0.94: the moment the strikes level shows a strike glyph on a carrier that is not drawn. The readiness of the second wave in the report's own words, the public-domain basis for what the histories argue over: "CarDiv 1 advised that its ship-based attack planes (torpedo equipped) would be ready for the take-off at 0730 and CarDiv 2 that its ship-based attack planes ... would be ready for the take-off by from 0730 to 0800" (NAG p. 7; 10:30 to 11:00).
- **TF 16 strikes.** In the air heading 239 at 19,000 feet (the bombers) and "at 100 knots below the clouds" (VT-8, HOR para. 7). `hornet-group` turning south; `enterprise-strike` turning north (CINCPAC para. 30: "This was one of the most important decisions of the battle and one that had decisive results. Soon after 1000 he made contact and prepared to attack.").
- **Captions.** NAG p. 7: "It was therefore decided that we would await the return of the Midway attack unit and then carry out a grand scale air attack."

### 7. `torpedo-squadrons` — VT-8, VT-6 and VT-3 attack unsupported (09:20)

- **Time.** CINCPAC para. 31: "the Hornet's torpedo squadron led by Lt. Comdr. J. C. Waldron had found the enemy and without hesitation at about 0920 conducted a most gallant and heroic attack entirely unsupported." NAG p. 18: "0640 Sight 14 enemy torpedo planes in position bearing 140 degrees to port, elevation 1 degree, distance 40,000 meters. (Akagi)." (09:40); p. 19: "0658 14 enemy planes divided in two groups are heading for us, particularly for CarDiv 1. One section carry out torpedo attacks against the Kaga. Kaga successfully evades." (09:58, VT-6); "0715 Akagi sights enemy torpedo plane group of 12 planes" (10:15, VT-3). ENT: "1220 VT commenced attack" (10:20).
- **Mobile Force.** About 30.0 N 178.9 W, heading 300 (NAG p. 8: "At 0706, enemy torpedo planes were sighted bearing 118 degrees. To minimize the target area, the Akagi turned to course 300 degrees and stayed on this course"), `engaged`, formation loosening but cohesion held. CN ch. 7: "At the time of contact, the enemy ships were on a course of 270°, but as our planes appeared they turned to starboard, shifting their course to 000° and, before our planes dropped, to 180°."
- **`vt-8`.** `engaged` at 09:20, `destroyed` by 09:40, strength 0: "Not a plane survived this magnificent devotion to purpose" (CINCPAC para. 32). `enterprise-strike` and `yorktown-strike` `engaged` from 10:00, their torpedo squadrons cut to four of fourteen and two of twelve.
- **Captions.** CINCPAC para. 33: "The results was that the VT squadrons were a sacrifice that enabled the dive bombers to make their attack almost unopposed, with disastrous results for the enemy." Para. 32: "One pilot, after attacking and probably hitting the Kaga at close range, with his gunner already killed, crashed near the Akagi, ducked under his seat cushion to prevent being machine gunned, and from this reserved position observed the fierce attacks that followed."

### 8. `three-carriers-hit` — Kaga, Akagi and Soryu bombed within six minutes (10:22)

- **Time.** CINCPAC para. 36: "The dive bombing attacks by both Enterprise and Yorktown squadrons began at about the same time, between 1020 and 1025." ENT: "1222 VSB commenced dive bombing attack ... Position of enemy force, Lat. 30° 05' N, Long. 178° 50' W." NAG p. 9: Kaga, "9 enemy dive bombers were suddenly sighted among the clouds at 0722 ... #4 hit starboard, aft, while #7 was a direct hit in the vicinity of the forward elevator ... Bomb #8 hit in the vicinity of the forward elevator. Practically nothing of the bridge remained after this hit"; Akagi, "0726 Three bombers dive on Akagi ... First was a near miss about 10 meters abeam of the bridge; second hit near the elevator amidship (fatal hit); third hit the flight deck on the portside, aft" (p. 20); Soryu, "Three hits were scored on her at 0725, 0726, and 0728" (p. 10).
- **Mobile Force.** 30.1 N 178.8 W, `broken`, strength 0.25; `akagi`, `kaga`, `soryu` `broken` (afire, out of action, afloat), `hiryu` `intact` and drawing off north. Nagumo "decided to direct the operations from the Nagara, and transferred to her at 0830" (NAG p. 8; 11:30).
- **Strikes.** `enterprise-strike` `engaged` at the target, strength 0.5 after the day; `yorktown-strike` `engaged`, VB-3 whole. Midway's aircraft home. Nautilus is caption matter: "at 1359 fired the first of 3 torpedoes into the smoking carrier Soryu" (CINCPAC para. 37); Amagai on Kaga: "while swimming in water several hours after attack saw a torpedo apparently fired from submarine strike side of ship at angle and bounce off. Didn't explode" (USSBS 1). The report and the survivor disagree on which carrier Nautilus hit; the file need not decide.
- **Captions.** CINCPAC para. 35: "Both Kaga and Akagi, between which he lay, were hit repeatedly, the planes on deck that they sought to launch being ignited until the two ships burned fiercely from stem to stern." NAG p. 7: "About 30 carrier-based bombers then attacked us resulting in fires aboard the Akagi, Kaga, and Soryu, forcing them to fall behind and leaving only the Hiryu untouched." CN ch. 7: "The fourth carrier, the Hiryu had withdrawn to the north undamaged."

### 9. `hiryu-strikes-back` — Hiryu launches 18 dive bombers; Nagumo shifts to Nagara (10:58)

- **Time.** NAG p. 8: "At 0758, Hiryu's attack unit (6 fighters and 18 bombers) took off and carried out the attack." (10:58). ENT: "1255 Commenced landing VF escort ... 1410 Completed landing attack group" (10:55 to 12:10). CINCPAC para. 40: "at 1150 Yorktown launched scouts to search sector 280-030 to 200 miles."
- **Hiryu.** `engaged`; `hiryu-bombers` heading about 100 toward TF 17, `intact`. The Nagara group forming for a surface action: "the following order was issued at 0853: 'We are now going to attack. ## The fourth arm: aircraft

v2's `arm` is `infantry`, `cavalry` or `ship`, "a closed list kept in code like the licence table: a new arm is a code change that also adds a sign in every view" (schema 2.3); ADR-0015 declined to reserve `air` "until a battle needs it". Midway needs it, and this is what a strike needs from each field.

- **`arm: "aircraft"`.** Identity, on the strike units and on the `midway` root. Every view gains a sign: the chart plate's chevron tick is already a ship; a strike wants a distinct repeated shape (the design decision's, not this note's), Atlas a block with its own diagonal. The legend gains an aircraft row because the roster has two arms.
- **Position.** Before launch, the carrier's position, copied. In flight, the strike's own position at the phase instant, tweened between phases: a strike launched at 07:00 and attacking at 10:22 tweens across the interval at the aircraft's constant speed, which is the schema's rule for every unit. At the attack, the target's position (offset a few miles so the two glyphs read). After recovery, the carrier's position again. **A round trip inside one interval cannot be drawn**: the tween is a straight line, so every strike needs a phase at its launch, one at its attack and one at its recovery, or its return is skipped by putting it back on the carrier at the next phase, a jump the track will draw as a straight flight home. The twenty-one phases above put a phase at every launch and every attack; recoveries fall in the next phase.
- **Heading.** Its course while flying; at the target, the direction of its attack run ("VT-8 ... followed all the way in"); on the way home, its course. A strike is never "retiring in good order toward the enemy", so heading and track agree for aircraft except at the target.
- **Formation.** `mass` for a strike group ("three divisional 'V''s of 9 planes in each formation", CN ch. 5), `column` for a torpedo squadron in file, `line` for a division line-abreast. Styled labels; no new value needed.
- **State.** `intact` on deck and en route; `engaged` from the attack; `broken` when it scatters (Hornet's group turning south and landing at Midway; the Hiryu bombers after the CAP cut them up); `destroyed` when nothing returns (VT-8, at 09:40, its lone survivor in the caption).
- **Strength.** The fraction of aircraft still with the strike: 15/15 to 0/15 for VT-8; 57 to 29 for Enterprise's group; 24 to 7 for Kobayashi's; 108 to 102 for Tomonaga's. Aircraft shot down have "left the unit"; aircraft that ditch on the way home too. A strike diverted to Midway is a `detachment` and a strength cut, as the schema already says for a returning detachment.
- **Moves.** `intent` from the carrier to the estimated target position at launch ("155 miles distant, bearing 239°"), for the level that draws no strike units. `detachment` for a division that split off.
- **Launch and recovery as position and state.** A launch is the strike's position leaving the carrier's at the phase instant and its state staying `intact`; a recovery is the position returning to the carrier's and the state staying `engaged`. Nothing in v2 says "embarked". The strike level therefore shows every strike of the day in every phase, sitting on its carrier before it flies and after it lands, and, because a carrier with children is not drawn at that level, sitting on open sea. Two ways out: a state value that draws nothing (`absent`, or `embarked`: "a condition, never a place"), or a rule that a parent is drawn at its children's level when its children are of a different arm. Both are schema or renderer changes; neither exists.
- **Midway's aircraft.** The `midway` root carries `arm: "aircraft"` and never moves; its strikes are children of `midway-air`. Its guns, radar, the submarine that shelled it and the PT boats are caption matter. Scouts, PBYs, the CAP and Tone's No. 4 plane are captions and `intent` arrows, not units.
- **Speed and scale.** On a 400-mile plate a strike at 110 knots crosses a fifth of the width in an hour; ships at 25 knots a twentieth. The tween shows both at their historical speed inside a phase. The 72-pixel glyph is the same for a 108-plane strike and a 10-plane one; strength thins the signs.

## Map data

**Extent.** A plate for 4 June alone: `north: 33.2, south: 27.7, west: -180, east: -173`, landscape, holding Midway, Kure, the launch point on the western edge, all four Japanese sinkings, TF 16 and TF 17 all day, Point Luck's neighbourhood and Yorktown's grave. Everything on 5 and 6 June west of the meridian, and the Japanese forces from the 4th evening on, is outside it.

The whole battle runs from 173 E (Mikuma) to 172.75 W (the 2 June rendezvous), a span of fourteen degrees across the antimeridian, and **no v2 extent can hold it**: `west < east` with both in `-180 <= lon <= 180`. Nagumo launched on the meridian, so even a tight 4 June plate has its western edge at 180. Options, for the map:

1. **Let the extent cross the antimeridian**: allow `west > east`, meaning the box wraps; the renderer adds 360 to any longitude west of `west` before projecting, in both files. The smallest change and the honest one; every other v0.2 battle is untouched because their `west < east`.
2. **Two battle files**, `midway.json` for 4 June and Yorktown's loss and `midway-pursuit.json` for 5 and 6 June. Writable today; ADR-0013 already rejected splitting the Nile across a page load, and the pursuit is a coda to the same battle.
3. **Cut the file at 4 June** and carry the pursuit as captions and `intent` arrows with heads beyond the edge; but a head at 175 E projects onto the far east of the plate, not the west, so the arrows would point the wrong way. Not usable without option 1's normalisation.

**What the map holds.** Natural Earth 10m `land` carries Midway as one `MultiPolygon` feature with two rings, Eastern Island (six vertices, 177.32 to 177.34 W) and Sand Island (nine vertices, 177.36 to 177.39 W), and Kure Atoll (ten vertices at 28.39 N 178.29 W) as another; `ne_10m_minor_islands` has nothing in the box; `ne_10m_reefs` has three features around Midway (eight, eight and five vertices), the atoll's reef ring. So the file is: two `land` polygons for Sand and Eastern Islands, one `land` polygon for Kure, one `shoal` (or three) traced from the reefs layer for the Midway reef, a `place` "Midway" and a `place` "Kure", and a `work` "Naval Air Station Midway" on Eastern Island, the target of Tomonaga's strike. No `river`, no `contour`. At the 4 June extent the atoll is six miles across on a 400-mile plate, a dot; the reef ring and the place label are what the viewer sees. **A map file for open ocean holds the one atoll and its reef and nothing else**; the sea is the parchment. Licence `public-domain`, attribution "Coastline and reef: Natural Earth (public domain)", like `cadiz.geojson`.

Alternatives for a finer atoll (not needed at this extent): NOAA chart 19480 and the USGS topographic sheet, both US government works, public domain; OpenStreetMap, which would make the file ODbL for no gain.

**What the map cannot hold.** The date line itself (a `place` at 180 would do); Point Luck (a rendezvous, not a place: caption); the fog bank the Japanese came in under; the Japanese submarine cordon "between 165W and 170W" (USSBS 13), off the plate.

## The model-extracted draft and how it fared

As the ticket allows, a first draft of the phase list was extracted by a model from the combat narrative alone, in a background agent told to use no other source and no memory of the battle, reading the fourteen HyperWar chapters through a summarising fetch. It took six minutes and about 83,000 tokens and returned twenty phases with a per-body table, a table of thirteen strikes, a list of the narrative's silences and ten internal inconsistencies.

**Where it did well.** Every one of ten verbatim claims spot-checked against the raw transcription matched character for character: the time-convention footnote, "Of the 15 planes, not one returned", "Four carriers were observed", the 2 June rendezvous position, the 0749 VT-6 launch, the 1528 recovery, the "180th meridian" sentence, and the narrative's own misprint of Yorktown's grave as "167°24' W." It found the Hornet 1445/1645/1528 inconsistency without being told about the clock, and the narrative's silences it listed (no Japanese launch times, no Japanese positions, no sinking positions, no American recovery times, no TF 16 lat/lon) are exactly the gaps the Nagumo report and the Enterprise report fill. Its times on the American side are the ones used above.

**Where it fell short.** It could not know that "At 1645" was an unconverted Zone plus 10 time, so it flagged the right sentence for the wrong reason. Its phases are event-grained (twenty for the narrative's American half alone) and every Japanese cell is a dash or a sighting; it drew the Occupation Force as "—" for three days. It split hairs the plate cannot draw (the 1350 refuelling of fighters as a phase) and merged what the plate must split (Yorktown's torpedoing and the discovery of Hiryu in one phase). The digest layer it read through returned paraphrase as often as quotation, so its quotes had to be re-checked against the raw text before any could be used, and one of its "verbatim" positions (174°30' E) is a real reading of the narrative that the ship's own log contradicts.

**Verdict.** Useful as a scaffold for the American side's times and captions, and as a checklist of what one source cannot say; not usable for the other side, for positions, or for the cut into phases. The list above kept its American times, replaced its Japanese cells from the Nagumo report, and cut twenty event-grained phases to twenty-one two-sided ones, which is fewer phases than it looks: seven of its twenty were 3 June and Midway-only sorties.

## Implications for `midway.json` and for the schema

1. **The antimeridian is the blocker.** A 4 June plate can be written with `west: -180`; the battle cannot. The recommendation is option 1 in the map section: let `west > east` mean the extent wraps, and have both files' consumers normalise longitude into the extent's frame. Until then the file is 4 June plus Yorktown's loss, which is `dates` of four entries with days 1 and 2 nearly empty, or two files.
2. **A fourth arm is required** and is the additive change ADR-0015 planned for: `aircraft` on the allowlist, a sign in each of the three views, a legend row. Its semantics for position, heading, formation, state and strength are all expressible today (fourth-arm section); nothing there needs a field.
3. **Embarked strikes cannot be expressed.** Before launch and after recovery a strike unit has a position (its carrier's), a state (`intact` or `engaged`) and a glyph; v2 has no way to say "not on the plate". ADR-0017 records that "units appearing mid-battle stays deferred"; Midway is the battle that undefers it. Two candidate answers: a `state` value that draws nothing, or a rule that a parent is drawn alongside children of another arm. This note does not pick; the map should.
4. **The depth rule hides the carriers at the strikes level.** ADR-0017's "draws every unit at that depth plus every shallower unit that has no children" assumes children partition the parent (a column into squadrons). Strikes are detachments of a carrier, not a partition of it, and the carrier vanishes when they are shown. Same decision as 3.
5. **Sixteen is enough only for a curated roster.** Ten strikes plus the three childless carriers plus CruDiv 7 is fourteen; the two distant Japanese forces must stay off the roster; the full sixteen strikes are twenty units at that level. Strikes as units fit; all strikes as units do not.
6. **Three clocks, one battle clock.** The file breaks "every time in a file is copied verbatim from the source that gives it" for two of its three source families. The fix is authoring discipline, not schema: `t` in Zone plus 12, the source's reading in `references[].quote`, the conversion in `notes`. A per-file statement of the convention has nowhere to live but the first phase's `notes` and the battle's `summary`; a `clock` display string on the battle would be a small addition if the map wants the caption band to say "Midway time".
7. **A unit that never moves.** `midway` sits at one position for four days with `heading` (say 320, toward the attack), `formation` (`mass`) and a state that changes once. Expressible, if odd; ADR-0015 already put Trekroner on the map as a `work` for the same reason, and the difference here is that Midway's aircraft flew.
8. **Positions off the plate.** The Main Body and Occupation Force are units with positions outside any 4 June extent in every phase; the validator does not check that and "the renderer clips". Better left off the roster until the extent can hold them.
9. **Damage that does not reduce strength.** Yorktown stopped and burning at 12:14 is `engaged` at strength 1; Akagi afire is `broken`; a carrier "listing 20 to 25°" is a caption. The schema's line is right (strength is fighting strength) and the plate will not show a crippled ship, which is the same limit the Nile had for dismasted ones.
10. **Submarines.** Nautilus, Tambor, Grayling and I-168 have no arm and would need `ship` with a caption. Nautilus's attack and I-168's are caption matter; the day the site wants a submarine on the plate it needs a sign, not a field.
11. **Sinking positions are the file's best-attested facts** and come from the Japanese report; the American launch positions from Enterprise's log; the American positions in between are derived from bearings and distances and should say so in `notes`.
12. **Wind.** South-east and light on the 4th and 5th, south-west and moderate on the 6th; every phase carries it. The engaged mark drifting to the lee flank will drift north-west over the burning carriers, which is right.

## Open follow-ups

- **CINCPAC enclosure (A)**, the composite track chart, would settle the Japanese launch point and the American track on the 4th and 5th; it is not in the HyperWar transcription. The NHHC action-report pages (fetched with a browser user agent) may carry the scanned enclosures; not examined.
- **The Nagumo report's Part VII** (pp. 40 onward: the strike compositions by section, the search-plane table, the losses) was grepped but not read through; it would give every strike's exact composition and the Hiryu torpedo strike's launch time if the outline has it.
- **The Yorktown report** was read only by grep; its narrative of 4 June (paras. on the launch, the two attacks, the abandonment) should be read whole before captions are cut from it.
- **The Midway garrison's own report** (NAS Midway serial 075, CINCPAC enclosure (E)) and the USMC official history chapter (HyperWar `USMC-I-V-4.html`, a government work) were not fetched; they are the sources for the VMF-221 and VMSB-241 captions and losses.
- **Copyright renewals** for Morison (1949) and Fuchida (1955) were inferred from the lending status of every Internet Archive copy, not read from the Copyright Office's renewal records; the conclusion is safe but the citation is indirect.
- **The reef geometry**: Natural Earth's three reef features around Midway were counted, not drawn; the authoring pass should render them once to see whether they close into a ring at this extent.
- **Mikuma's sinking position** is from Wikipedia only; the Japanese side's position, if any, would be in the Occupation Force's report, not the Nagumo report.
