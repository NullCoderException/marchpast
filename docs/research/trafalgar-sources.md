# Trafalgar sources: licence status and locators

*Research note for GitHub issue #10 (part of wayfinder map #1). Verified 2026-09-05 by fetching each locator; nothing below is from memory.*

## Gist

All six source families named in `docs/CONCEPT.md` are usable. Four are public domain outright (Nelson's memorandum, Collingwood's dispatch, Southey, Mahan). Two are CC BY-SA, not PD: the Wikipedia order-of-battle page (CC BY-SA 4.0, and its title has changed) and the most-used Commons battle diagram, `Trafalgar 1200hr.svg` (CC BY-SA 3.0, itself derived from a copyrighted magazine illustration). For diagrams that ship with the data, prefer the PD 1805/1848 plans listed below.

## Detail per source

### 1. Nelson's memorandum of 9 October 1805 ("the Nelson touch")

**Status.** Unpublished manuscript by an author who died in 1805; public domain everywhere. Wikisource's author page confirms: "Some or all works by this author were published before January 1, 1931, and are in the public domain worldwide because the author died at least 100 years ago." ([Author:Horatio Nelson](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Author:Horatio_Nelson_(1758-1805)))

**Text locators (verified).**

- **Nicolas (ed.), *The Dispatches and Letters of Vice Admiral Lord Viscount Nelson*, vol. VII (August to October 1805), London: Henry Colburn, 1846.** Internet Archive identifier `dispatchesletter07nels` (UC Libraries scan, 782 pp., rights "NOT_IN_COPYRIGHT"; PDF, EPUB and full text available). The memorandum begins on p. 89, headed "MEMORANDUM. (Secret.) Victory, off Cadiz, 9th October, 1805." Nicolas's provenance note: "Autograph draught in the possession of Vice-Admiral Sir George Mundy, K.C.B., except the words in Italics, which were added by Mr. Scott, Lord Nelson's Secretary; and the Original, issued to Captain Hope, of the Defence." This is the best primary transcription. The same volume reprints Collingwood's Gazette dispatch under "Public dispatches and other letters ... respecting the Battle of Trafalgar." https://archive.org/details/dispatchesletter07nels
- **Mahan, *Life of Nelson* vol. 2, ch. XXII** (Project Gutenberg #16915) reprints the memorandum in full ("MEMORANDUM. (Secret) Victory, off CADIZ, 9th October, 1805. Thinking it almost impossible to bring a Fleet of forty Sail of the Line into a Line of Battle..."). The PG plain text carries Mahan's marginal headings inline as `[Sidenote: ...]`; strip these before quoting.
- **Facsimile:** *The Nelson memorandum*, J. Newns, Tunbridge Wells, c. 1900s; 23 pp. of plates with letterpress. IA identifier `nelsonmemorandum00nels`, ark:/13960/t8mc90q9j, "Possible copyright status: NOT_IN_COPYRIGHT". Prepared while the original was owned by Benjamin Minors Woollan; on his death (1909) the manuscript passed to the British Museum. https://archive.org/details/nelsonmemorandum00nels

**Manuscripts (for provenance only, not for redistribution).**

- British Library Add MS 37953 (the Woollan copy above). The BL collection-item page that used to describe it returns 404 since the BL site rebuild.
- Royal Museums Greenwich AGC/N/25, "contemporary copy of memorandum, dated VICTORY, 9 Oct 1805" and a copy in A. J. Scott's hand. The RMG object page shows no Creative Commons licence and directs image reuse to its commercial licensing service. Do not ship RMG photographs. https://www.rmg.co.uk/collections/objects/rmgc-object-507441
- Royal Collection RCIN 1198306, fair copy signed by Collingwood, sent to Captain Grindall of *Prince*, dated 9-10 October 1805 (i.e. includes the 10 Oct addendum). Royal Collection images are rights-restricted.

**Caveats.** The text exists in multiple near-identical copies (autograph draft, Scott's fair copies, captains' copies). Wording differences are minor but real; cite Nicolas as the edition of record. A Commons search for an image of the manuscript found only IA book PDFs, no photograph of the document itself.

### 2. Collingwood's dispatch (London Gazette Extraordinary no. 15858, 6 Nov 1805)

**Status.** The 1805 text is public domain (Crown copyright in published works expires 50 years after publication). The Gazette website states: "All content is available under the Open Government Licence v3.0, except where otherwise stated." The Gazette's data terms require the attribution "Contains public sector information licenced under the Open Government Licence v3.0" when reusing its digitised content. ([thegazette.co.uk page 1365](https://www.thegazette.co.uk/London/issue/15858/page/1365); [Gazette data terms](https://www.thegazette.co.uk/data/terms-and-conditions))

**Locators (verified).**

- Issue 15858, dated Wednesday 6 November 1805, 4 pages, pp. 1365-1368. Page 1365 opens "Admiralty-Office, November 6, 1805. Dispatches, of which the following are Copies, were received at the Admiralty this Day, at One o'Clock A.M. from Vice-Admiral Collingwood ... Euryalus, off Cape Trafalgar, October 22, 1805. Sir, The ever to be lamented Death of Vice-Admiral Lord Viscount Nelson..." Also contains the second dispatch ("Euryalus, off Cadiz, Oct. 24, 1805"), the General Order of thanks, and the day-of-thanksgiving proclamation. Per-page: https://www.thegazette.co.uk/London/issue/15858/page/1365 through `/page/1368`; whole-issue PDF at https://www.thegazette.co.uk/London/issue/15858/data.pdf.
- **Wikisource transcription:** [The London Gazette/Number 15858](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_London_Gazette/Number_15858), full text of the issue, backed by `Index:The_London_Gazette_15858.pdf`, carrying Wikisource's public-domain notice (published before 1931, author dead 100+ years). Use this for quoting; the Gazette's own OCR reads "Commiffioners", "Lordlhip" etc. because of long-s glyphs.
- Also reprinted in Nicolas vol. VII (see above), "[From the London Gazette of the 6th November, 1805.]"

**Caveats.** The Gazette printed edited versions of the four documents Lapenotiere carried. Collingwood gives the enemy as "Thirty-three Ships (of which Eighteen were French and Fifteen Spanish)" and states the action began "at Twelve o'Clock"; clocks differed between ships, as CONCEPT.md already notes. Wikisource's transcription layer is CC BY-SA 4.0; the underlying text is PD.

### 3. Southey, *The Life of Horatio, Lord Nelson*

**Status.** Public domain. Project Gutenberg header: "Public domain in the USA"; Southey died 1843 so PD worldwide.

**Locator (verified).** Project Gutenberg ebook **#947**, "The Life of Horatio, Lord Nelson", released 5 Feb 2006, last updated 23 Oct 2012, credits "Produced by John Hill and David Widger". Formats: EPUB3, EPUB, Kindle, plain text (565 kB), HTML. https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/947

**Edition and caveats.** The PG text carries the 1813 dedication to John Wilson Croker and Southey's original preface ("Many Lives of Nelson have been written...") but names no source printing. Nine chapters; **Chapter IX (1805)** covers Calder's action, the return to Cadiz, Trafalgar and Nelson's death. Southey worked from Clarke & M'Arthur and other secondary accounts and does not reprint the memorandum (his only "memorandum" is Nelson's comparison of forces in an earlier chapter). Treat as caption colour, not tactical evidence. Commons also hosts a scanned edition as `File:The life of Nelson (IA lifeofnelson00sout 5).pdf` if a page-image edition is wanted.

### 4. Mahan, *The Life of Nelson: The Embodiment of the Sea Power of Great Britain*

**Status.** Public domain. PG header: "Public domain in the USA"; Mahan died 1914 so PD worldwide. The Library of Congress scan of the 1899 edition states "The Library of Congress is unaware of any copyright restrictions for this item."

**Locators (verified).**

- Project Gutenberg **#16914** (Volume 1) and **#16915** (Volume 2), both released 21 Oct 2005, "Produced by Steven Gibbs and the Online Distributed Proofreading Team". Vol. 2 last updated 12 Dec 2020. Title page in the text: London, Sampson Low, Marston & Company, **1897**. https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/16914 and https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/16915
- Internet Archive `lifeofnelsonembo01maha`: Little, Brown and Company, 1899 (Mahan's revised second edition), Library of Congress scan, ark:/13960/t9q24gx6s. https://archive.org/details/lifeofnelsonembo01maha
- IA also mirrors the PG text as `thelifeofnelsonv16914gut`.

**Edition and caveats.** The PG text is the 1897 first edition; Mahan's 1899 second edition contains corrections. For the extraction-pipeline test, vol. 2 **Chapter XXII "The Antecedents of Trafalgar" (Sept-19 Oct 1805)** contains the tactical analysis and the full memorandum; **Chapter XXIII "Trafalgar.--The Death of Nelson" (19-21 Oct 1805)** is the battle narrative. Mahan's analytical sections ("Discussion and comparison of these two papers") are his interpretation, not source fact; keep `source_refs` pointing at the memorandum itself where possible.

### 5. Wikipedia: order of battle

**Status.** CC BY-SA 4.0, **not public domain**. Footer: "Text is available under the Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike License 4.0; additional terms may apply."

**Locator (verified).** The CONCEPT.md title "Order of battle at the Battle of Trafalgar" (page ID 78236002) is now a redirect. Canonical title: **"Battle of Trafalgar order of battle"**, page ID **2313325**, latest revision **1366239788** (27 July 2026, 66,550 bytes). Cite the permalink: https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Battle_of_Trafalgar_order_of_battle&oldid=1366239788

**Content.** Tables of the 27 British ships of the line plus 6 supporting vessels by weather/lee column, and the 33 Franco-Spanish ships (18 French, 15 Spanish), with guns, commanders, casualties and fate. Tables cite Goodwin (2005) and Adkin (2005), with Clayton & Craig in notes. Images used: `File:Trafalgar 1200hr.svg`, `File:Map of the Battle of Trafalgar 1848.jpg`.

**Caveats.** The article itself says: "There is general disagreement on the exact order and the exact numbers, but, on the whole, the different estimates are close to each other." The underlying facts (ship names, guns) are not copyrightable, but the compiled tables are CC BY-SA text; if `trafalgar.json` is built from them, include a Wikipedia attribution with the revision permalink and license the derived data file CC BY-SA 4.0, or reconcile the list against PD sources (Nicolas vol. VII prints the fleet lists; Collingwood's dispatch names the captured ships) and cite those.

**Flags and recommendations.**

- **`Trafalgar 1200hr.svg` is not public domain**: CC BY-SA 3.0 (multi-licensed with GFDL 1.2+ and CC BY-SA 2.5/2.0/1.0). Redistribution requires attribution ("Pinpin, Wikimedia Commons, CC BY-SA 3.0") and share-alike on adaptations. More seriously, its parent GIF is declared to be "based on an illustration" in *Strategy & Tactics* no. 84, a copyrighted magazine; Commons has kept it, but for a project that wants to ship diagrams with the data this is a provenance risk. Recommendation: use it as a **reference when authoring positions**, do not bundle it, and if a position diagram must ship, draw our own from the primary texts (that is what the renderer does anyway).
- **Substitutes that are cleanly PD** for bundling or for a "sources" gallery: Dodd's 1805 *Plan of the Attack* (`{{PD-old-70}}`), the anonymous 1805 RMG plan K1079 (`{{PD-Art|PD-old-100-1923}}`), and Johnston's 1848 plates (`{{PD-old-100-1923}}` / `{{PD-US-expired}}{{PD-old-70}}`). Record the template string from this table in `source_refs`.
- Avoid `TrafalgarBattle.jpg` and `Trafalgar aufstellung.jpg`: PD tags without a named author or date cannot be defended.

## Not freely redistributable (do not use)

- Royal Museums Greenwich object photographs (memorandum copies AGC/N/25 and the Scott copy): no CC licence on the object pages; image reuse routed through commercial licensing. Substitute: Nicolas vol. VII text, IA `nelsonmemorandum00nels` facsimile.
- Royal Collection Trust images (RCIN 1198306 fair copy): rights-restricted. Same substitute.
- The two CC BY-SA position diagrams (`Trafalgar 1200hr.svg`/`.gif`) are redistributable *with* attribution and share-alike, but not PD; see above.
