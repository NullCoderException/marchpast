# The anatomy is fixed and a view owns how it is drawn, so a view may be a second aesthetic

The design-language decision ([#58](https://github.com/NullCoderException/marchpast/issues/58)) settled that there is one engraved system and that a view is a named treatment built from it, **never a second aesthetic**, and ADR-0014 cut the seam to match: a view is a palette, a set of pens and a glyph, and the map, furniture, caption and label passes are shared and never change. That list was cut when the only second views were an inversion of the plate and a block glyph, and both fit inside it.

Theming is the product goal for v0.3, and the **staff-map view** ([#139](https://github.com/NullCoderException/marchpast/issues/139)) — the picture in the idiom of a twentieth-century operations map — breaks the list in four places at once: its own terrain, its own compass and scale bar, its own type, its own label leader. Enumerating a longer list would only move the fence to wherever the fourth view finds it.

So the line is drawn by a rule rather than a list. **What the picture shows, where each thing sits and what it means is the same in every view; how any of it is drawn is the view's.** The fixed half is the **anatomy**; the idiom a view is drawn in is its **aesthetic**, and there may be any number of aesthetics. Decided on [#128](https://github.com/NullCoderException/marchpast/issues/128).

## Considered options

- **Enumerate again** — a longer closed list of what a view owns, reopened when the next reason arrives. Rejected: it is the move that produced this ADR. #58 guessed the seam, ADR-0014 cut it to the guess, and the first view outside the guess cost a whole ticket. A rule can be applied by the session that meets the next case; a list can only be amended by another one.
- **Only meaning is fixed**, layout being the view's too, so a staff map could put its scale bar where a staff map puts it. Rejected on the reader: a battle in two views would be two things to learn, and the comparison between views — the whole point of offering them — would go. It also collides with the phone collapse, which is layout the app already owns per layout mode and would then have to be re-decided per view *and* per mode.
- **A general table of overridable passes**, the view supplying whatever it likes. Rejected for ADR-0014's original reason, which four exercised extension points do not overturn: an extension point no view uses rots before the view that needs it arrives, and a table invites points nobody asked for. The named slots below are cut for a view that exists.
- **Richer values and no new modules** — a grid flag, a face list and a terrain recipe pushed onto the palette, every pass staying shared. Tempting, because it is already the house style: `Palette.relief` exists precisely so the map pass needs no view id. Rejected on the compass: a staff map's north arrow and an engraved rose are not one drawing under different values, and the honest expression of "not one drawing" is a function, not a flag.
- **Aesthetic as a first-class grouping** — views grouped under it, engraved code behind a shared base, the view chooser grouping its options. Rejected: the aesthetic a view belongs to is a distinction the viewer does not have and does not need. They pick a look; a grouped chooser would make them learn our factoring.
- **The whole type ramp fixed, only the face swapping.** Rejected on the page: IM Fell English runs small and dark, and a sans at the same pixel size is a different colour and a different measure, so one fixed size would make one of the two views wrong.
- **The grid as a sixth piece of furniture**, with the plate gaining an engraved graticule and a neat-line. Genuinely suits the chart plate, and rejected anyway: it re-opens a decided design in three existing views to serve one that has not been drawn. #139 may still ask for it as its own change.
- **Every view's face loaded before the first frame**, as ADR-0009 has it for the one bundled face. Rejected: it charges every visitor for faces most never see, and the charge grows with each view.
- **A fifth slot for moves now**, or a taper factor on `Pen`, against the staff map's thick arrows. Rejected as the first test of the rule below: those arrows are a sentence in a ticket body, not a drawing.

## Consequences

- **The anatomy is what every view keeps.** The projection and north up, the extent's fitting and its letterbox; which pieces of furniture there are and which corner each anchors to; the caption band full width below the plate with the clock at the left, and its slots; a label's two lines, the flank it sits on, that it is never ahead of the unit, and the order it collapses in; the four states and the three motion styles staying tellable apart when they overlap; side ink by roster order; the legend sampling its own view's glyph; a glyph's fixed length. A reader who has learned to read one view has learned to read them all.
- **A view owns how every one of those is drawn**, with no exception carved for a particular pass. This supersedes #58's "a view owns paper and inks, the glyph, the stipple and line weights, and nothing else".
- **There may be any number of aesthetics, and nothing counts them.** The chart plate, the night plate and Atlas share the engraved one; the staff map is the second. The plate and the night plate share by naming the same modules, which is value reuse and not inheritance. A new aesthetic is gated by cost, not by permission: it costs its font, its modules and its artboards, and it is worth cutting when someone can name the battle it serves — which is why a parchment view stays out of scope on map [#121](https://github.com/NullCoderException/marchpast/issues/121) today rather than being forbidden.
- **A view is a palette, pens, and four modules: `glyph`, `type`, `ground` and `furniture`.** Every pass splits into a shared half that decides where things go and a **hand** the view supplies that draws them. The night plate names the plate's four; Atlas names the plate's ground with its tint band on; the staff map names its own. The caption and label passes gain no slot of their own — they take their faces from `type` and their inks from the palette, as they do now.
- **A fifth slot is cut for a view that exists, never for one that might.** That rule is what keeps the four from becoming the rejected table, and #58's guess is the evidence for it. `pens` is therefore left exactly as it is; if #139's drawing shows that a tapered operations arrow cannot be had from a width, a dash and a head, the handoff ([#141](https://github.com/NullCoderException/marchpast/issues/141)) cuts a `moves` hand then, against a drawing. Coming back to ask is the expected path, not a failure.
- **Furniture is drawn in canvas space and anchored to a corner; ground is drawn in map space through the projection.** That is the seam, and it settles the cases after this one: the staff map's grid is a graticule in lon/lat running under the units, so it is **ground**, drawn by the view's `ground` module beside land and contours, and its edge labels ride with it. A north arrow is the rose's hand, not a new piece.
- **The furniture set stays closed, and every view draws every piece.** A view that cannot draw a scale bar is broken, not degraded — the same rule ADR-0015 sets for arms. A piece absent for want of *data*, such as the wind rose on a battle that tracks no wind, is absent in every view alike; that is the picture, not the view.
- **Type: the roles, their rank and the name/fact distinction are fixed; the face, the sizes and the device are the view's.** There is a clock, a title, a caption, a unit name, a state word, a legend line, a scale caption and a credit; they rank in that order of size; and a name reads differently from a fact. Whether that difference is italic, weight, caps or tracking is the view's, as is the face and the pixel size of each role. **"No bold" becomes a rule of the engraved aesthetic, not an invariant.**
- **A view's type moves the boxes measured from it, and that is not the layout the anatomy protects.** The caption band's height and a label's box are measured from the type today, not constants. What is protected is the band being full width below the plate with the clock at the left, and the label being two lines on the glyph's flank.
- **`src/render/style.ts` becomes `anatomy.ts`**, because it stops being "the engraved system: what every view is built from and no view may change". It keeps `GLYPH_PX`, `PLATE_MARGIN`, `STATES`, `axes`, `leeDrift` and `sideColours`. `SIGNS_PER_GLYPH` and the sign box describe how the *engraved* glyph fills its 72 pixels and mean nothing to a symbol rectangle, so they move into the engraved glyph modules.
- **`GLYPH_PX` is anatomy and ADR-0016 stands untouched.** The fixed 72-pixel long axis is the comparability guarantee at unit scale: it is what makes Trafalgar at 13:30 equally crowded in every view.
- **`MARK_REACH` moves onto the glyph, beside `halfWidth`.** Its comment says it sits in `style.ts` "because the label pass is shared across views and may not read one view's glyph (ADR-0014)", and that reason is already false — the label pass calls `glyph.halfWidth`. Reach along the axis is the same kind of fact as reach across it, one view's engaged mark is nothing like another's, and a shared worst-case constant charges every view for the plate's Billow.
- **The default view's face blocks the first frame; every other face is fetched at idle after first paint.** A switch applies when its face has resolved, which after an idle prefetch is always already true; if it somehow is not, **the switch waits rather than drawing wrong**. ADR-0009's absolute — never draw in a fallback face, because canvas fails silently — is kept by making the failure mode late instead of wrong, and first paint stays at one face however many views there are. Research [#126](https://github.com/NullCoderException/marchpast/issues/126) found the build-time still renderer needs a `.woff2` import hook, so a second face is a second hook entry and the Node path loads faces the same way. Which face the staff map carries is #139's call.
- **The anatomy is enforced by a conformance test parameterised over `VIEWS`, and it is a handoff deliverable.** It asserts only what survives a view moving its type: which corner each piece of furniture anchors to, that the caption band is full width below the plate with the clock at the left, that a label sits on its glyph's flank and never ahead of it, that a glyph's long axis is `GLYPH_PX`, that the legend samples its own view's glyph. **Anchors, containment and order — never pixels.** The precedent is `Glyph.signs: Record<Arm, Sign>`, which makes a new arm fail to compile until every view has drawn it; types alone cannot do this job, because no type says the scale bar landed in the bottom left.
- **ADR-0014 stands superseded in named clauses.** What no longer holds: that a view is a palette, pens and a glyph and nothing else; that the map, furniture and caption passes never change; that the label is not a view's to replace; and that adding a fourth view is a value in the views file. What stands, and is not restated anywhere else: the view as a per-frame argument to `render` rather than a member of the `Picture` or `createRenderer` configuration, the glyph's two halves and why the units pass runs every mark before any body, the scratch canvas the engaged cloud is composited on, and a glyph never learning a compass bearing. ADR-0009, ADR-0015 and ADR-0016 stand untouched.
- **ADR-0014's canvas-edge clause is not this decision's to settle.** Whether a view reaches the player's controls is [#127](https://github.com/NullCoderException/marchpast/issues/127), open at the time of writing; this ADR changes nothing about it.
