# Conjecture is notes matter, and a battle authors one reading

The Little Bighorn research ([#123](https://github.com/NullCoderException/marchpast/issues/123)) reports a battle where one side's whole action and the other side's decisive hour are reconstruction: no army witness survived Custer's fight, so his battalion's position, heading, formation and state in phases 5 to 9 are read off the dead, the cartridge cases and the Native accounts, and the readings disagree. The note carries a conjecture table — five units across fourteen phases, each cell marked T for testimony, M for the Maguire survey or Godfrey's plan, A for archaeology, R for reconstruction — and asks whether the schema should hold any of it. We decided that **it should not**: no field marks a position, a state or a phase as conjectural, a battle authors one reading and names the one it declined, and the account of what the reading rests on lives in `notes`, which becomes required. Nothing is added to `src/schema/` and `schema_version` stays `2`.

The rule already existed three times over — `docs/schema.md` §1 ("No uncertainty or confidence field anywhere"), ADR-0001 ("Positions carry no uncertainty field"), ADR-0006 ("No time-uncertainty field") — and the map file already carries the geometry half of it, "Nothing marks geometry as conjectural" (§3.5), reaffirmed for a rampart trace by ADR-0026. What was missing is the battle-file sibling, written down at the battle that tests it hardest, and the obligation that makes the rule honest rather than merely quiet.

## Considered options

- **A `conjectural` boolean on the UnitSnapshot.** The smallest thing that could work, and rejected on the grounds that killed a fifth state ([#69](https://github.com/NullCoderException/marchpast/issues/69)) and an `anchored` flag ([#72](https://github.com/NullCoderException/marchpast/issues/72)): it prices a glyph treatment and a legend row for a *reason* rather than a picture. Under ADR-0021 the price is higher than it was for either of those, because what the picture shows and what it means is anatomy: a conjecture mark would have to appear in every view or none, so no view could decline it, and it buys a fifth thing every reader must learn to read.
- **A `basis` enum on the UnitSnapshot** (`testimony`, `survey`, `archaeology`, `reconstruction`), the conjecture table's letters made data. Rejected because **the claim is compound and the field is atomic**. Phase 8's Keogh wing is survey and archaeology for where it stood, testimony (Gall) for the order it collapsed in, and reconstruction for which companies were in it. One value flattens that into a falsehood, and four values still cannot say which part of the assertion each covers. The research reached the same conclusion by another road: "a `basis` enum would be styling in data the moment a view drew it."
- **A per-phase confidence.** Rejected more easily: a phase asserts a time, a caption and up to sixteen units' positions and states, and at the Little Bighorn phase 8 is testimony for Reno and Benteen and reconstruction for Custer in the same instant. A single number over that is not a summary of anything.
- **A mark on the Reference** — annotate the pointer rather than the unit. Rejected: a reference vouches for the phase as a whole (ADR-0006), so marking it says nothing about which of the phase's claims the doubt attaches to, and it is the same enum one level away from the thing it describes.
- **A `kind` on the source entry.** Rejected: the sources table exists for the licence audit and the display label, and the classes do not partition. Godfrey's 1892 plan is testimony *and* a survey; Fox is reconstruction built *from* archaeology; Gall's account is testimony taken fourteen years later through an interpreter. `label` and `work` already let a reader see that "Maguire, 1876 survey" is a survey.
- **Two phase lists for a contested interval** — Custer went to Medicine Tail ford (Curley, Martin, and the cartridge cases), Custer did not (Godfrey) — the ticket's alternative-readings option. Rejected: the player would have to answer "which one plays?", and every answer to that (a chooser, a default, a split scrubber) is the source-comparison tool ADR-0006 declined to build. The battle authors the reading it can best defend and names the other.
- **Absence for a unit with no witness**, now that ADR-0024 has given a phase the right to omit a roster unit. Rejected on ADR-0024's own terms, which are already exactly right: absence means the unit does not exist on the plate at that instant, "never 'unknown' and never 'off the extent'". A glyph that vanishes for five phases reads as *destroyed* or *left the field* to a viewer who has learned the four states, so silence would be misread as a claim — a louder lie than the guess it was meant to avoid. Custer's battalion is present, fighting, and unwitnessed; it is drawn in all fourteen phases.
- **T, M, A and R as project-wide vocabulary.** Rejected: it is the rejected enum smuggled back as prose. The four letters fit the Little Bighorn because that battle has a survey of its dead and a cartridge-case archaeology; Trafalgar has neither, and forcing four letters onto a battle with one kind of evidence produces filler.
- **Leaving `notes` optional**, as ADR-0006 made it. Rejected: if conjecture lives in `notes` and `notes` may be absent, the schema permits a file to assert Custer's five phases with no account of where they came from. All four existing battles already write one on every phase, so the obligation costs nothing and closes the hole the rest of this decision rests on.

## Consequences

- **No field is added and `schema_version` stays `2`.** Like ADR-0018, ADR-0019 and ADR-0026, this is wording and authoring discipline. `src/schema/` gains no type; the one validator change is that `notes` is now a required shape rule, which no existing file violates.
- **`notes` is required on every phase**, amending ADR-0006's "optional". It is the field that carries the whole of this decision, so it stops being something an author may omit.
- **A phase's `notes` accounts for what its assertions rest on**, in the labelled-strand style Cannae arrived at without anyone writing it down: `Time:` for the reading of the clock, `Positions:` for where the units are and what fixes them, and a third strand where the evidence itself is the story. What words a battle uses for its evidence are its own; the obligation is the rule, the vocabulary is not.
- **A battle authors one reading and names the one it declined.** Cannae already does this — Kromayer's right bank drawn, with Delbrück, Lehmann, De Sanctis and Connolly named in `notes` — and it is now the rule rather than one author's habit. The declined reading goes in `notes` and its advocate in the sources table, whether or not any phase references it.
- **A unit whose position nobody witnessed is authored like any other.** The schema treats Custer's battalion exactly as it treats Reno's, and that is right: the file is the author's reading, and the reading is what the app shows. `notes` says five times over that it is a reading off the ground.
- **The Little Bighorn's letters are local shorthand.** Its conjecture table is five units by fourteen phases, which is unreadable as prose, so its `notes` carry T, M, A and R with the key given once in the battle's first phase. No other battle inherits them.
- **ADR-0024's absence is available to the Little Bighorn for the right reason.** The village's two bodies exist as distinct bodies only from phases 3 to 9, when the fighting divides them; outside that interval they may be absent under ADR-0024 because they were not two bodies, never because nobody knows where they were. That distinction — non-existence, not ignorance — is the whole of ADR-0024's narrow reading, and this is the case that shows the two apart.
- **Nothing is retroactive and no battle file changes.** Cannae's Aufidus, Trafalgar's clocks and the Nile's groundings are already doing this. This ADR writes down a practice rather than imposing one, which is why it costs the four existing files nothing but a re-read.
- **The glossary sharpens two entries.** **Notes** widens past a disputed *time* — the Little Bighorn's disputes are about place, and Custer's about a place with no witness — and gains the declined-reading obligation. **Position** stops reading as a fact: it is the author's reading of where a unit's centre was, which does more honest work on every battle in the library than any per-phase flag would have done on one.
- **`docs/schema.md` gains the battle-file half of §3.5's rule**, beside the map file's "Nothing marks geometry as conjectural", and its §1 Uncertainty row now names conjecture and evidence class among what has no field.
- **Nothing new reaches the plate or the player.** The Details panel already shows the current phase's `notes` (ADR-0023), and Cannae's already run to seven hundred characters, so the viewer who wants to know what a position rests on can already find out. The research's parting observation — that this is the battle where a viewer would most want the evidence on the plate — is answered by the anatomy argument above, not deferred.
