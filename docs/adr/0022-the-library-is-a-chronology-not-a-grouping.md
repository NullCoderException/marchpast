# The library is a chronology, not a grouping

*Amended 2026-09-07: the restatement below of ADR-0011's `?battle=<name>` and "no memory" is overtaken — a battle is a page at `/<name>/` ([ADR-0028](0028-a-battle-is-a-page-at-its-own-path-and-its-card-is-its-own-still.md)) and the viewer's view is remembered ([ADR-0023](0023-a-view-reaches-the-player-surface-and-is-remembered.md)); #151 set the look — a 52rem measure, a 48px gutter for the rail and the thumbnail at 288 × 182, recorded in ADR-0025 (#164). The chronology, the rail, the intervals and the twelve-battle trigger stand.*

*Amended 2026-09-07, second: **Alesia → the Nile is 1,848 years, not the 1,849 this document was written with**, and every occurrence below is corrected. The rule is untouched — the number was computed from the years alone, at a time when Alesia was a bare “52 BC” with no day, which is the arithmetic the Considered Options reject by name. Alesia’s `sort_date` is `{ -52, 9, 20 }` ([#178](https://github.com/NullCoderException/marchpast/issues/178)) and the Nile’s is 1 August, six weeks short of the anniversary. The three numbers are forced: Cannae (2 August) and the Nile (1 August) straddle Alesia, so 164 and this one must sum to Cannae → the Nile, which is 2,012. Found while building the rail on [#177](https://github.com/NullCoderException/marchpast/issues/177); the five other rows hold on the real dates.*

ADR-0011 put the library's grouping off "until the list is long enough to need one". v0.3 makes the list seven — Cannae (216 BC), Alesia (52 BC), the Nile (1798), Copenhagen (1801), Trafalgar (1805), the Little Bighorn (1876) and Midway (1942) — which is the moment that clause named, so [#129](https://github.com/NullCoderException/marchpast/issues/129) came back to it and answered in the negative.

**The library does not group. It stays one flat list, oldest first, and gains a chronology rail: a line down a gutter to the left of the cards, a bare node per battle, and the run between two battles carrying its elapsed interval in words.** Decided on [#129](https://github.com/NullCoderException/marchpast/issues/129).

The fact that settled it is that **the sort already makes every cluster worth having.** Cannae and Alesia are adjacent in date order. Nelson's three are adjacent in date order. There is no third cluster in seven battles. Grouping would paint headings onto runs the existing order produces for free, and three headings over a 2/3/2 split is more chrome than list.

What the list did lack is a spine. The product's claim is two millennia, and a flat list of seven dated cards states the span without ever showing it: the 1,848 years between Alesia and the Nile pass in the same 1px border as the two years between the Nile and Copenhagen. The rail is what makes the span visible, and the intervals are what make it visible **without a scale**:

| | interval |
| --- | --- |
| Cannae → Alesia | 164 years |
| Alesia → the Nile | **1,848 years** |
| the Nile → Copenhagen | 2 years |
| Copenhagen → Trafalgar | 4 years |
| Trafalgar → the Little Bighorn | 70 years |
| the Little Bighorn → Midway | 65 years |

A rail true to those numbers is arithmetically dead. To draw the Nile → Copenhagen gap at even 8 pixels, the Alesia → the Nile void is 7,400 pixels — twenty screens of nothing — and at that scale Nelson's three still land inside a single card's height. So the rail is **ordinal**: equal spacing, the gap written rather than drawn. *1,848 years later* followed immediately by *2 years later* and *4 years later* makes Nelson's cluster legible **as a cluster**, which no amount of spacing can do at this span.

## Considered options

- **Group by era**, ancient / age of sail / modern, two and three and two. Rejected as the best of the groupings and still not worth it: every group it makes, the sort already makes, and it charges three headings to a seven-item list to say what the dates on the cards say. It also asks us to name eras, which is a historiographical claim the library has no reason to make and would have to defend at the boundaries — the Little Bighorn and Midway under one word is a stretch in any vocabulary.
- **Group by theatre**, land and sea and air. Rejected twice over: Midway is a sea battle fought by aircraft and would straddle two groups or misrepresent itself in one, and grouping by theatre breaks the chronological order, which is the one thing ADR-0011 fixed and the thing the span depends on.
- **Group by war or campaign.** Rejected on the arithmetic: it yields six groups over seven battles, five of them singletons, and it splits Nelson's three across two wars — the Nile and Copenhagen are the French Revolutionary Wars, Trafalgar is Napoleonic — so the one real cluster in the library is the one it would break.
- **Group by commander.** Rejected: Nelson has three and nobody else has two, so it is a group of three and four groups of one, and it is already contiguous without any grouping at all.
- **A rail true to scale.** Rejected on the numbers above. **A broken scale**, to-scale within a run with an axis break across the void, was the honest version of it and is rejected too: Nelson's three still collide, and a per-run scale is a chart convention that a reader has no reason to expect on a front door and no key to read.
- **The year on the node**, so the rail reads as a timeline axis. Rejected on a schema fact: `sort_date` restates `dates[0]` in machine form and **nothing checks the two agree**, so a card would carry two independently authored years and a typo would show as a disagreement on the front door. `dates[0]` is a display string that is never parsed, so it cannot be split to avoid the repeat either. One date per card, and the rail says nothing a card does not.
- **Era names as ticks on the rail** rather than as group headings. Rejected for the same reason as grouping by era, with none of the navigation benefit that made grouping worth asking about.
- **An `era` or `war` field on the battle file**, so the grouping is authored rather than derived. Rejected with the grouping it would serve. It is worth recording why it was tempting: a derived grouping would have had the build make an editorial claim in code, and authoring is the honest way to make claims here. Since no grouping survives, neither does the field, and the whole decision costs the battle file nothing.
- **Newest first**, or a "new" badge, so a returning visitor sees what has arrived. Rejected: the site remembers nothing and the file carries no added-date, so "new" could only mean when *we* got to a battle rather than when it was fought, and reading the span forward is what makes it a span.
- **A navigation timeline** — a sticky condensed rail at the top of the page, a year clicking through to a battle. Rejected for now: seven entries are one thumb-scroll, so a jump control would be more interface than list; a horizontal timeline scrolls sideways and fights touch; and **scrubber** already means the control you drag through a battle, so a second draggable timeline over history on the front door would blur a defined term. This is the option the trigger below re-arms.
- **The interval computed from `sort_date.year` alone.** Rejected: it says three years between the Nile and Copenhagen, which are twenty months apart, and seventy-one between Trafalgar and the Little Bighorn. Cheap and wrong in the two places a reader can check.
- **Dropping the `<ol>`** for a sequence of `<div>`s, so the interval lines can sit between entries. Rejected: `<ol>` may only contain `<li>`, and the fix is to move the interval inside the following entry rather than to give up the list semantics and the count.
- **The interval inside the entry's `<a>`.** Rejected: ADR-0011 makes the whole entry the link, so every link would announce as "1,848 years later, The Battle of the Nile, …" and the interval would become part of the name of the thing being chosen.

## Consequences

- **No grouping at seven, and the deferral now has a number instead of a feeling.** ADR-0011's "until the list is long enough" is replaced by a trigger: revisit when the library passes **twelve** battles, or when any grouping would hold three or more entries that chronology does not already make contiguous. The navigation timeline comes back with it.
- **The library gains a chronology rail**: a thin gutter to the left of the whole card, a line, a bare node per battle, and the interval between two battles set in the run between them. The rail is the library's, not a view's — the library has one look and never follows the viewer's view (ADR-0014, #47).
- **An interval is whole elapsed years, floored, computed from the full `sort_date`.** Phrased *"1,848 years later"*, thousands separated; the word *later* is what makes it a chronology rather than a caption. Under a year it falls back to whole months, and two battles in the same year read *"later the same year."* The first entry has no interval.
- **The arithmetic is calendar-naive and knows about the missing year zero.** Cannae's 216 BC is a proleptic Julian date and Midway's is Gregorian; the years between them are counted as though one calendar ran throughout. A floor to whole years absorbs that everywhere except where two battles fall within days of the same anniversary, and this is a stated cost, not an oversight: the library's job is to show a span, not to date an event.
- **`sort_date`'s "never counted from" is amended, in a named clause of ADR-0013.** The new rule: **compared freely; counted from only for the interval between two battles, never within one.** ADR-0013's reason stands untouched and is what the amendment is cut around — the battle clock is a reading rather than an instant, there is no point on a global timeline to be had, and so no duration *inside* a battle may be computed from `sort_date`, which is never a timezone, never UTC and never a JavaScript `Date`. The distance between two battles is a different kind of number: it is about the library's shelf, not about either battle's clock.
- **The interval is the first child of the following `<li>`, outside its `<a>`.** The `<ol>` stays valid and keeps its count, the link's accessible name stays the battle, and the reading order is "1,848 years later" then the entry, which is the order the page means. **The rail's line and its nodes are `aria-hidden`**: they are a drawing of what the text beside them already says.
- **The card gains its `sides`.** `data/index.json` has carried `LibraryEntry.sides` since ADR-0011 and the page has thrown it away; it is set as an aside beneath the date, in roster order. Seven cards of title and one long sentence is a wall of prose, and the sides are the one fact that answers "who fought" without reading it. The arms are **not** added: they would need a new field in `index.json` to say what the still is about to say better.
- **The card's insides are unchanged and the rail sits outside them**: the still to the left of the text on a desktop, above it on a phone, so the title stays the first thing read. The library's measure **may widen** to hold rail, still and text; by how much is the drawing's call. **The rail survives on a phone**, narrower — the intervals are content and carry the chronology even if the line thins to a hairline. If three columns cannot be made to work at any sane measure, the still moves to the right of the text and the rail keeps the gutter: the spine outranks the picture's side.
- **No new field and no new validator rule.** `data/index.json` is unchanged, the battle file is unchanged, and everything above is the page's own arithmetic over what the library already holds. That is the strongest property of this decision and the reason it could be taken before the thumbnails and the brand.
- **The Picker gains nothing.** It is a flat `<select>` of titles in library order and would have taken `<optgroup>` for free had a grouping survived; none did, so it is untouched.
- **ADR-0011 stands, less its grouping clause.** Everything else it decided is unaffected: the library built from the battle files, `?battle=<name>`, no default battle and no memory, `summary`, `data/index.json`, oldest first, and only battles in `data/battles/`. Its "grouping the library is out of scope until the list is long enough to need it" is superseded by this ADR.
- **The look is not this ADR's, and is [#151](https://github.com/NullCoderException/marchpast/issues/151).** Gutter width, line weight, what a node is, how the interval line is set, how the rail meets the masthead and the credit line, and the measure. This ADR fixes what the rail is and what it says; the drawing decides how it reads, against the brand's type and materials and beside the still.
