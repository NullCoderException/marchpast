# Anchoring and the truce are caption matter, and a unit's heading is its fighting front

ADR-0003 fixed a four-value **state** enum; ADR-0008 gave a phase its wind; ADR-0016 gave formation a third word and kept heading as the front; ADR-0018 held all of it against Cannae, the hardest land case, and left the two naval questions open. The Nile ([#42](https://github.com/NullCoderException/sandtable/issues/42)) and Copenhagen ([#43](https://github.com/NullCoderException/sandtable/issues/43)) are the naval reuse tests, and between them they put three things to v1 that Trafalgar never did: fleets that fight at anchor and cannot move, a battle that ends in a truce rather than a rout, and ships that ground and are still aground when the file runs out.

We decided that **v1's vocabulary carries both battles unchanged**. Being at anchor is caption matter, the truce is caption matter, and the groundings are the `detachment` moves and strength decay ADR-0018 already describes. Nothing is added to `src/schema/`. What does change is that a rule authored into one Trafalgar phase's `notes` and never written down becomes a glossary rule: **`heading` is where a unit's force is directed, not where its bows point**, so a moored fleet in line of battle is a `line` whose heading is its broadside.

## Considered options

- **A fourth formation word, `anchored`**, the smallest addition the Nile research proposed. Rejected on ADR-0018's own grounds: **Formation** is the shape a unit's signs are arranged in, and the Danish line is a line *and* anchored. One enum would carry a shape and a condition, and the shape is the half that would be lost.
- **An optional per-phase `anchored` flag** the glyph and the unit card could carry. Rejected: it prices a glyph change and a legend row for a *reason* rather than a picture. The plate already shows a unit that does not move, because its position repeats; and the "at anchor or merely becalmed" ambiguity the research worried about is answered by the wind's `force: calm`, which ADR-0008 already provides.
- **A fifth state for a ceasefire.** Rejected, and more easily than ADR-0018 rejected `routed`: a truce is a fact about the *battle*, not a condition of a unit. Both sides acquire it in the same instant, so a per-unit enum would carry it redundantly on every unit at once, at the price of a fifth glyph and a fifth legend row.
- **A battle-level `truce` flag.** Rejected on ADR-0013's precedent, which gave the Nile no `night` flag because the clock already says it is 23:00. `caption` is required, and the caption already says the guns have stopped.
- **The bows convention for an anchored line**, which both research notes assumed: Copenhagen's British line as a `column` heading NNW, the Nile's French line as a `column` heading NW, because that is where the ships' heads actually lay. Rejected: it contradicts the glossary's **Heading** as written and ADR-0016's restatement of it, it makes the v1 Trafalgar file wrong, and it splits the word `line` by arm — a naval line of battle would be a `column` while a line of infantry stayed a `line`.
- **Reading "does not return" past the last phase**, so *Defiance* and *Elephant* cost Nelson's division nothing because they floated off on a later tide. Rejected: strength is a per-phase reading, and the file is the whole of the evidence.

## Consequences

- **Anchoring is caption matter, on the same shelf as a crescent, a wedge and a hollow.** The French at Aboukir meant to fight under way and were caught at their cables; the Danish line was blockships that were never going anywhere. That difference is a sentence, not a field, and it is the sentence each phase's caption exists to write. The glossary had already leaned this way: ADR-0015 defined **Ship** as a unit of vessels "whether they are under way or at anchor", which says anchoring is not part of what a unit is.
- **`heading` is where the unit's force is directed**: its course while it is moving, the way its guns or its line face while it is fighting. This is what the v1 Trafalgar file does — the Combined Fleet is `line`/275 at 10:00, "its front faces the attack coming from the west and its ticks run north and south as the fleet did, with the ships' heads at 005" — and it was recorded only in that phase's `notes`. It is now the **Heading** entry's, so the next author does not have to find it.
- **A moored fleet in line of battle is a `line`, not a `column`.** Copenhagen's British line is a `line` with its heading on the Danes, the Danish line a `line` with its heading on the British, the Nile's French line a `line` with its heading seaward. Both conventions draw the unit on the same axis; they differ in which way each ship-sign points and which flank the label pass and Billow take as the front. Choosing the front keeps `line` meaning one thing across every arm.
- **A unit fought from both sides still has one front.** The Nile's French van is doubled, so the author picks the side the attack came from and the caption is about; the doubling is shown by the two British units' positions, one inside the line and one outside, which is what ADR-0002's "the phase *is* the positions" intends.
- **The two research notes are corrected at authoring time.** Their `column` headings for the anchored lines predate this rule; the phase tables, times, positions and sources they carry are unaffected.
- **A fleet under a flag of truce is `engaged`.** ADR-0003's rule that a unit which has fought stays engaged when the firing stops is what carries Copenhagen's phases 7 and 8, where nothing fires and the battle is not over. The Danish line's `destroyed` at 15:15 is right by the definition even though the hulks are still afloat, and the flag-of-truce boat is the `intent` move it always was. The Nile's overnight lull asks nothing further: Berry's ten minutes of silence falls inside a phase.
- **Trekroner never reaches this question.** ADR-0012 made it a `work` and ADR-0015 kept it out of the roster, so the one thing at Copenhagen that ceases fire without being a unit has no state to change.
- **The groundings are ADR-0018's rule used as written.** *Agamemnon*, *Bellona* and *Russell* are detached from Nelson's division and do not come back, so each is a `detachment` arrow toward the Middle Ground and each costs strength; the division steps 1 → 0.92 → 0.75.
- **"Does not return" means does not return within the battle.** *Defiance* and *Elephant* ground during the withdrawal and are still fast when the file ends, so they cost strength in the phases they are aground, and the caption says they were got off afterwards. Strength is the fraction still fighting *as part of the unit during a phase*; a rule that read past the last phase would make it depend on events the file does not contain.
- **Nothing in `src/schema/` changes and no field is added.** Like ADR-0018, this is wording and authoring discipline. It amends the glossary's **Heading**, **Formation** and **Strength** entries; `docs/schema.md` stays the v1 spec until the handoff ([#53](https://github.com/NullCoderException/sandtable/issues/53)) writes v2.
- **The three v1 gaps the naval research raised are now all closed**: midnight by ADR-0013, the shoal and the fort by ADR-0012, and anchoring, the truce and the groundings here. The Nile and Copenhagen are authorable against v2 with no further design decision.
