# Hierarchy is authored at every level, and the viewer picks the level shown

Map #1 ruled unit hierarchy out of v0.1 and left it to return with squadron subdivision. It returns in schema v2 with Trafalgar at squadron level as the test: Nelson's and Collingwood's columns into their van and rear, the Combined Fleet into van, centre, rear and Gravina's squadron of observation. We decided that a roster unit may name a **`parent`**, that a parent and a child are both **full units** with a snapshot in every phase, that nothing rolls up from one level to the other, and that the **viewer picks the level shown** with a Level chooser beside the View chooser, every visit opening on the coarsest. A battle names its levels in a top-level `levels` list; showing a level draws every unit at that depth plus any shallower unit that was never subdivided. The tree stops at units, a subtree shares one side, a parent carries one arm, and the label proof's ceiling of sixteen units drawn at once becomes a validation rule per level. Trafalgar grows in place into one file at columns and squadrons.

## Considered options

- **A resolution switch, both levels authored** (chosen): the only reading under which every question the ticket raised has an answer, and the only one where the coarse and the fine picture of a battle are both facts from the sources rather than one derived from the other. ADR-0003 already forbids deriving state and strength, and a column's position while its squadrons lie strung across two miles is not a centroid anyone would author. The cost is authoring, not schema: Trafalgar gains about seventy snapshots.
- **A grouping only**: squadrons authored, a column a named group with no snapshot, used for the unit card and the legend while the plate always draws the finest level. Rejected: the parent becomes a half-unit every rule must special-case, and Trafalgar at three units survives only as the v1 file, so the Library lists two Trafalgars or loses one.
- **No hierarchy in v2**: a flat nine-unit file replacing the three-unit one. Rejected: honest, but it leaves the unit card with nothing to show and the map had already ruled hierarchy in.
- **Roll-up**: a parent's position, state or strength computed from its children. Rejected by ADR-0003 before this decision was asked; a broken fleet's centroid is nowhere, and a strength weighted by ships would need the ship counts the schema refuses to carry.
- **The side as the root of the tree**, reopening the `sides` block ADR-0015 deferred here. Rejected: a side has no position, heading, state or strength, so it cannot be a unit, and making it one forces a snapshot for "British" into every phase. The `sides` block stays closed.
- **A `mixed` arm for a parent of foot and horse.** Rejected: a parent carries one arm like any unit, that of its main body, and no battle on this map authors a mixed parent. Cannae at wing level needs no parent at all.
- **A depth cap of two.** Rejected as a rule no battle exercises: `parent` is just an id, and the sixteen ceiling applies at every depth.
- **The battle file or the view choosing the level.** Rejected: ADR-0014 made a view a palette, pens and a glyph and nothing else, and a file-chosen default would be a second rule beside "open on the coarsest". The viewer chooses, as with the view, and for the same reason: a short fixed list of viewer preferences that must show its value unopened.
- **A checked consistency between levels** (a parent `intact` while a child is `engaged`, a parent at 0.6 whose children are all at 1). Rejected: each level is a self-contained picture authored from the sources, and a check would be a roll-up by another name.

## Consequences

- **Schema v2 adds an optional `parent` to the roster unit**, the id of another roster unit. A parent appears before its children in the roster, so depth falls out in one pass and roster order stays the label-priority and numeral order within a level. Every unit in a subtree carries the same `side`; the validator checks it. A child's `arm` need not match its parent's.
- **Schema v2 adds an optional top-level `levels`**, one display string per depth from the roots down, required when any unit has a parent and forbidden otherwise, its length equal to the tree's depth. Trafalgar's is `["Columns", "Squadrons"]`. The renderer never invents a level name.
- **ADR-0002 holds unchanged: every roster unit, parent or child, has exactly one snapshot in every phase.** A parent keeps its own position, heading, formation, state, strength and moves in phases where only its children are drawn. Absence is the error it is today, and units appearing mid-battle stays deferred.
- **A level is a depth.** Showing level n draws every unit at depth n and every shallower unit with no children, so a unit the author chose not to split stands for itself at every finer level and no ships vanish from the plate.
- **More than sixteen units drawn at one level is a validation error**, the label proof's break point made a rule; twelve is guidance in `docs/schema.md`. Counted per level, never per roster: Trafalgar at three columns and nine squadrons is twelve on the roster and never more than nine on the plate.
- **The Level chooser is a `<select>` beside the View chooser**, shown only when the battle has more than one level. Every visit opens on the coarsest level, the choice does not stick, there is no `?level=`, and a switch is instant and non-interrupting: clock and play state untouched, no crossfade. This follows the memory rule the View chooser settled and closes the question of whether a view picks the level.
- **The scrubber and the legend have no level of their own.** Phases are level-independent; the legend already varies only with what is drawn, so it gains numeral-key or arm rows for the units on the plate at this level and nothing more. The numeral stays the whole-roster index, stable across levels.
- **The unit card can read the tree**: a parent's card can list its children by name and a child's can name its parent. What the card shows is the card's decision.
- **Trafalgar is one file.** The v1 file grows in place to carry the squadron level in the same data issue that adds `arm`, `summary`, `dates` and `sort_date`; which eight or nine squadrons it authors is that issue's call.
