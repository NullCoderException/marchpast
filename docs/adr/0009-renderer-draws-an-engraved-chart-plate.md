# The renderer draws an engraved chart plate: ink ships on parchment with the state written beside them

The concept set the bar at "History Channel map with arrows" on a stylised parchment map, which left the actual visual language open. A throwaway static frame of Trafalgar at three units ([prototype/static-frame](https://github.com/NullCoderException/sandtable/blob/prototype/static-frame/prototypes/static-frame.html)) tried three structurally different treatments: an engraved 1805 **chart plate**, a dark **broadcast** map with solid blocks and a lower-third, and a strategy-game **replay** with badges and a timeline strip. We chose the chart plate's look and materials, and carried over from the broadcast variant the two things that made it more useful: a written state and strength beside every unit, and a full-width caption band that anchors each frame to the battle clock. The replay's timeline strip is player controls, not picture, and is covered by the player-controls decision.

## Considered options

- **Chart plate** (look chosen): parchment, ink, ships as ticks, smoke for engaged, serif plate typography. Reads as a document of the battle rather than a game of it, and sits naturally beside the public-domain 1805 and 1848 plans the sources draw on.
- **Broadcast** (two elements carried): high-contrast blocks and glows read from across the room but fight the parchment; the explicit state label and clock band were kept.
- **Replay** (rejected): badges, strength bars and the timeline strip pull toward the game the concept rules out; its wind feathers were kept for force.

## Consequences

- **A unit is drawn as ink ship-ticks.** `column` is ticks in line ahead along the heading; `line` is ticks abreast across the heading. These are the two **formation** labels in v1; a crescent is authored as `line` and is caption matter. The tick count is a renderer constant per glyph, not data: no schema field carries a ship count (ADR-0003, ADR-0010), so every unit at full strength shows the same number of ticks, and the shown ticks follow **strength** (a unit at 0.33 shows a third of its ticks). The static-frame prototype hard-coded real ship counts; the renderer cannot. Glyphs are styled labels, not geometry (ADR-0001): a glyph is never shorter than a readable minimum on screen, whatever the extent, because a true-scale twelve-ship column is about 2.4 nautical miles and vanishes at a battle-wide extent.
- **State is drawn and written.** Intact: clean ticks. Engaged: grey smoke puffs along the unit. Broken: gaps in the ticks and thinner smoke. Destroyed: a hollow outline with no ticks. Beside every unit the renderer writes its name and a second line with the state word and, when strength is below 1, the percentage. A sail count is caption matter, not label matter, because the schema does not carry one (ADR-0010). No glows, no clash marks.
- **Motion is three line styles.** The **track** (ADR-0004) is a fine dotted line ahead of the unit with an open head. An `intent` move is a dashed ink line with an open head. A `detachment` move is a solid line in the unit's side colour with a filled head; a head beyond the extent is clipped. During an approach the track and an intent arrow overlap by nature, so the three styles must stay distinguishable when drawn on top of each other.
- **The caption is a full-width band along the bottom of the picture,** ink-ruled, in the plate typeface: battle clock and date on the left, the phase label and caption to the right, and beneath the caption the short line of source labels for the phase's references (player-controls decision). The band is part of the picture so a screenshot stands alone; the scrubber's clock readout is a control and may show the same number.
- **Furniture** stays north-up. Top-left: a pen compass rose with the wind as a pen arrow blowing across it, feathered by **force** (ADR-0008: one feather for light through four for gale, none for calm), and the wind written as text beside it. Bottom-left: the scale bar in the battle's unit, and the **legend**, always shown in v0.1: each side's colour and name, the four state glyphs, and the three line styles. The map's credit line stays where the licensing decision put it.
- **Side colours are a fixed palette by side order,** red ink for the first side and blue for the second, further sides from a short ink list. No colour, glyph, or style field in any data file (ADR-0003, ADR-0005).
- **The plate typeface is IM Fell English,** bundled with the app under its SIL Open Font License and loaded before the first frame, because Canvas text falls back silently if the font is not ready and the renderer must work offline.
- **Extents should be landscape.** An authoring guideline, not a renderer rule: a square or portrait extent letterboxes to a strip on a wide window with the battle huddled in a corner. Trafalgar's extent runs Cadiz to Cape Trafalgar at roughly 0.56 degrees of latitude by 0.80 of longitude.
- **Rendering is checked by eye,** as the concept says: screenshots against the prototype, no pixel tests.
