# Land units draw as ranks of signs at a fixed plate size, and formation gains `mass`

*Amended 2026-09-07: a fourth arm, `aircraft` ([ADR-0024](0024-aircraft-are-an-arm-and-a-unit-may-be-absent-from-a-phase.md)), takes its sign in the same eight slots — decided on #140's canvas as an aeroplane in plan, a cross at the plate's pen, on the chart plate, the night plate and Atlas, and as a stroked arrowhead on the staff map (#139), whose 72 × 18, 18 × 72 and 36 × 36 frames keep this ADR's formations. Billow stays the engaged mark for every arm under the surface wind, ADR-0024 declined a fourth formation word, and ADR-0021 made `GLYPH_PX` anatomy. Everything here stands.*

ADR-0009 drew a unit as ink ship-ticks in one of two formations, `column` or `line`, at a length that was the greater of a readable floor and a nominal true length of 1.6 nautical miles. ADR-0015 gave every unit an **arm** and every view a **sign** per arm, and left to this decision the chart plate's infantry and cavalry signs, the formation words v2 adds, whether a land unit's engaged mark is the plate's smoke, and what a land glyph's length means. The Cannae research ([#41](https://github.com/NullCoderException/marchpast/issues/41)) authored every wing as `line` and put the Roman mass "several times deeper than its front", the crescent, the wedge and the hollow into captions, while naming Cannae as the battle where a formation changing shape is the story. The label prototype ([#39](https://github.com/NullCoderException/marchpast/issues/39)) found that the nominal length draws a unit wider than the whole battle line on a land extent.

We decided that a land unit is drawn the way a ship unit is: a fixed count of signs arranged by a formation word, at a length that is a **plate constant** and means nothing on the ground. Formation gains one word, **`mass`**, for a body in ranks. The chart plate's infantry sign is a short rank bar across the heading and its cavalry sign the same bar with one diagonal, echoing Atlas. Billow is the engaged mark for every arm. Heading stays the direction the front faces, so a unit retiring in good order keeps its heading and its track runs behind it.

## Considered options

- **Formation as a depth ratio** (`depth: 4`) rather than a word. Honest about the maniples, rejected: it is geometry in data, which ADR-0001 rules out, and no source states it as a number.
- **Adding `crescent`** alongside `mass`, a line whose ends trail back from the heading. Drawable from a centre and a heading, rejected: it reopens ADR-0009's call that Trafalgar's crescent is `line` plus caption, and the phase that matters at Cannae, the bow turning hollow, is made by the centre's position falling behind the two Libyan units, which three markers show without a word. Crescent, wedge and hollow stay caption matter.
- **A nominal true length per arm** (1.6 nmi for ships, a kilometre for foot, half that for horse) with the floor and a cap. Rejected: two more constants that are also guesses, for a length the glyph never honestly shows. The floor was already drawing Trafalgar: 1.6 nmi at its extent is about 45px on a 1120px canvas, below the 72px floor, and only just above it on a wide monitor. The nominal length bit only at Cannae, where it draws a 560px unit on a 6 km extent.
- **An authored `frontage` in metres.** ADR-0001 leaves the door open for a length in metres, rejected: it is geometry in data and no source gives a wing's frontage honestly.
- **A glyph length proportional to the plate**, the label prototype's "twelfth of the plate" plaster. Rejected: it makes a unit a different size on every window for no reason a viewer can see. The glyph is type-sized, like the label.
- **A `facing` field beside `heading`**, so heading could become the direction of travel. Rejected: redundant with the track (ADR-0004) and a second number every phase; the glossary already says heading is the front.
- **A separate land engaged mark** (crossed sabres, a hatched clash zone). Rejected: a second engaged vocabulary the legend would key twice. Cannae's dust is in Livy, and Billow under the Volturnus reads as dust blown into the Roman faces.
- **Cavalry as an open bar** against infantry's solid bar. Rejected: the plate already uses hollow for destroyed, so an open bar reads as state, not arm. Pictorial signs (a pike, a horse's head) are too pictorial for an engraved plate.
- **A different sign count per arm** (foot in a three-by-four block, horse in a single rank of six). Rejected: more constants, and the strength fractions stop lining up across arms.
- **A square Atlas block for `mass`.** Rejected: nothing about a square says deep rather than small; the two-rank footprint does.
- **Rejecting `ship` + `mass` in the validator.** Rejected: one rule now and a table to maintain once `artillery` or `air` arrive; ADR-0004 already keeps the validator to shape checks.
- **A legend row for `mass`.** Rejected: column and line have never had one; two ranks of four are as self-evident as eight abreast.

## Consequences

- **Formation is one of three words: `column`, `line`, `mass`.** `column` is signs in file along the heading, `line` abreast across it, `mass` in ranks, four across and two deep. The list is one flat vocabulary for every arm; the validator checks the word and never cross-checks it against the arm. Cannae's Roman infantry is `mass` from deployment on; its crossing in phase 1 is `column`; the crescent and the hollow are `line` plus caption, as at Trafalgar.
- **Eight signs per glyph for every arm, and strength drops signs.** ADR-0009's tick count and strength rule stand unchanged and now cover foot and horse. A `mass` at strength 0.5 is one rank of four, still a body: signs leave from the rear rank first.
- **The chart plate's land signs.** Infantry: a short solid rank bar across the heading, at the tick footprint. Cavalry: the same bar with one diagonal through it. The ship sign is the v1 chevron tick. The convention is the same one Atlas uses inside its block (ADR-0015), so a viewer who switches views carries the same reading. The Night plate uses the plate's glyph and gains nothing to draw.
- **A glyph is a plate constant.** Its length is one fixed on-screen value, the present 72px, on every screen and for every arm; the nominal-length constant is deleted. A unit never shows its ground frontage: the scale bar says the field is twenty kilometres, and the phone collapse (issue #58) shrinks furniture and labels, not the units. This supersedes ADR-0009's clause that a glyph has a nominal true length with a readable minimum; what stands is that glyphs are styled labels, never geometry.
- **Atlas draws `mass` as the two-rank footprint:** a block half the length of a line and twice its thickness, the filled fraction growing from the rear as it does now, the arm sign inside it unchanged. Both views agree on what a mass is: the same eight signs, or the same block, four across and two deep.
- **Billow is the engaged mark for every arm**, under the same wind rule (ADR-0014). On land it reads as dust; when a battle has no wind it falls back as the glyph already provides.
- **Heading is the front, and the track may run behind the unit.** The Spanish and Gallic centre retires south facing north; its heading stays 20 and its track points back through its own rear. Nothing in the schema changes, and the label pass must not assume a track runs ahead: the label prototype's collision model already treats the track as an obstacle wherever it lies.
- **The destroyed outline and the broken knock-out follow the formation.** A destroyed `mass` is the hollow outline of the two-rank footprint; a broken one has its signs thinned and knocked out of rank, as ticks are now.
- **The label's clearance follows the glyph.** ADR-0014's one number, how far the glyph reaches across its axis, is two ranks for a `mass`, so the label clears the rear rank as it clears a line's ticks.
- **The legend keys no formation.** Its rows stay sides, the four states, the three line styles and, from ADR-0015, arms when there are two or more.
- **Which Cannae phases are `mass`, and the sign's exact pixels, are the build's.** This decision fixes the words, the arrangement, the convention and the size rule; the renderer is checked by eye against the design canvas, as ADR-0009 says.
