# The surface derives its tones from the ink, the chooser keeps its popup, and the strip carries the way back

ADR-0023 derived every token the player's surface uses from the palette a view already has, and locked the derivation with a conformance test over `VIEWS`. It then handed two questions on against a drawing: whether the view chooser stays a native `<select>`, and whether the derivation is right for the staff map — naming a `surface` hand as the expected path if it is not, "rather than a failure". ADR-0028 has since made `/trafalgar/` a real page with its own card, which reopens a third question ADR-0023 closed by saying there was nothing to decide.

The staff map ([#139](https://github.com/NullCoderException/marchpast/issues/139)) is the fourth view and the first one the derivation was not written against. Drawn on the canvas for [#137](https://github.com/NullCoderException/marchpast/issues/137) — [The Player Surface](https://claude.ai/code/artifact/0817cc35-8ad9-404f-b5ad-590e8130913e), source `prototypes/player-surface/` on [prototype/player-surface](https://github.com/NullCoderException/marchpast/tree/prototype/player-surface/prototypes/player-surface) — it fails, twice, and the conformance test catches one of them.

**The surface takes its two toned values from the ink over the ground, the three choosers stay native `<select>`s with the caret drawn in the view's ink, and the strip's head carries `← All battles`.** Decided on [#137](https://github.com/NullCoderException/marchpast/issues/137).

## What the drawing measured

The pictures on the canvas are the real app captured at 1120 with the strip cropped off, and the strip drawn beneath them is `player.css` and `controls.ts` transcribed at the same scale — so a number on the canvas is a number the app produces. It reproduces the app's own strip to the pixel: 98px on Trafalgar at 1120, 54 on Cannae, 175 on a 390 phone against the app's 172.

Under ADR-0023's derivation, with its own arithmetic:

| | ink : ground | ink : fill | rule : ground | fill : ground |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Chart plate | 12.04 | 10.36 | 3.33 | 1.16 |
| Night plate | 12.28 | 10.01 | 4.71 | 1.23 |
| Atlas | 12.04 | 10.36 | 3.33 | 1.16 |
| Staff map | 9.07 | 8.61 | **2.91** | **1.05** |

The first failure is the one the test names: the rule is the ink at a flat 55%, and on the staff map that measures 2.91 against the ADR's own 3:1. It misses by **two hundredths of an alpha** — 0.57 gives 3.07.

The second failure is not tested at all. `--st-sunk` does three jobs — the hover fill, the scrubber's trough and the Details toggle's `.st-on` state — and the test asks only that text stay legible *on* it, never that it be distinguishable *from* the ground. On the staff map it sits 1.05 from the ground, which is no step: on the artboard the entire scrubber trough disappears.

Both have one cause. `--st-sunk` is `palette.land`, and `land` is a **picture** value — what ground is coloured on a map. The engraved plate happens to put its sea and its land 1.16 apart, so borrowing one for a control worked; a twentieth-century printed operations sheet colours its sea and its land one tone apart *on purpose*, and 1.05 is that decision showing up somewhere it was never meant to be read.

## Considered options

- **Leave ADR-0023 as written and change the staff map's palette instead.** Rejected. #139 chose `#ded7c2` and `#d9d2b8` against a drawing, as the two tones a printed sheet actually uses; moving them to make a control legible would let the strip dictate the picture, which is the wrong way round in a project where the picture is the product.
- **Relax the conformance test to 2.9:1.** Rejected on sight. A threshold moved to admit the first thing that failed it is not a threshold.
- **Cut a `surface` hand on `View`**, which ADR-0021 provides for and ADR-0023 named as the expected path. Genuinely arguable, and the drawing is the argument against it: the failure is not that the staff map wants control tones of its own, it is that the *derivation* reached for a picture value. A hand would put two more values on every future view to fix one view's arithmetic — the general table ADR-0021 rejected by name — and it would abandon ADR-0023's central claim, that a view supplies nothing new. **The slot stays uncut**, and this ADR is the drawing it was to be cut against.
- **Raise the rule's alpha for everyone**, to 0.62 or 0.65. Rejected: it thickens the rule on three views that already pass to fix a fourth, and the engraved strip's hairline is the plate's hairline.
- **A drawn chooser** — no `<select>` at all, a button over a listbox in the view's hand. It is the only candidate where the chooser belongs to the aesthetic, and the drawing gives it its best case: the engraved hand doubles the rule the way the plate's neat line is doubled, the staff hand squares the corners, sets the value in caps and files a solid caret. Rejected on what it throws away. It is a listbox per view — popup positioning, roving tabindex, type-ahead — it loses the phone's wheel picker, and it takes back the arrow keys [#130](https://github.com/NullCoderException/marchpast/issues/130) leaned on when it let `answersItself` stand aside for a focused `<select>`. It also puts a new hand on every future view before that view has a picture.
- **Labels beside each chooser.** Rejected. The strip's tail is three unlabelled dropdowns in a row and the level's words are the battle's own, so *Columns* and *Strikes* arrive with no word saying what they are the level of. A prefix inside each control — *Speed 1x*, *View: Chart plate*, *Level: Columns* — is the cheapest fix and it is still not cheap: the drawing measures it at **about 130px** across the three, in a strip that is already two rows at 1120. Deferred rather than refused; it costs a control's width somewhere else, and nothing has offered one.
- **A masthead or header on the player page**, carrying the brand and the battle's title. Rejected: it is a landing page's anatomy, the caption band already names the battle, and it spends the plate's height on something the picture says better.
- **#135's mark as the way home**, at 20px in the view's ink — a third of the link's width, and it reads as a home affordance the way a masthead does. Rejected for now on a question it opens and cannot close: #135 decided the mark is never in a side ink, and whether it may take a *view's* ink at all is undecided. A link that already exists beats a brand question that does not.
- **Making the Picker do it**, with an "All battles" entry at the head of its list. Rejected: the Picker's contract is that choosing an entry plays that battle, and one entry that leaves instead is a trapdoor in a list of battles.

## Consequences

- **`surfaceOf` derives `--st-sunk` and `--st-rule` from the ink over the ground.** The fill is the ink at **8%**; the rule is the ink at the **least alpha from 0.55 up that clears 3:1**, which is 0.55 for all three engraved views and 0.57 for the staff map. `--st-ink`, `--st-ground` and `--st-edge` are unchanged, and `palette.land` leaves the surface entirely. This **amends ADR-0023** in the one clause that named `land` and the flat 55%; everything else it decided stands.
- **This is not a repaint.** The plate's fill moves `#e3d3ac → #dfd4b8` and the night plate's `#2a3340 → #2c333c`: the same luminance step against the ground to two decimal places (1.16 and 1.23), a little greyer in hue, and no rule anywhere changes. The staff map's fill goes from a step of 1.05 to 1.14 and its rule from 2.91 to 3.07.
- **The conformance test gains a fourth row: the fill against the ground, at 1.1:1.** It is the test that would have caught this, and it is deliberately a *low* bar — a hover fill is a hint, not text — chosen because 1.05 fails it and 1.14 passes. It sits beside the three ADR-0023 already locks.
- **The three choosers stay native `<select>`s, with `appearance: none` and a caret drawn in the view's own ink.** The popup stays the operating system's, so the phone picker, the arrow keys, the screen-reader semantics and `color-scheme`'s fix for a dark view are all untouched; the one glyph the aesthetic could not reach changes hands. The engraved views draw a two-stroke chevron, the staff map a filed triangle — the same caret its `moves` hand already files.
- **`appearance: none` drops the native focus ring on some platforms**, so `player.css`'s existing `:focus-visible` rule — a 2px `--st-ink` outline at 2px offset — becomes load-bearing for the choosers rather than a supplement, and the padding grows to `padding-right: 28px` to make room for the caret. That is the whole cost.
- **The caret is drawn by the view's `type` hand**, not by a new module: #139 widened `type` to *a view's hand for setting a name against a thing*, and a caret set against a value is that. No slot is cut, and the module count stays at five.
- **The strip's head carries `← All battles`.** `libraryPage.ts` already builds it for the error path and ADR-0023 already took it off the library's own declaration so it inherits the strip's tokens; this promotes it to always-on. It costs about 90px and no new rule. **This supersedes ADR-0023's clause** that "there is no header and no back link on the player page to decide about", which was true when every battle was `/?battle=<name>` reached from the library and stopped being true when [ADR-0028](0028-a-battle-is-a-page-at-its-own-path-and-its-card-is-its-own-still.md) made a shared link the ordinary arrival: from `/trafalgar/` the Picker moves *sideways* to another battle and the library was unreachable.
- **The link is a link, not a button**, with a real `href` to `/`, so it is middle-clickable and its target is visible on hover — the one thing on the strip that leaves the page, and the only one that should behave like the web.
- **`forced-color-adjust: none` is placed on `.st-controls` and `.st-details`**, which is the code ADR-0029 assigned to this ticket. Drawn under emulated forced colours, the split ADR-0029 predicted is there and is worse than either extreme: the canvas keeps the view's parchment, because forced colours reach CSS-painted DOM and never a `<canvas>` or an `<img>`, while the strip beneath it flips to the system palette. That is not an emulation artefact; it is what the feature is defined to do.
- **The opt-out's *effect* is unconfirmed and is handed to [#141](https://github.com/NullCoderException/marchpast/issues/141).** Chrome's emulated forced-colors did not restore the author's ground even with `forced-color-adjust: none` set on the strip and all 31 of its descendants, so this drawing establishes the problem and not the fix. Someone must look at the built page in real Windows High Contrast before v0.3 ships. Recording that is the honest end of "nobody has yet viewed this page in that mode" — one person now has, in emulation, and emulation was not enough.
- **The strip wraps to two rows at 1120 on any battle with a level chooser and a Picker**, squeezing the scrubber to about 215px for Trafalgar's eight phases; Midway wants twenty-one ticks in the same room. Cannae, which has no level chooser, is one row, and the staff map is one row at 1120 because Archivo at width 75 is that much narrower than IM Fell English. **Nothing here fixes it** — the wrap is `player.css` working as designed, and the drawing records it so the next person to want a control's width knows what it comes out of.
- **On a phone the strip is 175 of 844 pixels, in four rows, and within about ten pixels of a fifth.** One more character in a level name, or one more view in the chooser, tips it. The canvas takes what the strip leaves (`canvas { flex: 1 }`), so a fifth row costs the picture, not the page.
- **The details panel is mostly below its own fold and that is accepted.** At `max-height: 40vh` in a 720-tall window the panel shows 288px, which at Trafalgar 13:30 is the heading and the notes and nothing else; the References and the whole Sources table are inside the panel's scroll. Both states are on the canvas. Raising the cap would eat the plate, which is the thing the panel is about.
- **Atlas's surface stays identical to the chart plate's**, under this derivation as under ADR-0023's, because Atlas spreads `CHART_PLATE.palette`. The canvas draws it anyway, which is what shows that the identity is a fact about the palette and not an oversight.
- **No motion is introduced.** ADR-0029 named #137 a live candidate to introduce *motion nobody pressed play for*; the caret is static, the link does not animate, and the reopening trigger stays unfired.
- **No schema change, no validator rule, no field, and nothing a battle file can say.** A view is still a palette, pens and five modules.
- **The glossary's Controls gains the way out**, and its Surface keeps every word ADR-0029 gave it: the drawing confirmed rather than moved it.
