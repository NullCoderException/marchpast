The still box
ADR-0025 fixed everything about the still but its height, and handed that here: “The plate box's height is #151's to set against its drawing, starting from 630.” The width is 1200 CSS px at DPR 0.5 and is not in question. Three heights, three extents — a landscape one, a portrait one and one that is nearly all sea.
Trafalgar · a landscape extent
the box at 630 · 288 × 151 · ADR-0025's starting number
the box at 760 · 288 × 182 · the drawing's choice
the box at 900 · 288 × 216
71.7 by 62.3 km, an aspect of 1.15 — squarer than the box at any of the three heights, so the plate is height-fitted and the letterbox falls on the flanks. Raising the box raises the map with it: at 900 the coast, the two columns and Cadiz are all half again the size they are at 630, in the same 288px of card width.
Alesia · a portrait extent, 8.9 by 12.0 km
the box at 630 · 288 × 151 · ADR-0025's starting number
the box at 760 · 288 × 182 · the drawing's choice
the box at 900 · 288 × 216
The extreme case at 0.74 — taller than it is wide. At 630 the whole siege is a strip down the middle of the card with two thirds of the picture empty; at 900 the ground reaches the frame. The real Alesia file will have this shape, because 8.9 by 12.0 km is what the two lines enclose.
Midway · four hundred miles of open sea
the box at 630 · 288 × 151 · ADR-0025's starting number
the box at 760 · 288 × 182 · the drawing's choice
the box at 900 · 288 × 216
0.97, and a picture that is mostly empty ocean whatever the box does. Midway is the case that says the box height is not the only thing at stake: a still can be sparse and still be the right picture, and the card must read when the still gives it almost nothing.
The seven extents, and why 630 is the wrong shape
A 1200 × 630 box is 1.90 : 1. Not one of the seven extents is that wide:
Alesia8.9 × 12.0 km0.74
the Little Bighorn10.5 × 13.4 km0.79
Midway430 × 445 km0.97
Trafalgar71.7 × 62.3 km1.15
Copenhagen31.4 × 24.5 km1.28
Cannae20.9 × 14.5 km1.44
the Nile52.3 × 32.3 km1.62
So every battle in the library is height-fitted in a 630 box, and the width the box does not use is letterbox on the flanks. Raising the box raises the drawn map one for one — the glyph stays the plate constant it is on every screen (ADR-0016), but the ground under it grows — right up to the point where the box gets wider than the extent, after which the extra height is genuinely wasted.
What the drawing takes
760, up from ADR-0025's 630. Some flank letterbox is unavoidable — no box holds 0.74 and 1.62 at once — so the number to find is the one where every battle is still gaining. Take the plate rect inside the 20px margin: at 760 it is 1160 × 720, an aspect of 1.61, and the widest extent the library holds is the Nile's 1.62. So 760 is the last height at which every one of the seven is height-fitted and every extra pixel of box goes straight into the drawn map, one for one. Below it, all seven are losing picture to nothing. Above it the Nile has reached its full width and starts paying letterbox at the top and foot instead, Cannae follows it at about 830, and by 900 the still (216px) plainly governs a card whose text is about 150.

It costs 31px per card — 288 × 182 rather than 288 × 151 — which is 217px down a page of seven. The still is then a little taller than the text beside it rather than a little shorter, which is the one thing to look at on the desktop artboard before this is settled.

The number is not "the widest extent, forever": it is where the widest extent we have sits, and a future battle wider than 1.58 would be letterboxed on its flanks like any plate in the player. That is the ordinary condition of a fixed frame, not a reason to keep re-cutting the box.
