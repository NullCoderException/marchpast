The rail, drawn out
The gutter at twice size. Nothing here is new material: the rail is the mark's own rule stood upright, and the node is the mark's tick — thin and pale against thick and dark, which is the contrast #135 found the mark only reads at.
What it is made of
The rail. One line, 1px, ink at 30%. Single, never doubled: the page has one doubled rule and it is the masthead's horizon. The rail hangs from that horizon and stops dead at the last node — it does not run on past Midway, because the library is not claiming there is more below.
The node. A bar 13 × 3 in full ink, crossing the rail: short and thick against thin and pale, the mark's own proportion. Not a dot — a dot on a line is a bullet, and this is a tick on a stand. It sits level with the top edge of the still, which is the one line that is in the same place on the desktop and on a phone.
The interval. 13px italic at 72% ink, starting on the rail and reading right, in the run between two cards. The rail breaks for it. Same size as the date, one step quieter, because it is a fact about the shelf rather than about a battle.
The measure
The library's 46rem holds text alone. Rail, still and text want 52rem — 832px: 24 padding, a 48 gutter, a 288 still, a 24 gap and 424 of text, which is 58 characters of the summary at 15px and inside the measure the old 46rem gave it (about 60). Widening past 56rem gives the summary a line too long to track back; narrowing to 48rem takes the still under 240 and the text under 55 characters at once.

What the rail replaces
The 1px hairline between entries goes. It ran the full measure, so it would cross the rail; and with an interval line and a still edge already separating two entries, a third separator is one too many. The hover ground (--st-sunk) still gives a card its extent, and it is what the reader is actually choosing.
